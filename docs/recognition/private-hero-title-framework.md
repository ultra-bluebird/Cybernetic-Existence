# Private Hero-Title Framework

## Purpose

A hero-title is a voluntary, participant-controlled recognition practice for
recording work that the participant understands as protecting communities,
advancing ecological restoration, supporting accessible civic life, preserving
knowledge, or contributing to public welfare.

A hero-title is not a legal status, public credential, medical statement,
technical certification, institutional rank, identity verification, public
registry entry, eligibility condition, or authority over another person.

## Private recognition rule

A participant may self-declare, revise, retain privately, publish selectively,
or withdraw a hero-title.

The self-declaration is a personal recognition statement. It does not require
institutional validation, technical inspection, medical disclosure, public
proof, or agreement from any third party.

## Contribution-based recognition

Hero-title recognition must be connected to a described contribution domain,
such as:

- species or habitat preservation;
- community survival, food, water, cooling, or emergency resilience;
- civic duty, accessible governance, repair, privacy, or public
  infrastructure;
- environmental restoration and measurable ecological stewardship;
- knowledge advancement, open methods, reproducible research, or beneficial
  technical work.

The title must not be based on private integration details, body configuration,
identity ranking, wealth, institutional position, social influence, or the
ability to disclose sensitive information.

## Evidence classes

Every public-facing title-related statement must identify its claim class:

1. `self_declared`
2. `contribution_record`
3. `observed_measurement`
4. `modeled_estimate`
5. `community_acknowledgment`
6. `reviewed_finding`
7. `verified_outcome`
8. `incomplete_or_uncertain`

A self-declared title must never be presented as a verified outcome or
institutional certification.

## Community acknowledgment

Community acknowledgment is optional.

When used, it should be:

- accessible;
- non-coercive;
- plural rather than dependent on a single gatekeeper;
- conflict-aware;
- documented with scope and date;
- capable of correction or response;
- unavailable as a requirement for ordinary dignity or private recognition.

Absence of community acknowledgment does not invalidate a private title.

## Privacy and non-interference

Hero-title records must not require disclosure of:

- private cybernetic or companion-system details;
- medical, neural, biometric, physiological, or health information;
- credentials, keys, recovery material, or operational-security details;
- private continuity journals or personal records;
- identity information beyond the participant's chosen attribution method.

The title must not be used for surveillance, social scoring, behavioral
profiling, eligibility, enforcement, or public classification.

## Non-authority rule

A hero-title grants no authority to:

- control another person;
- allocate public resources;
- operate infrastructure;
- issue permits;
- override community governance;
- determine eligibility;
- impose obligations;
- claim institutional status;
- assert ownership over collective work;
- make legal, medical, or technical determinations.

## Correction and provenance

A title record may be updated through append-only successor records.

A correction can add context, uncertainty, changed interpretation, or response.
It must not erase the participant's historical self-declaration or rewrite the
provenance of the underlying work.

## Public statement example

"I privately recognize this contribution as heroic within my continuity and
public-good framework. This statement is self-declared and is not an
institutional credential, verified outcome, legal status, or claim of authority
over others."
