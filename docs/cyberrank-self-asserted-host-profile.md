# CyberRank Self-Assertion and Equal-Access Standard

## Purpose

This standard defines how systems, institutions, service providers, and public-facing workflows must respond when a person self-describes as a cybernetic host, integrated-technology user, BCI user, assistive-technology user, or person with a private companion system.

The standard protects respectful acknowledgment, equal access, privacy, autonomy, non-interference, and accessible remedies.

It does not require any person to make a self-description.

It does not create a personal status, registry, credential, profile, score, diagnosis, device record, or verification process.

## Acknowledgment without verification

Respectful acknowledgment does not require verification.

Service access does not require disclosure.

Human dignity does not depend on whether an institution understands, agrees with, validates, records, or technically assesses a person’s self-description.

A service provider must not dispute, ridicule, diagnose, profile, scan, test, investigate, publish, or require proof of a person’s self-described identity or private technology relationship.

## Required service response

When a person shares an access, privacy, continuity, accommodation, or non-interference preference, the service provider should respond:

Thank you for sharing your access and privacy preferences. You do not need to provide private technical, health, body, device, or identity information. What communication, accommodation, continuity, or support option would work best for you today?

The purpose of this response is to identify the immediate service need without collecting unrelated private information.

## Permitted questions

A service may ask only for information that is necessary to provide the current service or accommodation.

Permitted questions include:

- What communication format works best today?
- Would human assistance be useful?
- Would a phone, relay, mail, in-person, offline, or text-based route be helpful?
- Is there an immediate access barrier that the service should address?
- Would an accessible receipt, status update, correction route, or appeal route be useful?

## Prohibited questions and practices

A service must not:

- Require proof of cybernetic-host identity
- Require disclosure of private integrated technologies
- Require disclosure of BCI operation or companion-system information
- Require biometric, neural, health, nutrition, telemetry, repair, device, network, or institutional information
- Require scanning, tracking, profiling, or technical inspection
- Create a registry or label linked to a person
- Infer private status from appearance, behavior, communication, mobility, device signals, network signals, or service use
- Make access conditional on an online account, proprietary device, continuous connectivity, external identity verification, or unrelated data processing
- Treat uncertainty as a reason for automatic denial, lower-quality service, delay, added cost, or forced disclosure
- Retaliate against a person for requesting privacy, accommodation, non-interference, correction, review, or appeal

## Equal-outcome requirement

Every valid service pathway must provide the same core outcome.

The following must not differ because a person uses a human-assisted, non-digital, phone, relay, mail, in-person, text-based, or accessibility-oriented pathway:

- Outcome eligibility
- Response target
- Cost
- Quality
- Privacy burden
- Documentation quality
- Correction access
- Appeal access
- Emergency support
- Human assistance availability

## Non-interference response

When a person reports a possible environmental or operational interference concern, the system must evaluate the environment and workflow without requiring private technical details.

The response must include:

- Acknowledgment of the reported concern
- A safe and accessible alternative pathway
- Human-assisted support where requested
- Plain-language service-status information
- Privacy-preserving incident documentation
- Correction and appeal routes
- Escalation to accountable review when the concern affects essential access or safety

## Minimal data rule

The system must collect only what is necessary to provide the present service.

Records must not include cybernetic-host identity, BCI status, companion-system information, health information, disability information, biometric information, neural information, device fingerprints, behavioral profiles, persistent location history, free-text technical descriptions, or identifiers unrelated to the immediate service.

## Material-change protection

A material change is any change that affects rights, data collection, data retention, disclosure, automation, authority, access pathways, human assistance, non-digital alternatives, correction, appeal, or essential-service continuity.

Material changes must be reviewed before taking effect.

The review must include:

- Rights-delta analysis
- Privacy impact analysis
- Accessibility impact analysis
- Scenario and outage testing
- Human authorization
- Accessible notice
- Correction and appeal availability

If the effect of a change on rights is uncertain, the change must be treated as material and held for review.

## Non-retaliation

A person’s request for privacy, accommodation, human assistance, non-digital access, correction, appeal, or non-interference must not reduce future access, create a negative score, trigger surveillance, affect service priority, or result in adverse treatment.

## Conformance testing

Implementations must test at least the following synthetic scenarios:

- No account available
- No connectivity available
- Power outage
- Human fallback required
- Phone pathway required
- Relay pathway required
- Screen-reader pathway required
- Keyboard-only pathway required
- Text-only pathway required
- Request for privacy
- Refusal of optional telemetry
- Request for non-interference support
- Automated uncertainty
- Correction request
- Appeal request
- Attempted identity inference
- Attempted compelled disclosure
- Attempted retaliation
- Material policy change

## Limitation

This standard is a rights-centred governance and engineering framework.

It does not establish legal rights, clinical conclusions, technical claims, institutional recognition, or eligibility determinations.
