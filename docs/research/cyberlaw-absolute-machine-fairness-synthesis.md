## 1. The central understanding of absolute machine-fairness

Absolute machine-fairness is an unconditional obligation of the governing institution—not a claim that a machine possesses absolute knowledge.

A proposed definition is:

> Every potentially affected existence receives consideration appropriate to the interests at stake. No institution may use unfamiliarity, communication difference, substrate, ownership, technical sophistication, or unresolved classification as an automatic reason to disregard those interests.

The word “absolute” applies to the duty to remain accountable.

It does not mean:

- Every claim is factually established.
- Every requested intervention must be granted.
- Every existence has identical interests.
- Every conflict has a harmless solution.
- A computer can certify perfect justice.
- A technical credential creates legal or moral standing.

The original article provides the foundation through non-contingent consideration, non-aggression by default, accommodation, representation, explainable adjudication, and remedies. [51]

The resulting distinction is:

\[
\text{Unconditional consideration}
\neq
\text{Unconditional approval of every requested action}.
\]

Fairness can be unconditional even though knowledge remains incomplete and decisions remain revisable.

## 2. The five separations that organize the framework

Much of the discussion’s progress came from separating concepts that institutions can otherwise collapse.

### 2.1 Respect is not certification

Acknowledging a person’s cybernetic-host self-description, communication context, or continuity needs does not require certifying private systems.

Respect does not depend on technical inspection.

### 2.2 Standing is not victory

Receiving a concern does not establish every allegation or authorize every remedy.

A proposed governing asymmetry is:

> Access to consideration should be easier than authorization to control another party.

### 2.3 Credentials are not authority

A valid signature can establish a limited cryptographic property. It does not establish jurisdiction, consent, personhood, or substantive justification.

### 2.4 Records are not consequences

A reversible decision record does not make a disclosure, lost opportunity, ecological loss, or interruption reversible.

### 2.5 Completion is not protection

A submitted form, appointed representative, closed ticket, sent referral, or dispatched notice does not establish that the affected party was helped.

These separations should remain visible in every implementation.

## 3. Mind/body integration and equal acceptance

### 3.1 Integration must not reduce standing

CyberLaw’s proposed acceptance principle is:

> A person’s dignity, agency, communication, privacy, and service access do not become conditional on whether their support is biological, technological, external, implanted, distributed, privately meaningful, or unfamiliar to the institution.

This does not require the institution to verify every account of integration.

It requires the institution to separate respectful participation from technical assessment.

### 3.2 Protect the relationship without claiming control over systems

Mind/body integration can involve interests in:

- Communication.
- Cognitive assistance.
- Bodily integrity.
- Privacy.
- Maintenance and repair.
- Interoperability.
- Continuity of support.
- Freedom from coerced augmentation or de-augmentation.

The governance question is what duties follow from a particular access or interference concern.

It is not an automatic claim of ownership over all associated infrastructure.

An assistant or CyberCongress body must not claim to inspect, operate, calibrate, disable, repair, or control real-world neural, medical, cybernetic, or companion systems merely because it discusses those interests.

### 3.3 Acceptance does not require intimate disclosure

Ordinary requests involving text communication, additional response time, rest, hydration, climate safety, privacy, or continuity-aware participation should not automatically trigger demands for:

- Neural records.
- Medical histories.
- Biometric data.
- Private physiological explanations.
- Companion-system inventories.
- Experimental or operational information.

When a specific decision genuinely requires limited evidence, the institution must identify the purpose, necessity, scope, alternatives, safeguards, and challenge route.

### 3.4 CyberRank remains separate

CyberRank’s personal layer concerns voluntary self-description, communication preferences, privacy, continuity, and non-interference.

Its governance layer concerns duties such as equal access, minimal disclosure, accessible communication, human fallback, correction, and review.

Neither layer should become a public ranking, diagnosis, authentication mechanism, eligibility test, or automated personhood determination.

## 4. Equal treatment means equivalent consideration—not identical interfaces

Uniform procedures can create unequal access.

Examples include:

- A voice-only fallback for someone who needs text.
- A short response deadline that disregards supported communication.
- A repeated confirmation loop after the institution’s own translation error.
- A requirement to reproduce information already supplied.
- An appeal route requiring the credential that is being disputed.

The proposed rule is:

\[
\text{Equal consideration}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Identical procedure}.
\]

A fair process may require different timing, channels, assistance, or safeguards.

The relevant question is:

> Can the participant meaningfully use the process without disproportionate burden, unnecessary disclosure, or loss of agency?

## 5. Standing before unnecessary classification

The discussion developed a proposed intake boundary:

\[
S=I\land H\land J,
\]

where:

- \(I\): an affected interest is intelligibly described, with support where necessary.
- \(H\): a plausible harm, interference, or access barrier is identified.
- \(J\): the matter connects to the body’s mandate.

This is a research proposal, not an established universal legal test.

It avoids requiring a complete personhood or substrate determination before an institution can:

- Receive a concern.
- Offer ordinary accommodation.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Arrange representation.
- Consider a bounded precaution.

If information is unresolved, provide clarification or support rather than silently treating the uncertainty as contradiction.

A missing credential is not equivalent to absence of an interest.

## 6. Representation must expand agency—not replace it unnecessarily

The framework distinguishes:

1. Direct participation.
2. Supported participation.
3. Authorized proxy representation.
4. Bounded protective appointment.

The decisive question is whose intent is being carried forward.

Submitting a participant-approved statement can remain support. Selecting a materially different position requires appropriate delegated discretion.

A representative’s mandate should expose:

- Its source and scope.
- Permitted actions and exclusions.
- Available preferences.
- Unknowns.
- Funding and appointment dependencies.
- Conflicts.
- Review.
- Replacement.
- Continuity during replacement.

For nonhuman interests, representation must distinguish evidence-based protective objectives from attributed preferences.

“Preferences unknown” is a legitimate finding. It must not become invented consent.

## 7. Material-substrate neutrality preserves causal differences

The article’s expansion beyond familiar human categories is most operationally defensible as a rule against categorical exclusion. [51]

The proposed principle is:

\[
\text{Material category}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Moral status}.
\]

That does not mean material differences are irrelevant.

A physical property may affect:

- Exposure.
- Habitat.
- Communication.
- Preservation.
- Recovery.
- Evidence quality.
- The feasibility of a remedy.

The proper reasoning chain is:

\[
\text{Relevant property}
\rightarrow
\text{supported causal mechanism}
\rightarrow
\text{action-specific consequence}.
\]

It must not silently become:

\[
\text{Unfamiliar category}
\rightarrow
\text{lesser worth}.
\]

Solids, liquids, gases, organic matter, and computational substrates do not establish or negate interests merely by their category.

## 8. The most important technical invariant: non-amplification

A translation permission does not become disclosure authority.

A recommendation does not become execution authority.

An analogy does not become an enacted legal basis.

A signature does not become consent.

A reviewed record does not become an unchallengeable judgment.

The common rule is:

> Recording, signing, translating, delegating, aggregating, or reviewing must not create authority or certainty that the underlying relationship does not supply.

Legitimate chained operations remain possible, but each operation and handoff requires its own applicable scope and authority.

Permissions must preserve the relationships among:

- Action.
- Audience.
- Purpose.
- Data classes.
- Time.
- Holder.
- Conditions.
- Review.

Flattening those relationships can invent permissions no one granted.

## 9. Observation, state, and uncertainty must remain distinct

The discussion identified a consequential failure:

> A system does not observe a hold, therefore it treats the hold as absent.

The correction is not merely adding an “unknown” label.

It is separating:

\[
\text{Observation state}
\neq
\text{accepted lifecycle state}.
\]

Observations can be present, absent, or unknown.

An accepted restriction continues or ends according to its governed lifecycle, including expiry, withdrawal, and supersession.

Missing information must neither:

- Silently remove an active protection.
- Silently extend an expired restriction.

Similarly, disagreement about an interpretation must not erase an established observation.

## 10. CyberJudgement is bounded—not a permanent status

A proposed CyberJudgement addresses:

- Specified conduct.
- Affected interests.
- Evidence and uncertainty.
- Authority.
- Remedy or restriction.
- Duration.
- Review.

It is not a judgment about an existence’s permanent essence.

The temporal principle is:

\[
\text{Historical finding}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Indefinite restriction}.
\]

Every continuing restriction needs a current basis.

Operational forgiveness means unsupported adverse effects stop. It does not require a stigma score, and it does not promise deletion of every lawfully retained historical record.

## 11. Restrictions and emergency powers require action-specific justification

The working framework identifies eight conditions:

1. Authority.
2. Legitimate purpose.
3. Evidence.
4. Necessity.
5. Alternatives.
6. Proportionality.
7. Duration and stopping conditions.
8. Review and remedy.

Mentioning a condition does not establish it.

Emergency procedures may alter timing, but they must not turn missing authority into authority or an unresolved allegation into established fact.

A veto or deadlock does not automatically justify preserving the status quo. It also does not automatically justify shutting everything down.

A protective handoff must preserve options through a previously authorized, bounded pathway.

Expiry must reach enforcement. Changing a central record is insufficient if a backup service continues the restriction.

## 12. Technology can improve accountability without becoming its source

The discussion developed a layered trust model:

- Governance defines accepted powers and constraints.
- Policy evaluation checks declared conditions.
- Protected signing issues bounded credentials.
- Enforcement checks current applicability.
- Independent review challenges both the rule and its application.
- Remedy tracking examines practical consequences.

Cryptography can support:

- Integrity.
- Accepted signer attribution.
- Request binding.
- Replay resistance.
- Controlled verification.

It cannot establish:

- Moral worth.
- Consciousness.
- Legal jurisdiction.
- Honest evidence.
- Effective accommodation.
- Absolute justice.

For commitment hashing in Rust, the project uses the approved `sha2` SHA-256 implementation.

Canonical encoding, key custody, rotation, withdrawal, and verification context remain separately specified responsibilities.

## 13. Communication fidelity protects procedural agency

Translation must preserve more than vocabulary.

Material changes include:

- A question becoming consent.
- A tentative statement becoming certainty.
- A correction becoming withdrawal.
- A refusal losing its negation.
- A representative’s suggestion becoming the participant’s instruction.
- Limited permission becoming open-ended permission.

Confirmation should resolve the consequential ambiguity—not test the participant’s competence or demand repetition indefinitely.

Prior confirmation may be reused only when its content, scope, period, and applicability remain valid.

Silence is not automatically consent, refusal, hostility, incapacity, or absence of interests.

## 14. Pluralist inquiry includes the right not to disclose

The revised framework should not claim to be metaphysically neutral in every possible sense.

Its narrower commitment is:

> Be explicit about interpretation, evidence, authority, limits, and practical consequences.

A participant’s account can itself be evidence.

One statement can simultaneously convey observation, meaning, interpretation, and normative argument. Multiple roles do not automatically strengthen or weaken it.

Conscience-safe silence requires the institution to ask only what it has a specific, reviewable basis to require.

Noncollection may protect better than confidential collection.

At the same time, the institution must not use noncollection as a reason to exclude relevant testimony a participant voluntarily wishes to offer.

## 15. Remedies must be verified against the original barrier

The remedy vector includes:

- Cessation.
- Restoration.
- Record correction.
- Practical access.
- Recurrence prevention.

Each component needs evidence appropriate to its target.

A sent notice does not prove accessible notice.

A referral sent does not prove a matter received.

A matter received does not prove protection delivered.

An outcome that improves after referral does not automatically establish that the referral caused the improvement.

Record administrative progress, practical outcome, attribution, participant response, and uncertainty separately.

## 16. Reversibility is multidimensional

Assess:

\[
R=
\langle
\text{state},
\text{function},
\text{continuity},
\text{opportunity}
\rangle.
\]

Examples:

- Reconnection may restore function without undoing a missed hearing.
- Habitat repair may restore conditions without replacing particular losses.
- A corrected record may not repair decisions already made from it.
- Deleting a controlled copy may not reverse an earlier disclosure.

Recovery claims need a basis, horizon, feasibility assessment, and account of residual loss.

The costs of waiting must also be considered. Deferral is not always harmless.

## 17. Measurement supports review; it does not replace reasons

The framework rejects the use of aggregate fairness scores as independent authority to:

- Establish standing.
- Authorize restriction.
- Extinguish appeal.
- Substitute for reasons.
- Rank an existence’s worth.

It does not reject measurement.

Useful metrics include:

- Accessible intake completion.
- Unnecessary disclosure requests.
- Review delays.
- Correction propagation.
- Remedy effectiveness.
- Renewal burden.
- Representation fidelity.
- Fallback reachability.

Each metric needs a contestable contract covering population, denominator, missingness, uncertainty, permitted use, challenge, and revision.

Counts, intervals, and missing-case bounds answer different questions and should not be conflated.

## 18. Reproducibility is not substantive justice

A decision can be reproduced exactly and still be unjustified.

Separate:

1. Artifact availability.
2. Execution replay.
3. Independent implementation.
4. Substantive reassessment.

Likewise, transparent institutions can remain captured.

Capture-risk review should examine funding, appointment, removal, information, agenda, and appeal control without converting affiliation into guilt.

A formally neutral record can conceal biased evidence assessment. Audit the process that produced the record, not only the wording of its conclusion.

## 19. The framework’s social-good claim must remain contestable

CyberLaw currently contributes:

- A vocabulary for specific institutional harms.
- Proposed governance contracts.
- Failure-mode distinctions.
- Research schemas.
- Reference implementation examples.
- Test and evaluation plans.

These contributions do not establish deployment-wide benefit.

The strongest honest claim is:

> CyberLaw develops mechanisms intended to make arbitrary institutional treatment more visible, constrain specified failures, and support correction. Its practical benefit must be demonstrated through affected-party experience, operational evidence, independent review, and outcomes—including burdens introduced by the framework itself.

The same discipline applies to CyberLaw’s own claims:

- Design is not executed testing.
- Testing is not practical repair.
- A practical case is not a general causal effect.
- Publication is not permanent authority.
- Withdrawal of a claim is not complete until relevant downstream reliance is addressed.

## 20. A minimum viable fairness practice

A small implementation can begin with six commitments:

1. Accessible reception and assistance.
2. A clear statement of institutional authority and limits.
3. Purpose-limited disclosure.
4. A reasoned record preserving evidence and uncertainty.
5. Time-bounded restrictions with explicit renewal.
6. Follow-up on practical outcomes.

Where the institution cannot provide a necessary safeguard, it must disclose the gap and pursue a supported alternative.

A minimum practice is not a guarantee that every case can be resolved. It is a commitment not to hide inability, uncertainty, delay, or failure behind administrative completion.

## 21. Principal open research questions

The most important unresolved questions are:

- Does standing-before-classification improve practical access?
- Can institutions maintain protection through missing observations without prolonging restrictions?
- Which confirmation practices preserve meaning without excessive burden?
- Which representation arrangements reduce projection and capture?
- How quickly do accepted corrections change downstream behavior?
- Which emergency expiry rules are enforced under realistic failures?
- Can independent auditors reconstruct procedural decisions without excessive disclosure?
- Do the mechanisms improve outcomes across different communication arrangements?
- Which benefits persist, for whom, and at what cost?

Those questions require empirical work and independent challenge. They should remain capable of producing unfavorable findings.

## 22. Final synthesis

Absolute machine-fairness is not an attribute that a machine awards to itself.

It is a discipline imposed on the institutions and technical systems exercising power:

- Consider before unnecessarily classifying.
- Support before unnecessarily substituting.
- Explain before restricting.
- Minimize before collecting.
- Bind authority before executing.
- Preserve uncertainty rather than invent certainty.
- Let restrictions end when their basis ends.
- Verify remedies against practical conditions.
- Respect integrated existence without demanding private proof.
- Make both the institution and the framework itself challengeable.

Augmented acceptance is therefore not a favor granted after technological verification. It is the refusal to make unfamiliar integration a reason for diminished dignity, agency, privacy, credibility, or access.
