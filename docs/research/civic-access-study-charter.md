# Civic Access and Participatory Research Charter

## Status

Proposed research protocol for local review.
No participant data have been collected.
No effectiveness, legal-compliance, or deployment claim is made.

## Purpose

Study whether an accessible, plain-language civic workflow reduces
avoidable procedural work while preserving understanding, privacy,
correction, and access to human assistance.

## Research question

Compared with the existing workflow, does the proposed workflow improve
correct task completion and reduce repeated information requests?

## Scope

Use public procedural materials and fictional civic scenarios.

Exclude:
- live legal cases and individualized legal advice;
- medical, neural, biometric, or companion-system information;
- authentication credentials and private institutional records;
- person ranking, eligibility decisions, and identity inference;
- hardware operation and external submissions.

## Community authority

Before recruitment, community reviewers approve:
- the tasks and intended benefits;
- accessible participation methods;
- compensation terms;
- outcome definitions;
- reporting and deletion rules.

Disagreement and dissent remain visible in the study report.

## Participation

Participation is voluntary and separate from access to services.

Participants may pause, skip a question, or stop without penalty.
Compensation must not depend on successful task completion.

A person may choose their communication method and request assistance.
No diagnosis, augmentation inventory, or proof of identity category is
required for participation or communication support.

## Study design

Use equivalent fictional tasks in existing and proposed workflows.

If both workflows are accessible for a participant, counterbalance task
order where feasible.

Never withhold a necessary accommodation.
If a comparison is not accessible, stop that comparison, offer an
accessible alternative, and report the resulting comparability limit.

Distinguish workflow defects from task misunderstanding and study-design
defects. Preserve participant corrections.

## Measures

Primary:
- correct next-step completion count and attempt count;
- repeated-information-request count and total request count.

Secondary:
- active task time in minutes;
- correction attempts;
- calendar waiting time, if applicable;
- optional participant-rated effort;
- unwanted disclosure requests;
- assistance availability;
- unresolved workflow failures.

Keep measures separate rather than constructing a person score.

## Analysis

Define success criteria before examining outcomes.

Report denominators, missing observations, withdrawals, protocol changes,
and accessibility-related exclusions from comparisons.

Report paired differences only for genuinely comparable paired tasks.
Do not count multiple tasks from one person as independent participants.

Do not infer causation from an uncontrolled before-and-after comparison.
Do not infer demographic equity from communication-method comparisons.

## Data minimization

Use blank forms, public instructions, and fictional scenario content.

Do not record names, addresses, case details, device identifiers,
diagnoses, exact location histories, or free-text personal narratives.

Do not make audio or video recordings.

Keep any operational recruitment or compensation records outside the
analysis dataset, under a separately approved administrative process.

## Consent and deletion

Provide accessible, purpose-specific consent information.

Participants may decline optional questions without losing access to the
study or its compensation.

Delete a participant's identifiable or linkable study material on request
while it remains retrievable.

Explain before consent that a contribution cannot necessarily be
separated from an already published, non-linkable aggregate.

Default retention for temporary task sheets and local analysis records:
30 calendar days after the community-approved report is finalized.

Do not publish raw participant-level records.

## Reporting

Publish:
- research question and protocol version;
- workflow versions and source review dates;
- methods and deviations;
- aggregate results and uncertainty;
- unresolved barriers and dissent;
- limitations and remediation recommendations.

Withhold breakdowns that could expose a participant.
If safe aggregation is not possible, report a design finding without
publishing the underlying participant breakdown.

## External action boundary

This charter authorizes no external action.

Contacting an institution, submitting a report, publishing findings,
changing a live service, or transferring data requires separate approval
for the exact target and content.

## CyberRank and personal self-description

Respect voluntary self-description and communication preferences.

CyberRank is private project governance vocabulary, not a public status,
verification system, diagnosis, ranking, or eligibility criterion.

Acknowledgment does not imply technical assessment or certification.
No private system access or monitoring is performed.

## Stop and review conditions

Pause the affected activity if:
- participation becomes coercive;
- private information is requested outside the approved scope;
- an essential communication method fails;
- the task could affect a real entitlement or legal deadline;
- a material protocol change lacks community review.

Offer accessible correction and a human review path.

## Acceptance criteria

The study is ready to begin only when:
- community reviewers approve the protocol;
- tasks use verified public materials and fictional cases;
- accessibility testing is complete for offered methods;
- compensation and assistance are available;
- retention and deletion responsibilities are assigned;
- publication and external-action gates are understood;
- all measures have explicit definitions and units.

A favorable result supports a bounded recommendation for further study,
not an automatic production deployment.
