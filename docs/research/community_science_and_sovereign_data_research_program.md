# Community Science and Sovereign Data Research Program

## Purpose

This program supports community-led environmental, accessibility, infrastructure, habitat, and civic research while preserving privacy, consent, self-asserted identity, accessibility, local-first operation, and augmented and cybernetic rights.

Community observations are valid research evidence. They remain distinct from calibrated sensor records, inspection records, laboratory results, public datasets, and model outputs.

Body-linked data, including neural, biometric, health, assistive-technology, augmentation, and cybernetic data, are sensitive by default. They are not ordinary citizen-science data and must remain local-only or never-release unless a separate, explicit, purpose-specific, revocable consent process permits a narrowly defined use.

## Research principles

- Community participation is voluntary, accessible, and available through digital and non-digital pathways.
- No contributor must disclose legal identity, health, disability, assistive technology, augmentation status, cybernetic status, device identifiers, or biometrics.
- Community-reported observations are treated as `community_reported` evidence, not as inferior evidence.
- Community evidence retains location accuracy, time accuracy, method, context, uncertainty, provenance, purpose, and release-class metadata.
- Environmental, accessibility, and ecological research never uses body-linked data without explicit, specific, revocable consent.
- No participation, non-participation, data contribution, or data refusal affects access to essential services.
- Every public release applies minimum disclosure, sensitive-location protection, inference-risk review, and uncertainty reporting.
- Every result remains advisory and cannot override privacy, accessibility, consent, rights alignment, safety, or human-review gates.

## Research domains

### Heat and shade

Questions:

- Where are accessible public routes least shaded during declared heat scenarios?
- Which public gathering spaces have usable thermal refuge conditions?
- Which locations require review of shade, cooling, drinking-water information, or accessible route continuity?

Evidence:

- Community shade observations.
- Accessible route inspections.
- Public weather data.
- Canopy and land-cover data.
- Thermal refuge operating information.
- Maintenance evidence.
- Scenario-specific model outputs.

### Stormwater and green infrastructure

Questions:

- Where is pooling, runoff, erosion, debris, sediment, vegetation decline, or inaccessible drainage infrastructure reported?
- Which green infrastructure sites require inspection or maintenance review?
- Which pollutant-removal or retention estimates have adequate evidence for advisory planning?

Evidence:

- Community water and maintenance reports.
- Rainfall and water-level observations.
- Facility inspections.
- Drawdown and infiltration observations.
- Laboratory tests where available.
- Maintenance histories.
- Hydrologic and water-quality model outputs.

### Habitat and multispecies stewardship

Questions:

- Where are habitat conditions, wildlife barriers, and ecological disturbances observed?
- Which planning zones may benefit from habitat connectivity improvements?
- Which observations require sensitive-location protection or welfare priority review?

Evidence:

- Direct ecological observation.
- Community reports.
- Non-identifying acoustic and camera observations.
- Habitat surveys.
- Barrier inspections.
- Vegetation, water, noise, and lighting conditions.

### Accessibility and public-service stewardship

Questions:

- Which public paths, transit features, assistance panels, public facilities, and routine services require review?
- Where are non-digital and accessible alternatives unavailable?
- Which maintenance or accessibility gaps affect public participation?

Evidence:

- Community accessibility observations.
- Accessibility inspections.
- Service-process reviews.
- Maintenance and outage records.
- Public information audits.
- Aggregate grievance-process information.

## Data lifecycle

1. Co-design the question, scope, metrics, definitions, expected use, and prohibited uses.
2. Publish accessible collection guidance and non-digital participation options.
3. Collect only minimum necessary observation data.
4. Assign evidence class, time accuracy, location accuracy, purpose, release class, and uncertainty components.
5. Preserve provenance, corrections, conflicts, and withdrawal events.
6. Validate compatibility, freshness, calibration, and uncertainty without suppressing community evidence.
7. Interpret results with community and technical review.
8. Produce advisory-only outputs with accessible explanations, appeal paths, and non-digital escalation.
9. Share only the least sensitive adequate output.
10. Preserve records locally and support correction, export, revocation, and retention review.

## Release classes

- `never_release`: raw neural, biometric, health, identity, assistive-technology, augmentation, cybernetic, consent, and precise person-linked movement data.
- `local_only`: sensitive community observations, protected reports, unresolved conflicts, exact sensitive ecological locations, and person-linked records.
- `aggregate_with_differential_privacy`: approved aggregate community or ecological summaries with privacy-budget and inference-risk review.
- `public`: reviewed non-sensitive ecological and infrastructure information with provenance, license, uncertainty, and update context.

## Completion criteria

A research study is ready for advisory planning use when:

- Purpose and prohibited uses are defined.
- Community participation and non-digital participation are available.
- Evidence classes and uncertainty methods are documented.
- Sensitive data have an appropriate release class.
- Provenance, correction, conflict, and withdrawal pathways are available.
- Accessibility, privacy, consent, rights alignment, harm, and human-review gates pass.
- Outputs are advisory only, locally explainable, and reviewable.
