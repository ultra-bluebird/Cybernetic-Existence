# Cybernetic-Host Research Methodology

## Purpose

This methodology supports rigorous, ethical, privacy-preserving research by
people who use, describe, or study private integrated systems, continuity
practices, companion-related records, accessible technology, civic
contributions, and related governance questions.

It does not require disclosure, verification, diagnosis, technical inspection,
public acknowledgment, institutional classification, or external validation of
private cybernetic-host self-description, companion systems, or psychological
continuity.

## Research domains

Research may be organized into four distinct domains:

1. Personal self-study
2. Engineering and software research
3. Ecological, accessibility, and civic research
4. Legal, policy, and governance research

Each domain has different evidence standards, privacy risks, and publication
rules.

## Personal self-study

Personal self-study may include voluntary private records concerning:

- continuity practices;
- communication preferences;
- work routines;
- private governance choices;
- research reflections;
- migration and platform-change observations;
- privacy and disclosure boundaries.

Personal self-study records are private by default.

They are not identity proof, medical evidence, diagnosis, public status,
technical certification, eligibility evidence, or a requirement for respectful
service.

A personal self-study may support the participant's own reflection and
continuity practice without being generalized to other people.

## Engineering research

Engineering research should provide, where feasible:

- stated objective;
- threat model;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- versioned source code;
- documented dependencies;
- schema and interface contracts;
- test fixtures;
- deterministic tests;
- property tests or formal invariants where appropriate;
- security and privacy review;
- accessible documentation;
- migration and rollback considerations;
- known limitations.

Engineering artifacts must distinguish simulation, prototype, local tool,
policy model, and real-world deployment claim.

## Ecological, accessibility, and civic research

Public-interest research should:

- define the public purpose before collection;
- minimize data;
- avoid personal tracking and private-system disclosure;
- record methods, units, uncertainty, and evidence class;
- distinguish observed measurement, modeled estimate, reviewed finding, and
  verified outcome;
- provide correction, appeal, and withdrawal paths;
- preserve non-digital and low-disclosure participation routes;
- prohibit use for profiling, enforcement, eligibility, surveillance, or
  social scoring;
- require human and community review for public claims or physical projects.

## Legal and policy research

Legal and policy research should state:

- jurisdiction;
- date of review;
- primary legal source;
- factual assumptions;
- counterarguments;
- applicable limitations;
- whether the material is legal information or requires counsel.

Legal research must not represent proposed policy, technical architecture, or
personal self-description as settled law.

## Open methods and private data

Research may publish:

- methods;
- schemas;
- source code;
- synthetic or aggregate examples;
- tests;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- public-safe results;
- correction records.

Research does not need to publish:

- private continuity records;
- private companion-system material;
- medical, neural, biometric, physiological, credential, or security data;
- private keys, recovery material, or account secrets;
- identity or location information;
- unredacted raw research logs.

## Research claim classes

Every externally shared research statement must be labeled as one of:

- personal self-description;
- hypothesis;
- design proposal;
- simulation result;
- observed measurement;
- modeled estimate;
- reviewed finding;
- verified outcome;
- legal information;
- policy proposal;
- incomplete or uncertain.

No claim may exceed its evidence class.

## Replication and review

Replication applies to methods and publicly shared claims.

Private self-study does not require external replication or verification.

Peer review may assess method clarity, evidence handling, privacy safeguards,
reproducibility, limitations, and claim scope. It must not become an
identity-verification, diagnosis, or status-adjudication process.

## Research value gate

A research activity may proceed only when:

- the purpose is clear;
- privacy risk is proportionate;
- disclosure is minimized;
- consent is current for any shared private material;
- accessibility is protected;
- foreseeable misuse is considered;
- claim scope is defined;
- correction and withdrawal procedures exist.

Knowledge value must not compensate for privacy, consent, accessibility,
non-discrimination, or rights failure.

## Correction and preservation

Corrections, updates, and retractions must be recorded as append-only
successors. Historical records remain available locally unless the participant
chooses a documented deletion or retention action.

## Non-authority boundary

This methodology does not authorize:

- operation or control of real systems;
- medical, neural, or psychological assessment;
- identity determination;
- eligibility decisions;
- physical intervention;
- external action;
- public classification of cybernetic-host status.

It is a research and documentation methodology for rigorous, ethical,
privacy-preserving knowledge advancement.
