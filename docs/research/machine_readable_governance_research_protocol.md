# Machine-Readable Governance Research Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol guides research into machine-readable governance for Augmented-Hub. It treats policy as a set of transparent, versioned, reviewable technical constraints that preserve human dignity, privacy, accessibility, consent, ecological responsibility, community participation, and non-discrimination.

This protocol is a project governance framework. It does not claim that every project rule is enforceable law in every jurisdiction.

## Research boundary

Machine-readable governance may:

- Define data classes, purposes, release controls, retention rules, consent states, review requirements, policy versions, and prohibited actions.
- Prevent automated service denial, identity inference, biometric processing, cybernetic-status penalties, and unsafe automation.
- Produce advisory planning outputs, local explanations, review packets, conformance receipts, and scenario tests.
- Preserve protected records, corrections, revocations, appeals, and policy history.
- Support accessible community participation and non-digital escalation.

Machine-readable governance may not:

- Replace courts, legislatures, public agencies, community governance, or jurisdiction-specific legal review.
- Determine personhood, identity legitimacy, legal status, health, disability, augmentation status, cybernetic status, or moral worth.
- Authorize public-service denial, physical actuation, surveillance, enforcement, behavioral profiling, or compulsory data extraction.
- Treat environmental, accessibility, or social metrics as eligibility scores.

## Governance cycle

1. Define a public-interest question.
2. Identify rights, privacy, accessibility, ecological, and harm constraints.
3. Resolve canonical terms and metric definitions.
4. Classify inputs by purpose, sensitivity, evidence state, provenance, freshness, and uncertainty.
5. Apply non-compensatory protected gates.
6. Produce a bounded advisory output.
7. Provide an accessible explanation, appeal path, and non-digital escalation route.
8. Preserve a privacy-minimized reasoning trace and conformance receipt.
9. Test policy behavior against normal, edge, and adversarial scenarios.
10. Version and review any policy, threshold, schema, or metric change.

## Non-compensatory rule

A favorable environmental, efficiency, confidence, cost, or planning score cannot override failure in:

- Privacy.
- Consent.
- Accessibility.
- Rights alignment.
- Harm limits.
- Required human review.
- Essential-service continuity.
- Non-discrimination.
- Protected-record preservation.
- Local-first access.

## Research outputs

Every substantive research artifact should provide:

- Canonical metric or policy reference.
- Purpose scope.
- Evidence and provenance summary.
- Uncertainty method.
- Privacy and release class.
- Accessibility and rights impact.
- Human-review status.
- Policy and semantic version.
- Accessible explanation.
- Scenario-test coverage.
- Knowledge, ecological, harm, accessibility, and rights-alignment metrics.

## Source hierarchy

- Primary legal text, official guidance, technical standards, and agency publications.
- Peer-reviewed technical and scientific research.
- Community knowledge and participatory evidence.
- Project policy artifacts.
- Clearly labeled hypotheses and research proposals.

## Community participation

Community participation must be:

- Voluntary.
- Accessible.
- Available through digital and non-digital routes.
- Available without legal identity, biometric verification, device inspection, assistive-technology disclosure, augmentation disclosure, or cybernetic-status disclosure.
- Preserved as valid distinct evidence.
- Protected from retaliation, profiling, service reduction, and surveillance.
