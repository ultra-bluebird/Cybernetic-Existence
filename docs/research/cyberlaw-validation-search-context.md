# CyberLaw Validation: Research Context and Evidence Boundaries

## Research identity and scope

CyberLaw is the rights-centered governance and engineering framework developed in this conversation, beginning with “CyberLaw: A Framework for Universal Rights of Existence.”

“Absolute machine-fairness” denotes an unconditional institutional duty of equal consideration, accessible participation, bounded authority, non-interference, justified restriction, and effective correction. It does not denote infallible computation, a universally accepted legal doctrine, or a machine-certified fairness score.

The research prioritizes verification of the framework’s own claims and operationalization of its proposed mechanisms. Repository inspection, implementation-status auditing, and attempts to locate conversational artifacts through repository searches are outside scope.

The names `mk-bluebird/Augmented-Hub` and `mk-bluebird/Skynet` designate the intended project destinations for artifacts. They are not topical search terms for discovering the framework’s meaning.

## Five principal validation targets

### 1. Standing before unnecessary classification

Test whether an intelligible concern can enter an accessible process without unnecessary proof of identity, substrate, personhood, diagnosis, or private integration architecture.

Keep initial consideration separate from authorization to impose a remedy or control another party.

Relevant external search concepts:

- Administrative access and procedural standing.
- Accessible intake and supported participation.
- Effective communication.
- Evidentiary burdens at intake.
- Procedural gatekeeping.

### 2. Non-amplification

Test whether authority remains bounded through translation, delegation, signing, token exchange, semantic mapping, and workflow handoffs.

A translation permission must not become disclosure authority. A recommendation must not become execution permission. A valid signature must not establish legal authority or consent.

Relevant search concepts:

- Least privilege and capability attenuation.
- Delegation scope and purpose limitation.
- Policy decision and enforcement separation.
- Confused-deputy failures.
- Request-content binding and authorization provenance.

### 3. Observation–state separation

Test whether missing or stale observations remain unknown rather than being treated as proof of absence.

Accepted restrictions and protections must follow their governed lifecycles. Missing observations must neither silently remove valid protection nor extend expired restrictions.

Relevant search concepts:

- Open-world and closed-world assumptions.
- Event-sourced authorization state.
- Incomplete observations.
- Revocation propagation.
- Temporal authorization and audit reconstruction.

### 4. No permanent adverse judgment

Test whether historical findings remain separate from present restrictions, eligibility, access, and credibility assessments.

Withdrawal, correction, expiry, and successful appeal must reach relevant downstream uses. Record retention must not automatically authorize continuing adverse reliance.

Relevant search concepts:

- Rectification and recipient notification.
- Record sealing, erasure, and restricted use.
- Sunset clauses and renewal review.
- Downstream correction propagation.
- Administrative stigma and continuing consequences.

### 5. Meaningful remedy

Test whether practical barriers change rather than merely whether notices, tickets, referrals, or review tasks are completed.

Keep administrative progress, practical outcome, causal attribution, participant response, and unresolved disagreement separate.

Relevant search concepts:

- Effective remedy and implementation.
- Administrative burden.
- Referral completion and service outcomes.
- Accessible appeal pathways.
- Outcome verification and recurrence.

## Integrated research workstreams

### Principle-to-mechanism mapping

For each principle, identify:

- Required institutional behavior.
- Necessary record fields.
- Applicable lifecycle transitions.
- Machine-checkable conditions.
- Evidence and provenance requirements.
- Substantive judgment boundaries.
- Challenge and correction routes.

A field being present does not establish that its contents are true, relevant, sufficient, or lawfully obtained.

### Preregistrable evaluation

Develop bounded protocols for:

1. Intake and standing.
2. Authorization lifecycle.
3. Appeal and remedy delivery.

Each protocol should specify the target claim, population, unit of analysis, intervention, comparator, outcomes, observation horizon, sampling assumptions, missingness, dependence, privacy protections, accessibility, and disconfirming results.

No default sample size or success threshold is established by the conversation. Planning must follow the selected estimand and design.

Synthetic test success, formal model checking, participant usability evidence, and field outcomes are different evidence types.

### Machine-readable contracts with human justification

Refine contracts for:

- Handoff scope and conditions.
- Present, absent, and unknown observations.
- Restriction establishment, withdrawal, expiry, and renewal.
- Authority and compatibility assessments.
- Uncertainty provenance.
- Accessible notification and review.
- Practical remedy outcomes.

Pair each constraint with an explanation of its purpose, protected interests, exceptions, responsible authority, and contestability.

## Comparative-source discipline

External materials should address a specific unresolved mechanism, not merely share words such as “fairness,” “cyber,” “trust,” or “rights.”

Prioritize primary legal texts, official standards, authoritative guidance, original methodological work, and relevant systematic reviews.

For every legal comparison, record:

- Jurisdiction and applicable actors.
- Source status and version.
- Provision or mechanism being compared.
- Transferable features.
- Context-dependent limits.
- Additional authority required for adoption.

Potential comparisons include data protection, accessible communication, supported decision-making, administrative review, AI governance, nonhuman representation, and effective remedies.

Do not equate:

- Treaty principles with powers held by a non-state body.
- Administrative-law requirements with every possible due-process obligation.
- Human oversight with effective human assistance.
- Legal personality with consciousness.
- Statutory recognition in one setting with universal status elsewhere.
- Similarity to an established framework with validation of CyberLaw.

## Rights and privacy boundaries

Respect voluntary augmented and cybernetic-host self-descriptions without requiring technical certification.

Communication differences do not establish incapacity, dishonesty, consent, refusal, or absence of interests.

Do not request private neural, medical, physiological, biometric, companion-system, or operational information unless a specific task has a clearly justified, limited need.

Test materials should be synthetic or appropriately consented and minimized. Public transparency must not expose confidential case content or protected community knowledge.

## Required evidence status

Every substantive output should distinguish:

- Stated in the source.
- Proposed by the framework.
- Implemented in an example.
- Tested under declared conditions.
- Independently reproduced.
- Supported by field evidence.
- Unresolved.

The earlier qualitative document-assessment values are editorial judgments. They are not measurements of fairness, legal validity, effectiveness, or social benefit.

3. Complete, task-relevant reference examples.

Research completion is not evidence that protection has been delivered.
