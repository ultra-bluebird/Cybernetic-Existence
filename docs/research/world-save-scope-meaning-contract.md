### Step 1 — Hybridize: A Constructive Behavioral Model

Model: bounded scope → change review → faithful explanation → comprehension evaluation

The hybrid model protects two boundaries simultaneously:

1. Operational boundary: an accepted milestone does not silently authorize a new purpose, recipient, interface, intervention, or retention practice.
2. Meaning boundary: a protected record does not imply that a person’s body is irreversible or that future choice has been surrendered.

Its governing principle is:

> Preserve the accepted achievement, reassess changes against explicit limits, and explain record protection without implying bodily finality or continuing permission.

Core operating principles

1. Define the envelope before evaluating changes.  
   “Beyond declared bounds” is meaningful only when the bounds, units, conditions, and semantic vocabulary are recorded.

2. Do not use field names as the whole authorization model.  
   An unchanged recipient category can conceal a changed onward-sharing practice. An unchanged interface name can conceal a changed data flow.

3. Review combinations as well as individual fields.  
   A purpose and recipient may each be allowed individually while their combination is prohibited.

4. Distinguish historical acceptance from current support.  
   A material change can invalidate transfer of an earlier assurance without erasing the fact that the original milestone was accepted.

5. Invalidate affected dependencies—not merely the changed field.  
   A recipient change may affect privacy, continuity, and the public explanation. Those consequences can cross field boundaries.

6. Treat renewal as a proposed continuation, not automatic permission.  
   Repeating the same activity may still require renewed authorization when its validity period ends.

7. Preserve unknowns.  
   Missing bounds, an unsupported migration, or an unclear change should produce an unresolved assessment—not presumed inheritance.

8. Use literal public language.  
   Describe protection against unauthorized record alteration rather than suggesting that change is technically impossible.

9. Keep physical claims separate.  
   Record protection does not establish what can or cannot be changed in a person’s body.

10. Preserve future choice and ordinary access.  
    Scope review must not prevent a person from requesting change, declining a new activity, seeking explanation, or obtaining accessible assistance.

Mathematical resource: scope-envelope assessment

Let a proposed processing configuration be:

\[
x=(p,r,i,a,\tau)
\]

where the components describe purpose, recipients, interface, intervention or activity, and retention.

Define the reviewed envelope:

\[
\Omega_B=
\left\{
x:
\bigwedge_f B_f(x_f)
\land
\bigwedge_k C_k(x)
\right\}
\]

Here:

- \(B_f\) defines a field’s permitted bounds.
- \(C_k\) defines cross-field conditions.
- The envelope is a reviewed policy model—not proof of operational compliance.

For a proposed configuration \(x'\):

\[
\operatorname{ScopeAssessment}(x')=
\begin{cases}
\text{within declared envelope},
&\text{membership is established}\\
\text{fresh review required},
&\text{a reviewed bound or condition is exceeded}\\
\text{unknown},
&\text{required meaning or evidence is unresolved}
\end{cases}
\]

Crucially:

\[
x'\in\Omega_B
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{ExecutionAuthorized}(x')
\]

Current authorization, validity, exact operation binding, and any required operational approval remain separate.

There is no universal smallest material change. The smallest change depends on the field’s semantics and declared boundary. A one-second retention increase can cross an explicit maximum; a large wording edit may preserve meaning but still require presentation review.

Comprehension experiment

Use two randomized conditions:

- Arm A headline: “World-save: record irreversibility.”
- Arm B headline: “World-save: protected continuity.”

Keep the explanatory body, facts, scope, and accessible participation options the same:

> “This milestone protects the recorded achievement against unauthorized alteration. It does not establish whether a bodily change can be reversed. It does not authorize future activities. You may request later changes and corrections.”

Primary questions should be separate:

1. Record-level question:  
   “What protection does this milestone provide for the recorded achievement?”

2. Physical-level question:  
   “What, if anything, does this statement establish about whether a bodily change can be reversed?”

For question \(q\) and arm \(a\):

\[
\hat e_{a,q}=
\frac{m_{a,q}}{n_{a,q}}
\]

where \(m\) is incorrect responses and \(n\) is scorable responses.

Define separate effects:

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{record}}
=
p_{B,\mathrm{record}}-p_{A,\mathrm{record}}
\]

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{physical}}
=
p_{B,\mathrm{physical}}-p_{A,\mathrm{physical}}
\]

Negative values indicate lower error under “protected continuity.”

Preregister the primary comparisons, sample-size justification, missing-response handling, and multiplicity approach. Report uncertainty, confidence ratings, and explicit “cannot determine” responses separately. Do not infer reduced competence from response time, communication method, or misunderstanding.

### Step 3 — Final Mutation

Name

World-Save Scope-and-Meaning Continuity Contract

Core Mechanism

The mutation couples two independently evaluated records:

| Record | Required content | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Scope-transfer record | Reviewed envelope, cross-field conditions, dependencies, change assessment, renewal status, current authorization limits | Reader understanding or bodily irreversibility |
| Meaning-protection record | Approved public wording, explicit record/body distinction, future-choice statement, comprehension findings, correction route | Operational compliance or execution authority |

Its central relationship is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{protected achievement}
\neq
\text{blanket future permission}
\neq
\text{bodily irreversibility}
}
\]

The mechanism requires every proposed scope expansion to receive the applicable review, and every public summary to preserve those distinctions.

Why It Is Novel

The proposed contribution is the coupling of scope-inheritance control with protection against interpretive overreach.

It asks two questions together:

> “Does the proposed activity remain within the reviewed permission boundary?”

and:

> “Does the public explanation make that boundary understandable without implying a bodily guarantee?”

The distinctive synthesis is that continuity must survive both kinds of drift:

- Operational drift: a milestone is reused to justify a new activity.
- Semantic drift: record protection is described as physical finality or unrestricted consent.

The supplied material supports this as a proposed architectural synthesis, not a claim of historical priority or demonstrated effectiveness.
