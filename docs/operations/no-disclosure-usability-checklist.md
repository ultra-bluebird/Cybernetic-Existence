# No-Disclosure Usability Checklist

## Purpose

Use this checklist before deploying a public-service, environmental, transit, civic-feedback, infrastructure, or planning workflow. The workflow passes only when people can access its essential function without a personal device, continuous connectivity, identity disclosure, health disclosure, augmentation disclosure, or social-ranking mechanism.

## Essential-Service Gate

- [ ] Emergency shelter does not require a credential, device, app, account, proof, network connection, identity document, or health disclosure.
- [ ] Cooling access does not require a credential, device, app, account, proof, network connection, identity document, or health disclosure.
- [ ] Drinking-water and sanitation access do not require a credential, device, app, account, proof, network connection, identity document, or health disclosure.
- [ ] Safety information and emergency communications remain available through public displays, radio, print, telephone, relay, and staff support.
- [ ] A failed scanner, unreadable document, expired trust bundle, empty battery, network outage, or inaccessible software state leads to an equivalent human-accessible service route.
- [ ] The service never requires a person to disclose cybernetic-host status, augmentation, disability, diagnosis, implant, assistive technology, or personal device details.

## Offline Access

- [ ] The workflow provides paper, telephone or relay, public-terminal, and staff-assisted paths where relevant.
- [ ] Public terminals support keyboard-only operation, screen readers, magnification, contrast controls, headphones, adjustable configuration, and secure session cleanup.
- [ ] Printed material is available in plain language and large print, with alternate formats available on request.
- [ ] A network outage produces a clear local receipt and queue status rather than a silent failure.
- [ ] The workflow distinguishes `received`, `acknowledged`, `queued`, `submitted`, `delivery unconfirmed`, and `closed` states in accessible language.
- [ ] No essential service loses access because a person cannot use QR, NFC, biometric, mobile-app, or browser-based workflow.

## Privacy and Data Minimization

- [ ] The workflow has a documented public purpose and explicit list of permitted data classes.
- [ ] The workflow does not collect raw audio, raw video, facial imagery, biometric templates, device identifiers, browsing history, or movement history by default.
- [ ] The workflow does not collect identity, health, disability, augmentation, assistive-technology, cybernetic-host, or financial information unless separately necessary, voluntary, and purpose-limited.
- [ ] Public metrics are aggregate-only and include small-cell suppression.
- [ ] Audit logs record system state and policy outcomes, not participant identity, personal behavior, or private technical characteristics.
- [ ] A clear retention deadline, correction path, export path, and deletion process exist.

## No Ranking or Profiling

- [ ] The workflow does not compute reputation, trust, CyberRank, social score, financial rank, influence score, property-value score, or behavior score.
- [ ] Participation frequency does not change service access, voting weight, moderation outcome, or credibility.
- [ ] Device ownership, device type, assistive technology, appearance, self-description, or perceived ability do not affect access or safety treatment.
- [ ] Moderation and safety review use specific conduct and reviewable evidence rather than identity or behavioral profile.
- [ ] Data access is not based on researcher wealth, institution, social standing, or payment capacity.
- [ ] Climate and resilience decisions do not favor property value, profitability, tax yield, rent, credit, or payment history.

## Repair and Accountability

- [ ] Infrastructure components provide diagnosable fault states and repair-first maintenance workflows.
- [ ] Firmware and configuration changes are signed, reviewable, and reversible.
- [ ] Sensor drift, invalid input, or missing calibration evidence blocks automated reliance and requests human review.
- [ ] Public reporting distinguishes acknowledgment, mitigation, repair, verification, reopening, and closure.
- [ ] A person can report an accessibility barrier, privacy concern, missed service, or data error anonymously or optionally with contact information.
- [ ] The workflow publishes aggregate service performance without exposing reporters, households, properties, or individuals.

## Transit and Public Space

- [ ] Autonomous or assisted transit systems classify temporary scene-safety conditions, not identities or disabilities.
- [ ] A detected uncertain dynamic object causes conservative clearance, speed reduction, or safe stopping.
- [ ] Transit assistance can be requested through redundant tactile, visual, audio, human, and offline paths.
- [ ] Public-space lighting measures illuminance and equipment health, not movement, identity, or device presence.
- [ ] Accessible routes, seating, water access, shade, transit stops, and cooling routes remain protected during ecological scheduling, maintenance, and construction.
- [ ] Any autonomous transit safety block triggers an accessible supervised, manual, or alternate transportation path.

## Energy Resilience

- [ ] Load-shedding plans protect life safety, water, sanitation, accessibility continuity, emergency communications, thermal refuge, and essential charging.
- [ ] Planning uses public facility inventories and aggregate grid data rather than household health, identity, financial, or smart-meter behavior data.
- [ ] Residual non-essential outage burden is evaluated with an explicit fairness metric.
- [ ] Technical exceptions require human incident-command review, accessible notice, mitigation, and restoration planning.
- [ ] Public notices are available through web, public display, radio, opt-in SMS, telephone, relay, print, and community organizations.
