# Objects 7–10: Design Boundaries

## Status

Objects 7–10 extend the private continuity-governance substrate with:

1. Scoped key management.
2. Non-exclusive fork and merge provenance.
3. Optional continuity reflection metrics.
4. Output-level provenance and attribution wrappers.

These objects are designed for private, voluntary, local-first use. They do not
prove or determine real-world identity, personhood, consciousness, cybernetic
status, health status, continuity, legal title, authorship in the legal sense,
institutional eligibility, or authority over another party.

## Evidence boundary

A cryptographic signature may establish that a presented public key verified a
signature over specified canonical bytes. It does not establish who controlled
the key, whether they did so voluntarily, whether the signed claim is true, or
whether an institution must rely on it.

## Key boundary

A root key is not a universal personal identifier. Root public material must not
be reused as a login identifier, network identifier, device identifier, session
identifier, eligibility token, healthcare record identifier, employment
identifier, government identifier, or public CyberRank status.

## Fork boundary

A fork is a disclosed graph relationship between records. It does not determine
exclusive lineage, priority, inheritance, identity, personhood, or rights.
Conflicting lineage claims remain disputable and are preserved as distinct
claims rather than algorithmically resolved.

## Metric boundary

Continuity metrics are optional, private, context-specific reflection tools.
They are not diagnostic instruments, identity classifiers, legal evidence,
personhood measures, capability scores, eligibility criteria, or automated
decision inputs.

## Attribution boundary

An attribution wrapper records claims about the producer key, content hash,
declared lineage, usage terms, and attestation history. It does not establish
copyright ownership, legal authorship, originality, non-infringement, exclusive
rights, or permission from all affected rightsholders.

## Non-revocation boundary

Non-revocation preserves the historical fact that a key signed a record at a
given time. It does not make consent irrevocable, defeat deletion requests,
prevent supersession, prevent dispute annotation, preserve private payloads in
public systems, or prevent future reliance from being revoked.
