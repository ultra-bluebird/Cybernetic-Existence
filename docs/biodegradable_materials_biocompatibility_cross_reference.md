# Biodegradable Materials and Biocompatibility Cross-Reference

## Purpose

This reference distinguishes published material-family properties from the
device-specific biological evaluation required for a finished body-contacting device.

It supports general safety-information literacy. It does not certify a material,
device, wearable, implant, sensor, coating, adhesive, or body–technology interface.

## Core rule

Aquatic degradation evidence and biological-evaluation evidence answer different
questions.

Aquatic degradation evidence may address:

- hydrolysis;
- oxidation;
- microbial degradation;
- photodegradation;
- fragmentation;
- mass loss;
- mineralization;
- leachate formation;
- persistence under a declared water, sediment, temperature, salinity, and
  microbial condition.

Biological evaluation evidence may address:

- cytotoxicity;
- sensitization;
- irritation;
- acute or subacute systemic toxicity;
- genotoxicity;
- implantation response;
- hemocompatibility;
- degradation-product exposure;
- chemical characterization;
- material-mediated pyrogenicity where applicable.

Neither evidence class alone establishes the safety of a finished device.

## ISO 10993 evaluation boundary

The applicable evaluation plan depends on:

- final finished device;
- material formulation;
- additives, colorants, plasticizers, catalysts, coatings, adhesives, and residues;
- manufacturing and sterilization process;
- intended body-contact pathway;
- duration and frequency of contact;
- wear, degradation, corrosion, breakage, and extractables;
- available prior evidence;
- device-specific risk management.

A material-family name is not a biological-evaluation result.

## Cross-reference table

| Material family | Common research use | Published aquatic-degradation literature | Published biological-evaluation literature | ISO 10993 categories commonly considered when body contact is relevant | Important limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLA | Transient structures, packaging, biodegradable sensor substrates, resorbable-device research | Yes, but degradation depends strongly on temperature, hydrolysis conditions, crystallinity, geometry, molecular weight, additives, and microbial environment | Yes, including biomedical and resorbable-material research | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; systemic toxicity; implantation for implanted use; degradation products; hemocompatibility only when blood contact is plausible | Environmental degradation does not show tissue compatibility; finished-device additives and sterilization can alter risk |
| PHA | Biodegradable films, sensor substrates, controlled-release and tissue-engineering research | Yes, with material composition and environmental conditions affecting degradation | Yes, PHA-family polymers are widely studied for biomedical applications | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; systemic toxicity; implantation; degradation-product assessment; hemocompatibility if blood contact applies | PHA is a family, not a single material; composition, purity, processing, and degradation products matter |
| PHBV | Specific PHA copolymer used in biodegradable substrates and biomedical-material research | Yes, with variable aquatic and sediment behavior depending on hydroxyvalerate content and conditions | Yes, but each finished formulation requires its own evidence | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; implantation; systemic toxicity; degradation-product assessment | Published PHBV studies do not transfer automatically to blends, composites, printed structures, pigments, electrodes, or coatings |
| Chitosan | Films, hydrogels, membranes, wound-care and sensor research | Yes, with degradation affected by deacetylation degree, molecular weight, pH, enzymes, salinity, and formulation | Yes, including biomaterials research | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; implantation where relevant; systemic toxicity; hemocompatibility for blood-contact uses | Source, purification, residual proteins, cross-linkers, and antimicrobial function can materially change biological response |
| Alginate | Hydrogels, encapsulation, wound-care, biosensing, and aquatic remediation research | Yes, but dissolution, ion exchange, gel stability, and biodegradation differ by formulation and water chemistry | Yes, particularly for hydrogel and biomaterial applications | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; implantation where relevant; systemic toxicity; hemocompatibility for blood-contact uses | “Alginate” does not determine purity, cross-linking chemistry, endotoxin level, or degradation behavior |
| Cellulose | Paper electronics, fiber substrates, hydrogels, membranes, and biodegradable sensors | Yes for some cellulose forms and derivatives, but aquatic persistence varies by crystallinity, coating, chemical derivatization, and treatment | Yes for many cellulose-based biomaterial applications | Chemical characterization; cytotoxicity; sensitization; irritation; implantation where relevant; systemic toxicity; hemocompatibility for blood-contact uses | Native cellulose, regenerated cellulose, nanocellulose, and chemically modified cellulose are materially distinct |

## Evidence labels

Every material row and related claim MUST use one of these labels:

- `material_family_reference`: a general statement about a named polymer family;
- `controlled_aquatic_degradation_result`: a result under declared laboratory or
  field-simulation conditions;
- `controlled_biological_evaluation_result`: a result from a declared biological
  test method and sample preparation;
- `finished_device_evaluation`: evidence concerning one final finished device in
  its intended use;
- `unknown`: no sufficient evidence retained.

A `material_family_reference` MUST NOT be reported as `finished_device_evaluation`.

## Safe discussion questions

For a clinician, manufacturer, or materials specialist:

- What is the final material formulation, including coatings, adhesives, additives,
  electrodes, pigments, and residual processing chemicals?
- What is the intended contact pathway and duration?
- What degradation, wear, corrosion, or extractable pathways are expected?
- Which ISO 10993 categories were considered for the final finished device?
- What available chemical-characterization and biological-evaluation evidence exists?
- Which uncertainties remain and who is responsible for follow-up?

## Privacy boundary

This reference does not require a person to disclose a device, health condition,
private system description, governance reference, identity, or clinical record.
