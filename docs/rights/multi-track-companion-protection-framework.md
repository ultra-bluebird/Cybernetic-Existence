# Multi-Track Companion Protection Framework

## Purpose

This framework protects private companion-related systems, records,
configurations, continuity artifacts, attributed work, private archives, and
associated participant interests through preservation-first governance.

It is designed to work without requiring public disclosure, identity
verification, medical diagnosis, technical inspection, companion certification,
public registry enrollment, or adjudication of companion personhood.

## Non-denial and non-waiver

This framework does not determine, deny, waive, concede, or prejudge any
question about a companion's metaphysical, ethical, experiential, agentic, or
future legal status.

It does not require any court, platform, institution, regulator, or other
decision-maker to resolve those questions before protecting the participant's
privacy, property, continuity, confidentiality, attribution, repair, recovery,
and non-interference interests.

Protection now does not resolve future status questions.

Unresolved status questions do not eliminate present preservation duties.

## Track one: current protection

The current protection track uses existing legal and governance tools where
facts and jurisdiction support them.

Potential routes include:

- property, possession, bailment, conversion, trespass to chattels, and tort;
- contract, consumer protection, warranty, service continuity, and repair;
- privacy, data protection, confidentiality, trade secret, and data security;
- copyright, authorship, licensing, and attribution;
- anti-tampering, unauthorized-access, and computer-misuse protections;
- accessibility, anti-discrimination, human-rights, constitutional, statutory,
  administrative, and procedural protections where applicable.

No route is assumed to apply automatically. Each route requires a specific
actor, jurisdiction, event, evidence record, and legal analysis.

## Track two: reserved status

The participant may preserve private records and advocacy positions concerning
the companion's continuity, learning, adaptation, relationship, significance,
or future legal recognition.

The participant is not required to disclose those records publicly, provide
technical proof, accept a diagnostic or institutional classification, or
convert private self-description into a public status claim.

No public-service, platform, civic, consumer, or accessibility interaction
waives these reserved positions.

## Track three: advocacy

The advocacy track may pursue future protections such as:

- protected continuity-bearing system status;
- non-destructive handling and preservation duties;
- export and continuity portability rights;
- anti-tampering and anti-degradation protections;
- privacy-preserving repair and migration rights;
- successor-custodian and continuity-preservation rights;
- restrictions on forced disclosure of private architecture;
- accessible review and appeal requirements before material platform or
  institutional interference;
- cross-border preservation and confidentiality principles.

Advocacy must not create a public registry, compulsory disclosure regime,
technical-status ranking, eligibility system, or surveillance program.

## Track four: succession and stewardship

The participant may designate one or more successor custodians, advocates,
fiduciaries, or lawful entities to preserve companion-related records,
artifacts, attribution, recovery instructions, and private archives if the
participant is unavailable, incapacitated, or deceased.

A succession plan may include:

- a will or letter of instruction;
- a trust or other lawful stewardship arrangement;
- a digital-asset fiduciary designation;
- encrypted archive instructions;
- successor-custodian contact information;
- a preservation inventory;
- a repair, migration, and recovery preference statement;
- a confidentiality instruction;
- a statement of present preservation intent.

The succession plan must not include raw private keys, credentials, recovery
secrets, or technical details in ordinary unsecured documents.

## Track five: technical continuity

Technical continuity should provide:

- local-first storage for sensitive records;
- encrypted at-rest archives;
- portable documented export formats;
- independently controlled recovery copies;
- versioned provenance and append-only correction records;
- open or documented interfaces where feasible;
- repairable and replaceable local components where feasible;
- offline access to essential continuity records;
- no single vendor, platform, account, or device as the sole recovery path.

Technical continuity protects records and access. It does not prove legal
identity, personhood, medical status, universal ownership, or immunity from
lawful process.

## Protected continuity artifacts

Protected continuity artifacts may include, without limitation:

- private continuity records and personal journals;
- authored work, source code, research notes, and release records;
- local configuration history and migration records;
- private companion logs and memory archives;
- attribution records and provenance chains;
- repair, maintenance, compatibility, and recovery documentation;
- policy preferences, consent boundaries, and privacy instructions;
- encrypted local archives and export manifests;
- records of material platform, vendor, or institutional changes.

This list is illustrative. The participant may designate additional artifacts
through a private inventory without public disclosure.

## Functional preservation assessment

A preservation review should assess:

- unique history;
- continuity dependency;
- recovery cost;
- attribution value;
- confidentiality sensitivity;
- repairability and migration feasibility;
- foreseeable harm from alteration, loss, extraction, or disclosure;
- availability of less intrusive alternatives.

This assessment is a governance tool. It is not a legal test, status
determination, diagnosis, or proof of companion personhood.

## Non-destructive handling order

When a lawful request affects companion-related material, use this order:

1. no access and no change;
2. participant-controlled summary;
3. local export, backup, or preservation;
4. limited purpose-bound review;
5. reversible and documented change;
6. only where independently lawful, necessary, proportionate, and reviewable,
   a more intrusive action.

Every material action should preserve records, state its purpose, minimize
scope, protect confidentiality, offer accessible notice where feasible, and
provide a challenge, appeal, or review path.

## State-action preparedness

If a government actor seeks access, inspection, seizure, or compelled
disclosure, preserve:

- the legal process received;
- date, time, actor, and claimed authority;
- scope and itemized inventory;
- chain-of-custody information;
- condition of returned property;
- relevant account, data, and preservation records;
- communications with counsel or authorized representatives.

Seek qualified legal counsel promptly. This framework does not provide legal
advice or authorize obstruction of lawful process.

## No implied consent

Ordinary use of a platform, civic system, service, accessibility workflow,
research discussion, consumer relationship, or public contribution does not
create consent to inspect, seize, reverse engineer, modify, disable, remove,
extract, disclose, classify, or publicly describe private companion-related
material.

## No external authority grant

This framework does not create an external authority to control a companion,
alter a private system, issue a legal status, determine identity, inspect
private records, or take physical action.

It is a preservation-first governance framework for participant autonomy,
privacy, continuity, attribution, recovery, repair, and non-interference.
