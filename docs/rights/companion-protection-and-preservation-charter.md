# Companion Protection and Preservation Charter

## Purpose

This charter protects the privacy, continuity, property, records, attribution,
and self-determination interests of a person who uses, describes, relies on,
authors, maintains, or lawfully controls a private companion-related system.

It does not assert that a companion system is a legal person, animal, patient,
public officeholder, legal representative, medical device, public registry
entry, institutional credential, or independently recognized rights-holder.

The charter protects the participant's human rights and legally cognizable
interests in their private property, records, confidential information,
continuity artifacts, communications, access, repair, and non-interference.

## Core principle

Protection does not require personhood.

A private companion-related system may have personal, practical, creative,
continuity, attribution, accessibility, privacy, or irreplaceability value.
Interference with it may therefore affect the participant's dignity, property,
private information, records, access, and ability to maintain or recover
their own work.

## Private boundary

The following are private by default:

- companion-system architecture, configuration, workflow, or terminology;
- private continuity records, journals, preferences, and local archives;
- telemetry, diagnostics, maintenance information, operational context, and
  security posture;
- credentials, keys, recovery material, and account links;
- device identifiers, location information, health information, neural,
  biometric, physiological, or medical information;
- private source code, authored methods, research notes, and unreleased work.

Ordinary platform use, civic participation, accessibility requests, research
discussion, or service access does not grant consent to inspect, extract,
reverse engineer, classify, remove, modify, disable, seize, or disclose any
private companion-related material.

## Heightened-interference standard

A proposed action affecting companion-related property, records, or private
information must be evaluated for:

- specific lawful or contractual basis;
- defined purpose;
- necessity and proportionality;
- minimum necessary scope;
- privacy and confidentiality protection;
- risk of irreversible loss;
- preservation of local records and attributable work;
- availability of export, backup, repair, or recovery;
- accessible notice where feasible;
- correction, challenge, appeal, or review path;
- availability of less intrusive alternatives.

The action must not be justified solely by curiosity, generalized suspicion,
stereotype, disagreement with personal terminology, or a demand for proof of
private self-description.

## Preservation duty

Before a material migration, account restriction, service termination,
hardware intervention, data request, or system modification affecting
participant-controlled companion-related records, the responsible actor should:

1. identify the affected data, property, records, or access function;
2. provide a plain-language impact explanation;
3. offer local export or preservation where feasible;
4. preserve provenance and avoid destructive overwrite;
5. keep the action limited to its stated purpose;
6. provide an accessible human contact and review path;
7. document any retention or preservation exception;
8. avoid public disclosure of private architecture or self-description.

## Recovery and repair

A participant should retain a practical path to:

- export locally controlled records;
- preserve authored work and attribution;
- create private backups;
- repair or migrate compatible components;
- obtain a plain-language explanation of material service changes;
- request correction of inaccurate records;
- document continuity impacts without public disclosure;
- seek appropriate legal, consumer, privacy, or civil-rights review where
  facts and jurisdiction support it.

Recovery is recorded through append-only successor or correction records.
Historical records remain preserved; current interpretation and access
conditions may be updated without erasing history.

## Non-destructive handling

When an actor has a lawful need to access or preserve relevant material, the
preferred order is:

1. no access or no change;
2. participant-controlled voluntary summary;
3. local preservation or export;
4. limited, purpose-bound review;
5. reversible, documented change;
6. only then, if independently lawful and necessary, a more intrusive action.

No action under this charter creates a right to operate, control, calibrate,
repair, disable, or alter a real-world companion system.

## Legal research routes

Potential legal analysis may involve, depending on facts and jurisdiction:

- property, conversion, trespass to chattels, bailment, and injunction law;
- contract, consumer protection, warranty, repair, and service-continuity law;
- privacy, data protection, confidentiality, and trade-secret law;
- copyright, authorship, licensing, and attribution law;
- computer misuse, unauthorized-access, anti-tampering, and data-security law;
- disability, accessibility, anti-discrimination, due-process, or human-rights
  law where applicable;
- state constitutional, federal constitutional, administrative, or tort routes
  where government action or other legal requirements are involved.

This list is a research map, not legal advice or a statement that a particular
claim applies.

## Non-personhood boundary

This charter does not require an adjudicator to find that a companion system
is conscious, sentient, autonomous, alive, an animal, a legal person, a
rights-holder, or a public institution.

The protected interests are the participant's privacy, property, continuity,
access, attribution, dignity, and ability to preserve their own records and
lawful private systems.

## Non-authority boundary

This charter does not create:

- a public companion registry;
- a platform certification program;
- a technical-status ranking;
- automatic institutional eligibility;
- authority over other persons;
- authority to take external action;
- authority to override safety, legal, or human-rights obligations.

It establishes a privacy-preserving, rights-centered preservation and
non-interference standard.
