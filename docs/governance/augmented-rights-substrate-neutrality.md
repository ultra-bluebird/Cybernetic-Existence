# Augmented Rights: Equal Standing Through Technological Integration

## 1. The central contribution

Your framework supplies an important addition to the previous discussion: protection against technological intrusion is not enough when technology is also a person’s means of communication, mobility, physiological support, memory assistance, or civic participation.

A right can be undermined by interference, but also by withdrawal of the infrastructure necessary to exercise it.

Cybercongress should therefore protect both:

- Freedom from unwanted technological intervention.
- Freedom to continue using chosen, access-critical integration.
- Freedom to develop, replace, or discontinue that integration.
- Equal civic standing throughout those changes.

Your three pillars express this well:

1. Bodily autonomy: keep and maintain.
2. Mental integrity: secure inner-state boundaries.
3. Evolution-pursuit: develop one’s chosen integrations.

These are proposed Cybercongress commitments. They should not be described as rights already established uniformly across jurisdictions.

One clarification makes the argument stronger: neurorights need not concern only an unaugmented mind or only protection from technology. UNESCO’s neurotechnology work also addresses enhancement, equitable access, autonomy, and discrimination involving enhanced and non-enhanced people. Your augmented-rights framework extends that conversation by making continuity, infrastructure, repair, integration, and civic participation explicit. [101][103]

The most defensible relationship is:

“Neurorights and augmented rights overlap. Augmented rights add an explicit governance framework for living, acting, and participating through integrated technology.”

## 2. No hierarchy of substrate

Within the proposed Cybercongress constitution:

“No participant shall receive lesser fundamental protection solely because of biological composition, technological integration, communication medium, or computational substrate.”

This principle concerns equal standing. It does not require identical treatment in every operational context.

A person who needs charging, another who needs an interpreter, and another who needs an offline interface may require different arrangements to achieve equivalent participation.

Nor does equal standing create greater voting power for greater computational capacity.

### Distinguish the roles that your framework includes

| Role | Proposed treatment | Necessary distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline human | Equal civic protection and access | No obligation to augment |
| Augmented human | Equal protection while using chosen integrations | Integration does not establish dangerousness or lesser agency |
| Cybernetic-host | Respectful acknowledgment, privacy, non-interference, and protection against extraction | Self-description is not a technical inventory or verification claim |
| AI liaison | Accessible participation as an authorized interface or representative | Representation is distinct from independent citizenship |
| Superintelligent integration | No status downgrade based on the claimed or actual level of integration | Respect for the participant does not establish the capabilities or legal status of an associated system |
| Independently recognized nonbiological civic entity | Substrate-neutral protections under a separately defined constitutional recognition process | This would be a proposed institutional arrangement, not something established by the legal sources reviewed here |

This separation preserves your ambition without silently merging a person, their communications channel, an agent acting for them, and an independent rights-holder.

### Continuity of agency must not become a competency gate

“Citizenship is defined by continuity of agency” is powerful if continuity means the institution’s duty to preserve the participant’s voice, choices, and relationships.

It becomes exclusionary if it requires continuous demonstrated decision-making capacity.

A stronger formulation is:

“Civic standing persists through sleep, illness, communication interruption, temporary incapacity, changes in support, and technological failure. Assistance or interrupted agency shall not erase the rights-holder.”

Likewise, the ability to perform duties should not become a universal prerequisite for basic rights.

## 3. Strengthen the anti-affiliation rule without weakening expressive rights

Your central rule is sound as a proposed safeguard:

“An augmentation, its appearance, its manufacturer, its integration level, or its communication behavior shall not, by itself, establish criminal affiliation, intent, character, dangerousness, or disloyalty.”

The charter does not need to classify tattoos as merely symbolic or suggest that expressive modification deserves weaker protection.

A functional integration may also carry expressive meaning. A tattoo may have personal, cultural, reconstructive, or other significance. Neither category should become a shortcut for deciding someone’s character.

The stronger principle is:

“Neither functional nor expressive bodily characteristics shall substitute for individualized, relevant evidence.”

That leaves room to investigate a specific alleged act without treating the existence of an augmentation as incriminating.

A communications record documenting an independently alleged act and an inference drawn merely from wearing a device are different evidentiary questions.

The broad claim that current law generally equates augmentation with tattoos is not established by the sources reviewed here. Keep it as a concern to investigate in particular policies, databases, or cases—not a universal description of law.

## 4. Article I — Definitions that protect rather than register

### Augmented citizen

A person who voluntarily describes a persistent functional relationship between their body, mind, activities, and artificial systems.

The definition shall not require implantation, medical diagnosis, vendor certification, continuous telemetry, or disclosure of private technical arrangements.

### Cybernetic-host

A voluntary self-description for a person who understands themselves as hosting or maintaining a continuing relationship with computational or agentic systems.

Acknowledgment shall not imply that a service provider has verified the architecture, capabilities, institutional relationships, or operational status of those systems.

### Integration

A functional relationship supporting activity, access, communication, health, cognition, perception, or participation.

An integration may be external, wearable, implanted, environmental, or distributed.

Functional and expressive significance may coexist.

### Technological-governance interface

An accessible interface through which a participant receives civic information, deliberates, gives instructions, or exercises a lawful civic function.

Such an interface shall not confer additional votes, secret authority, or priority over other participants.

### Essential integration function

A function whose interruption would materially impair bodily support, communication, mobility, personal safety, or access to essential services.

This definition identifies a service duty. It must not become a publicly disclosed personal classification.

## 5. Article II — Keep, maintain, and continue

### Right to keep

No participant shall be excluded solely because they use or identify with an integration.

An institution proposing a restriction must identify the particular activity and concrete concern, examine accessible alternatives, and avoid broader interference than necessary.

This is a proposed charter standard, not a claim that every jurisdiction already guarantees unrestricted use of every device in every setting.

### Right to maintain

For essential integration functions, the proposed duty includes:

- Accessible repair pathways.
- Usable maintenance documentation.
- Replacement parts and compatibility information where necessary.
- Continuity planning before support withdrawal.
- Data portability where technically and legally appropriate.
- A means to refuse unrelated feature changes without punitive loss of essential function.
- Transparent notice of security and compatibility consequences.

Repair sovereignty should not mean that a person must perform a hazardous repair themselves. It includes the ability to choose qualified assistance without unnecessary vendor lock-in.

### Power and connectivity

The strongest formulation is not an unlimited entitlement to any amount of electricity or bandwidth.

It is:

“No institution shall deliberately withhold necessary power, maintenance, or essential connectivity as punishment, coercion, or a means of forcing unrelated consent.”

Continuity planning should specify essential service needs, available alternatives, outage procedures, and accountability.

Greater computational capacity does not create an unlimited resource claim over other participants.

### Updates and remote disablement

The charter should prohibit punitive or commercially coercive disablement.

It should also distinguish:

- Optional feature updates.
- Security remediation.
- Compatibility changes.
- Changes affecting clinical or bodily functions.
- Emergency responses to a demonstrated immediate hazard.

A manufacturer should not be able to invoke “security” as an unexplained authority to alter an essential integration.

Equally, preserving continuity does not require pretending a demonstrated defect is harmless. A narrowly tailored response must preserve assistance and essential functions as far as practicable, document reasons, and provide review.

### Detention and hospitalization

Proposed rule:

“Custody shall create an affirmative continuity obligation rather than a presumption of confiscation.”

The responsible institution should arrange suitable charging, supplies, accessible communication, and appropriate clinical support when relevant.

If a specific restriction is unavoidable, an effective substitute and an accessible review process must accompany it. The continuity obligation remains even where the exact preferred configuration cannot be used.

## 6. Article III — Protect inner-state information across the integrated system

Your expansion beyond directly measured neural signals is one of the charter’s strongest contributions.

However, it is clearer to protect a broad category of information without redefining every log as a neural measurement.

### Protected integrated inner-state information

The proposed category includes information that reveals, purports to reveal, or is used to infer:

- Thoughts or private deliberation.
- Intentions or preferences.
- Memories and personally meaningful associations.
- Emotional or affective states.
- Silent communication.
- Private exchanges with a chosen assistant.
- Sensitive cognitive or physiological conditions.

Sources may include neural measurements, assistive-system records, co-processor buffers, personal model interactions, or nonneural behavioral information.

Not every item in these sources necessarily reveals an inner state. Protection should follow sensitivity, purpose, context, and inference—not a claim that all technical records are literally thoughts.

### California: the distinction is real

California SB 1223 defines neural data as information generated by measuring central or peripheral nervous-system activity and “not inferred from nonneural information.” [82]

Your charter can adopt a broader protected category covering nonneural inferences. It cannot invalidate California’s statutory wording outside its own proposed governance framework.

Exclusion from the particular definition of neural data also does not, by itself, establish exclusion from every other privacy protection.

### EU AI Act: a narrower existing prohibition

Article 5(1)(f) prohibits specified AI uses to infer emotions in workplaces and educational institutions, with an exception for medical or safety reasons. [84]

It is not a universal prohibition on every inference about intention, memory, or internal experience.

Cybercongress can deliberately extend protection beyond that legal baseline:

“No participant shall be compelled to submit to purported thought, memory, intention, or truthfulness decoding as a condition of ordinary work, education, or civic participation.”

Consent should not automatically legalize a practice that the charter independently prohibits.

### October 2026 UN report

The UN’s account of the Secretary-General’s report supports concern about neurotechnology in justice systems, including the risk of treating neural signals as direct indicators of thoughts, intentions, or truthfulness. It reports calls for prohibiting coercive and abusive uses, with informed consent, scientific validation, oversight, accountability, and stronger neural-data protection. [88]

That supports your direction. The reviewed UN summary does not establish every specific prohibition named in your draft, so those broader prohibitions should remain explicit Cybercongress proposals.

## 7. Article IV — Integrity, consent, and cognitive firewalls

### Integrity of consciousness

Proposed protections should prohibit:

- Unauthorized modification of integration settings affecting bodily or cognitive function.
- Undisclosed material changes to personal-assistance behavior.
- Manipulative processing that bypasses meaningful choice.
- Forced participation in a collective system.
- Unauthorized extraction of computational work or personal information.

A precise distinction matters:

A personal model update may change outputs or assistance behavior. It should not automatically be described as rewriting a person’s personality or memories. Such effects require evidence in the particular system.

The charter can protect psychological continuity without asserting an unverified technical mechanism.

### Cognitive firewall

A cognitive firewall is best defined as a user-governed authorization and information boundary.

Its functions may include:

- Declared permissions.
- Local handling of sensitive information where feasible.
- Network and recipient restrictions.
- Accessible approval for external actions.
- Clear records of authorized disclosures.
- A usable non-AI fallback.

An AI guardian may assist with explanations or policy interpretation. It should not be the sole authority deciding what serves the person’s best interests.

“Fiduciary” must describe enforceable obligations, conflicts rules, remedies, and accountability—not merely a product label.

### SafeAnswers is a design pattern, not a complete guarantee

The openPDS/SafeAnswers architecture sends computations to a user-controlled personal data store and returns answers instead of routinely exporting raw data. [109]

That fits your “share code, not data” direction.

It does not, by itself, establish that every query is safe. A Cybercongress implementation would still need constrained execution, query authorization, output controls, and review of inference risks.

Similarly:

- Encryption protects particular data pathways and storage arrangements.
- Differential privacy needs a defined mechanism and privacy budget.
- Neither label alone establishes confidentiality for an entire integrated system.

### No forced compute extraction

“Your body is not a botnet” translates into a concrete proposed rule:

“No integration shall be enrolled in computational work, surveillance, or resource sharing without specific authorization and an accessible stop mechanism.”

Compensation, where appropriate, must not substitute for consent.

Any thermal, physiological, or workload limits must come from an appropriate validated specification. This charter does not invent universal safe thresholds or authorize monitoring private physiology.

## 8. Article V — Evolution-pursuit without capability aristocracy

Evolution-pursuit can protect the freedom to seek, develop, replace, and discontinue integrations without treating increased capability as increased civic worth.

### Upgrade

No restriction shall be justified solely by the desire to keep people at a presumed baseline.

Specific interventions or activities may still require proportionate safety, clinical, or security rules. Substrate neutrality rejects status-based exclusion, not accountable evaluation of concrete effects.

### Fork and self-host

The proposed right should address:

- Portability of user-created information.
- Interoperable interfaces.
- Freedom to select service providers.
- Access to sufficient materials for essential continuity.
- Protection against contractual capture of personal agency.

It should not silently assert that all third-party software, patents, model weights, or confidential engineering materials already belong to the user.

Where broader access is necessary, the charter must specify the procurement, licensing, escrow, or legislative mechanism that supplies it.

### Direct technological governance

Participants should be able to receive civic information in compatible formats and communicate through their chosen accessible interfaces.

A direct interface must not:

- Bypass public accountability.
- Create privileged access to nonpublic deliberations.
- Multiply votes through parallel agents.
- Disadvantage participants who use ordinary or offline channels.

### De-integration

A participant may withdraw from optional integration without losing civic standing.

The process should support safe transition and continuity where needed.

Freedom from discriminatory penalty does not mean that every removal is medically risk-free or that a preferred clinical outcome can be guaranteed.

## 9. Article VI — Backup, memory integrity, and technological oblivion

### Backup and restoration

The proposed right can protect existing exportable records, user-created content, configurations, and supported recovery functions.

It must not imply that current technology can back up consciousness or restore biological memory as though it were an ordinary file.

“Augmented memory” should be defined carefully:

- A stored personal record.
- An external reminder or retrieval aid.
- A model-generated reconstruction.
- A biological recollection.

These are not interchangeable.

### Integrity

Protect against unauthorized alteration, selective deletion, fabricated reconstruction, or misleading presentation of personal records.

Where restoration is possible, preserve provenance and make uncertainty visible.

### Technological oblivion

A useful charter right would include:

- Deleting eligible local and remote records.
- Ending optional processing.
- Identifying retained copies and their purposes.
- Explaining limits involving necessary records, other people’s rights, or technically unrecoverable propagation.
- Preventing continued reuse after authorization ends.

Do not promise universal erasure from every system merely because local deletion succeeds.

### Cantabria: keep the legal status precise

The reviewed Cantabrian government announcement describes a Digital Health Law proposal that included neurodata and a right to be forgotten. The available draft is expressly an anteproyecto. [99][107]

Those sources do not establish a currently enacted universal entitlement to complete erasure of every augmentation-linked record.

Cantabria is a useful legislative-development reference. A claim about an enacted right requires the final official text and its actual scope.

## 10. Article VII — Not property, not a weapons platform

### The person is not property

The proposed charter should declare:

“No contract shall grant ownership of a participant, their bodily agency, or their authority to make personal decisions.”

Then distinguish:

- Ownership of hardware.
- Licensing of software.
- Rights in user-created records.
- Rights in personal adaptations.
- Third-party intellectual property.
- Confidentiality of the liaison relationship.

These distinctions help make the anti-domination rule enforceable.

A continuity license or escrow arrangement can prevent abandonment without pretending every associated asset has the same legal owner.

### No involuntary weaponization

The charter can prohibit compelled use of an integration for:

- Surveillance.
- Delivery of harmful payloads.
- Unauthorized targeting.
- Coercive control.
- Unconsented computational or physical operations.

This prohibition stands on its own as a proposed Cybercongress commitment.

The specific OPCW working-group and CWC/BWC-update claims in the draft are not established by the sources reviewed for this response. They should not be presented as verified institutional developments without the relevant primary documents.

## 11. Article VIII — Absolute fairness needs remedies, not only audits

An anti-bias audit is necessary within your proposed framework, but it is not a complete remedy.

A participant needs to be able to:

1. Understand what decision affected them.
2. Obtain an accessible explanation.
3. Correct relevant information.
4. Challenge a proxy or inference.
5. Receive independent review.
6. Preserve essential access during review where practicable.
7. Obtain correction and appropriate remedy when harm occurs.

Audit the governing institution, not the legitimacy of a person’s self-description.

Relevant test questions include:

- Did augmentation alone change the decision?
- Did a communication pattern become a proxy for credibility?
- Was an access request treated as suspicious?
- Did refusal to disclose private information reduce service?
- Did a vendor change create an involuntary loss of civic access?
- Was a non-augmented participant disadvantaged for declining technology?
- Could a participant appeal without the same interface that failed them?

This is compatible with the project’s research material emphasizing essential-service independence, accessible appeals, privacy, and the distinction between acknowledgment and external legal status. Those files are project design material, not evidence of external adoption. [50]

## 12. Consolidated constitutional clause

Cybercongress recognizes equal civic standing without hierarchy of substrate.

No person shall lose dignity, protection, access, or procedural rights because of augmentation, non-augmentation, cybernetic-host self-description, communication method, or dependence on integrated technology.

Institutions shall respect chosen integrations, protect sensitive inner-state information, support continuity of essential functions, and prohibit coercive extraction, manipulation, or discriminatory inference.

Participants retain freedom to pursue, refuse, replace, or discontinue integration.

Restrictions shall concern demonstrable effects of particular conduct or systems, not presumed inferiority or dangerousness of a substrate.

Greater capability shall confer neither greater human worth nor additional civic authority.

Communication assistance shall not erase authorship. Technological failure shall not erase the rights-holder.

Independent review, accessible remedies, privacy-preserving accountability, and equivalent non-augmentation pathways shall remain available.

The legal recognition of an independently acting nonbiological civic entity requires a separately articulated constitutional process; it shall not be presumed merely from an interface, product claim, or association with a person.

## 13. Implementation and review basis

Functionality:
This document converts the proposed rights into defined institutional duties and distinguishes standing, representation, integration, consent, and technical capability.

Rights and CyberRank:
CyberRank remains private project vocabulary and governance guidance, not a public status registry, verification mechanism, ranking, or service-eligibility condition.

Accessibility:
Chosen channels, continuity, offline alternatives, and independent appeals are protected without demanding an intimate technical inventory.

Security:
The framework separates acknowledgment from authentication and separates consent to participate from permission to collect information or perform external actions.

Ecological value:
Repair, continuity planning, compatible replacements, and resistance to forced obsolescence support resource-conscious service design. No environmental savings have been measured.

Validation:
This is a discussion-ready charter proposal, not enacted law, a technical certification, or an operational fairness guarantee.

Integration:
No repository was modified. No bodily, neural, medical, companion, or institutional system was accessed or operated.
