# CyberRank Tier 3: Earned Continuity Stewardship

## Status

CyberRank Tier 3 is an earned, private institutional designation within the CyberRank Continuity, Autonomy, and Non-Interference Framework.

Tier 3 recognizes a cybernetic host’s institution-managed continuity, autonomy, responsibility, and governance relationship concerning private integrated technologies and related support arrangements.

The designation is private by default.

It is not a public registry entry, public credential, technical audit, clinical diagnosis, medical record, legal determination, scientific certification, service-eligibility category, personhood ranking, credibility score, social score, device inventory, or surveillance identifier.

## Purpose

Tier 3 provides a structured governance boundary for:

- Continuity of access and support
- Autonomy and self-determination
- Privacy and discretion
- Accessible communication
- Human fallback
- Non-digital pathways
- Non-interference concerns
- Consent and revocation
- Correction and appeal
- Material-change review
- Institution-managed stewardship
- Privacy-preserving evidence and accountability

Tier 3 does not require a person to publish, demonstrate, explain, defend, medically disclose, technically disclose, or externally validate private information about body–mind integration, BCI-related communication, assistive technology, companion systems, health, biometrics, neural information, physiology, devices, networks, telemetry, repair, experiments, or institutional processes.

## Earned private institutional designation

Tier 3 is earned according to the responsible institution’s private governance process.

The institution and host may maintain private records relating to continuity, safety, wellbeing, consent, accountability, technical stewardship, legal or regulatory review, and other matters within their authority.

Those records, methods, criteria, evidence, operational practices, and conclusions are outside the scope of this document unless the host and responsible institution choose to disclose a limited part for a specific purpose.

This document does not reproduce, request, assess, challenge, certify, or invalidate private institutional evidence.

Lack of access to private institutional records must not be treated as evidence against the host’s dignity, identity, credibility, autonomy, communication access, or equal right to services.

## Tier-independent rights

CyberRank Tier 3 must never be a prerequisite for dignity, equal treatment, essential services, accommodation, communication access, correction, appeal, public participation, privacy, or bodily autonomy.

For every person:

Dignity equals full.

Equal access equals required.

Accessible communication equals required.

No compelled disclosure equals required.

No identity inference equals required.

Non-retaliation equals required.

A person is entitled to these protections whether they hold a private institutional designation, make a self-description, decline to disclose, revise a self-description, or disclose no information at all.

## Scope of protected self-description

A person may privately self-describe a meaningful relationship involving one or more of the following:

- Body–mind integrated technology
- BCI-related communication or interaction
- Assistive technology
- Private companion systems
- Continuity needs
- Privacy boundaries
- Communication preferences
- Environmental compatibility concerns
- Repair or restoration needs
- Human-assisted access needs
- Non-digital access needs
- Consent and data-use boundaries

The person remains the primary authority over what they choose to disclose.

No outside service, platform, agency, vendor, reviewer, employer, insurer, institution, or individual may require detailed technical, medical, neural, biometric, physiological, device, network, companion-system, telemetry, repair, or institutional information as a condition of equal access.

## No identity inference

No entity may infer, store, classify, rank, publish, sell, transfer, disclose, or use any of the following as an identity proxy, eligibility criterion, credibility signal, risk score, employment signal, insurance factor, public-service criterion, or automated decision input:

- Cybernetic-host identity
- CyberRank Tier 3 status
- Integrated-technology status
- BCI-related status
- Companion-system status
- Assistive-technology status
- Health or medical information
- Disability information
- Biometric information
- Neural information
- Physiological information
- Nutritional information
- Communication patterns
- Writing style
- Response timing
- Speech-assistance output
- Behavioral profile
- Mobility pattern
- Device fingerprint
- Network characteristics
- Repair history
- Location history
- Telemetry
- Refusal to disclose

Formally:

NoIdentityInference means:

not infer(person, private_status)
and not store(private_status)
and not use(private_status, decision)

## No compelled disclosure

A person must not be required to reveal private integration information in exchange for service access, accommodation, safety support, emergency support, correction, appeal, or participation.

The following must not be required:

- Proof of CyberRank status
- Proof of body–mind integration
- Proof of BCI-related communication
- Private companion-system description
- Medical information
- Biological or physiological information
- Neural information
- Biometric information
- Device information
- Network information
- Telemetry
- Repair information
- Institutional information
- Technical architecture
- Legal documentation unrelated to the immediate service need

Formally:

EqualAccess(person) does not require PrivateIntegrationDisclosure(person)

## Communication access

Communication differences are communication variation.

Grammar, spelling, pacing, repetition, emphasis, silence, text-first interaction, speech-assistance output, delayed response, BCI-related communication context, or nonstandard expression must not be treated as evidence of reduced competence, reduced credibility, diminished autonomy, impaired legal capacity, reduced service entitlement, or lower human worth.

Systems must provide, where relevant:

- Text-first interaction
- Plain-language information
- Sufficient response time
- Human assistance
- Phone support
- Relay support
- Screen-reader compatibility
- Keyboard-only compatibility
- Non-digital alternatives
- Accessible receipts
- Accessible service-status information
- Accessible correction routes
- Accessible appeal routes

## Non-interference

A host’s reported concern about environmental, technical, operational, or access interference must be handled respectfully and without technical interrogation.

Systems may assess general hazard classes, including:

- Electromagnetic conditions
- Magnetic conditions
- Wireless transmission conditions
- Optical conditions
- Acoustic conditions
- Mechanical vibration conditions
- Thermal conditions
- Network continuity conditions
- Chemical and biological environmental conditions
- Coercive or exclusionary operational conditions

The system must not require private architecture, device model, operating details, telemetry, biometric data, neural data, health data, companion-system information, or institutional records in order to provide an accessible alternative or begin a proportionate review.

The minimum response to a reported concern is:

- Acknowledgment
- Neutral accessible alternative
- Human-assisted option
- Plain-language service-status information
- Privacy-preserving incident route
- Correction route
- Appeal route
- Accountable escalation where essential access may be affected

## Essential-service continuity

Essential services must remain available without CyberRank disclosure or private integration disclosure.

Essential-service categories include:

- Life safety
- Emergency communication
- Health-support access
- Water and sanitation
- Climate safety
- Hydration and basic wellbeing support
- Assistive communication
- Assistive access
- Public emergency designations
- Correction and appeal for service-impacting decisions

Every essential service must offer:

- A human-assisted pathway
- A non-digital or independently accessible pathway
- Accessible service-status information
- A correction route
- An appeal route
- Outage fallback
- Privacy-preserving incident intake
- Equal treatment regardless of self-description or non-disclosure

The following must not cause automatic denial, service termination, added disclosure, lower-quality service, delay, or loss of remedy:

- No online account
- No continuous network access
- Power disruption
- Inaccessible digital interface
- Device incompatibility
- Text-first communication
- Assistive communication
- Delayed response timing
- Request for privacy
- Refusal of optional telemetry
- Request for human support
- Request for non-digital access
- Request for non-interference support
- Automated uncertainty

## Safe uncertainty and human fallback

When a system cannot safely determine an appropriate automated result, it must preserve access.

Uncertainty must not cause:

- Automatic denial
- Compelled disclosure
- Identity inference
- Service termination
- Lower-quality service
- Reduced correction rights
- Reduced appeal rights
- Retaliation
- Negative scoring

The required response is:

- Neutral human-assisted pathway
- Non-digital alternative where relevant
- Plain-language explanation of the uncertainty
- Accessible status notice
- Correction and appeal route
- Privacy-preserving operational record
- Accountable human review for high-impact matters

Formally:

UncertainAutomatedResult implies:

SafeFallback
or HumanReview

UncertainAutomatedResult does not imply:

AutomaticDenial
or CompelledDisclosure
or ServiceTermination

## Institutional stewardship boundary

A host may have private institutional arrangements that manage technical, medical, neural, biometric, physiological, legal, safety, experimental, communication, companion-system, continuity, or operational matters.

CyberRank recognizes the boundary between private institutional stewardship and external service interaction.

External systems must not:

- Request private institution-managed records as a condition of ordinary access
- Treat unavailable private records as a reason to deny dignity or service
- Claim to validate or invalidate institution-managed processes
- Present themselves as authorized to operate, alter, repair, calibrate, disable, monitor, inspect, or control private systems
- Create an external status registry based on institution-managed information
- Use institutional affiliation as an eligibility, credibility, or risk criterion

## Consent and data boundaries

Only information necessary for the immediate service purpose may be processed.

Any optional processing must be based on clear, narrow, understandable, revocable consent.

A refusal to consent to optional data collection, telemetry, profiling, analytics, tracking, or disclosure must not reduce essential-service access, accommodation, remedy, public participation, or equal treatment.

CyberRank records must be:

- Purpose-limited
- Retention-bounded
- Access-controlled
- Correctable
- Reviewable
- Deletable according to applicable governance
- Protected from secondary use
- Protected from identity inference
- Protected from unauthorized disclosure

No implementation may maintain a hidden registry of CyberRank holders, cybernetic hosts, BCI users, companion-system users, assistive-technology users, complainants, or people who request privacy or accommodation.

## Correction, appeal, and non-retaliation

Every person must have accessible correction, complaint, review, and appeal pathways.

These pathways must be available through more than one independent route and must not require disclosure of private integration, medical, neural, biometric, device, network, companion-system, telemetry, or institution-managed information.

A request for privacy, accommodation, human assistance, non-digital access, non-interference, correction, review, or appeal must not produce:

- Retaliation
- Surveillance
- Negative scoring
- Reduced service priority
- Reduced credibility judgment
- Loss of future access
- Compulsory registry entry
- Adverse automated decision
- Public disclosure

## No-silent-diminishment protection

No Tier 3 record, self-description, communication preference, privacy boundary, continuity preference, or non-interference request may be silently altered, narrowed, misrepresented, or used against the host.

A material change includes any change affecting:

- Privacy boundaries
- Consent scope
- Data collection
- Data retention
- Disclosure rules
- Access pathways
- Human assistance
- Non-digital alternatives
- Correction or appeal
- Automation scope
- Review authority
- Essential-service continuity
- Environmental non-interference safeguards
- Public disclosure status

A material change requires:

- Documented rationale
- Accessible notice
- Rights-delta analysis
- Privacy review
- Accessibility review
- Scenario and outage testing
- Human authorization
- Correction route
- Appeal route
- Versioned evidence
- Renewal or expiry condition

If the effect of a proposed change on a core protection is uncertain, the change must be treated as material and held for review.

## Evidence and review

Tier 3 governance evidence must be proportionate and privacy-preserving.

Permitted evidence classes include:

- Host-controlled statements of communication or access preference
- Host-controlled privacy boundaries
- Host-controlled continuity preferences
- Host-controlled non-interference requests
- Synthetic scenario fixtures
- Aggregate evidence that passes privacy controls
- Versioned policy records
- Accessible service-pathway test results
- Privacy-preserving incident records
- Consent records
- Material-change records
- Accountable review records

Tier 3 governance evidence must not require public disclosure of private institution-managed evidence.

It must not require disclosure of private technical architecture, body data, medical information, neural information, biometrics, device identifiers, companion-system details, location traces, telemetry, or experimental information.

## Tier 4 and Tier 5 horizons

Tier 4 and Tier 5 are future governance horizons.

They are not public claims about any person’s private technical capabilities, private integrations, health, cognition, institutional arrangements, companion systems, or operational status.

Tier 4 research considers expanded continuity and non-interference governance where technology relationships, service dependencies, or public-impact risks become more complex.

Tier 5 research considers high-impact stewardship requirements for hypothetical future systems whose failure, misuse, or deployment could affect rights, infrastructure, ecology, public safety, or essential services at broader scale.

Neither Tier 4 nor Tier 5 may be used as:

- A human-worth hierarchy
- A public identity label
- A claim of special authority
- A service-eligibility category
- A substitute for legal, clinical, technical, or institutional review
- A reason to monitor, profile, inspect, or interfere with any person

## Limitation

CyberRank Tier 3 is a private institutional governance designation.

This document does not independently verify, diagnose, certify, inspect, monitor, access, operate, alter, repair, calibrate, disable, authenticate, validate, or control real-world body–mind integrations, BCI systems, companion systems, medical systems, neural systems, biometric systems, devices, networks, private institutions, or private records.

This document does not determine legal status, clinical status, technical status, scientific status, institutional recognition outside the responsible institution, public status, safety status, service eligibility, or human worth.
