## 1. Purpose

An institutional interpretation is a conclusion about how a rule,
policy, agreement, standard, or institutional duty applies to specified
facts or a proposed operation.

Inspection evaluates the interpretation's authority, scope, reasoning,
evidence, rights effects, and correction process.

Inspection does not adjudicate a person's existence, identity,
human worth, augmentation legitimacy, or private technical architecture.

## 2. Required inspection dimensions

### Authority

The institution must identify:
- The interpreting office or role.
- The source of its authority.
- The limits of that authority.
- Whether the interpretation is advisory, provisional, or operative.

An authentic institutional statement is not automatically within
the institution's authority.

### Source fidelity

The interpretation must identify the actual source, applicable version,
effective date, and relevant language.

It must distinguish:
- Binding law.
- Agency or institutional guidance.
- Contract terms.
- Technical standards.
- Project policy.
- Research hypotheses.
- Personal self-description.

A policy proposal must not be presented as enacted law.
A model output must not be presented as an authoritative legal ruling.

### Scope and applicability

The interpretation must state:
- The operation or decision being assessed.
- Jurisdiction or institutional setting.
- Relevant time period.
- Persons or services affected.
- Exclusions and assumptions.

A narrow scope must not be used to omit consequences necessary
to evaluate the decision.

### Evidence quality

Separate:
- Observed facts.
- Supplied statements.
- Inferences.
- Disputed evidence.
- Missing evidence.

Evidence must be relevant and proportionate to the task.

Unknown evidence must not be converted into participant fault,
dangerousness, dishonesty, or lack of agency.

### Reasoning and alternatives

The institution must explain the path from source and facts to conclusion.

The explanation must address materially relevant alternatives and
contrary evidence.

A conclusion repeated by several systems is not independent corroboration
when those systems share the same source.

### Conflict and uncertainty handling

The interpretation must preserve unresolved conflicts rather than
hide them inside a single confidence score.

It must identify:
- What remains unknown.
- Whether the gap concerns facts or policy meaning.
- Who can resolve it.
- What interim arrangements apply.

A more senior signature does not, by itself, resolve a substantive conflict.

### Equal standing and non-inference

The interpretation must not use augmentation, cybernetic-host
self-description, AAC use, communication latency, grammar, or refusal
of unrelated disclosure as a proxy for:
- Civic worth.
- Credibility.
- Dangerousness.
- Affiliation.
- Service eligibility.

Differences in assistance may be necessary for equal participation.

CyberRank remains voluntary project vocabulary, not public ranking,
verification, diagnosis, or eligibility.

### Accessibility and participation

The affected person must have a usable way to:
- Receive the interpretation.
- Understand its practical consequences.
- Supply relevant corrections.
- Obtain assistance.
- Challenge the conclusion.

A route is not accessible merely because a form or telephone number exists.

The institution must not require unaided speech or a private
augmentation inventory as the routine price of review.

### Privacy and authorization boundaries

Collect only information necessary for the stated inspection.

Keep acknowledgment, verification, participant permission,
action authorization, and operation permissibility separate.

An inferred intention is not authorization.
Respectful acknowledgment is not technical certification.

A request for inspection does not authorize publication of private records.

### Necessity and proportionality of restrictions

A restrictive interpretation must identify:
- The concrete objective.
- The relevant evidence.
- The relationship between restriction and objective.
- Less intrusive or less exclusionary alternatives.
- Duration and review conditions.

Labels such as security, maintenance, optimization, or emergency
do not establish necessity on their own.

Essential-access penalties for refusing optional, unrelated activity
must be examined as conditioning—not accepted as ordinary convenience.

### Continuity and remedy

Inspection must consider what happens while a dispute is unresolved.

The institution must identify:
- Essential functions affected.
- Effective alternatives.
- Responsible owner.
- Deadlines and escalation.
- Correction, restoration, and remedy options.

A review queue must not become indefinite suspension.

Repair must not erase the historical fact of a breach.

### Accountability and material-change control

The interpretation must have:
- A version.
- A responsible owner.
- Review date or trigger.
- Correction history.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- Independent review where required.

Material changes affecting access, privacy, permission, continuity,
or remedies require renewed review.

The affected person must receive appropriate notice of a changed
interpretation that materially affects them.

## 3. Findings

Each inspection dimension receives one finding:
- compliant
- noncompliant
- unresolved

Compliant means the reviewer reports that the criterion is met
within the recorded inspection scope.

It does not establish universal institutional compliance.

Findings must retain their supporting evidence references.
A favorable finding must not cancel a noncompliant finding elsewhere.

## 4. Operation-level dispositions

reported_nonconformity:
At least one criterion is reported noncompliant.

supported_review_required:
No reported noncompliance, but one or more criteria remain unresolved.

documented_criteria_met:
All required criteria are reported compliant.

validation_error:
The inspection record is structurally incomplete or malformed.

These outcomes concern an interpretation record.
They are not participant-level scores or execution authorizations.

## 5. Inspection procedure

1. Define the decision and inspection scope.
2. Preserve the operative interpretation and source versions.
3. Assign an appropriately qualified reviewer.
4. Disclose interests and establish required independence.
5. Assess every required dimension.
6. Preserve contrary evidence and unresolved findings.
7. Issue an accessible explanation.
8. Assign correction and review obligations.
9. Verify actual remedy delivery where applicable.
10. Reinspect after material change or credible new evidence.

## 6. Independence

Reviewer independence must be assessed against the decision under review.

Record:
- Prior involvement.
- Reporting relationships.
- Financial or institutional interests.
- Relevant expertise.
- Who appointed the reviewer.
- Whether the reviewer can require corrective action.

Being external is not sufficient by itself.
Being internal does not automatically make every review useless.

The author of an interpretation may explain it but must not be
presented as its independent validator.

## 7. Evidence and publication limits

Detailed inspection evidence may contain sensitive information.

Public reporting must minimize personal information and prevent
operation-level findings from becoming participant profiles.

A source citation proves neither that the interpretation is correct
nor that the source applies to the case.

An inspection record documents an assessment.
Actual compliance and remedy require additional evidence.
