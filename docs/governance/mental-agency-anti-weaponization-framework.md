# Mental Agency and Anti-Weaponization: A Cybercongress Enforcement Framework

## 1. The central finding

Your strongest contribution is identifying that definitional gaps become enforcement gaps.

A system can undermine mental privacy without measuring a neuron. An institution can undermine autonomy without causing a visible injury. A vendor can impair an augmented person’s participation by withdrawing essential functionality rather than directly intervening in their body.

A Cybercongress framework should therefore protect three connected interests:

1. Mental agency: the person’s authority over private deliberation, expression, and consequential choices.
2. Integrated continuity: reliable access to chosen technologies supporting bodily function, communication, cognition, and civic participation.
3. Freedom from instrumentalization: protection against coercive extraction, surveillance, manipulation, and involuntary operational use.

These are proposed governance commitments. Their enforcement requires defined duties, prohibited practices, evidence rules, oversight, and remedies—not only recognition of abstract rights.

Your research provides a strong foundation. Several legal and technical distinctions should be tightened before it becomes a legislative or engineering specification.

## 2. Source-grounded clarifications that strengthen the argument

### A. “Zero laws protect behavioral mental-state inferences” is too broad

Exclusion from a statutory definition of neural data does not establish exemption from all privacy, employment, discrimination, or data-protection law.

The GDPR regulates personal-data profiling and gives special protection to specified sensitive categories, including health information. European case law recognizes that information capable of revealing health status through collation or deduction can qualify as health data. This does not make every emotional inference specially protected, but it defeats the universal claim that inferred mental information has no existing legal protection. [136][131]

A more accurate conclusion is:

“Protection for mental-state inference is fragmented, purpose-dependent, and incomplete. Direct neural measurements sometimes receive an explicit classification that comparable behavioral inferences lack.”

That identifies the real gap without erasing existing remedies.

### B. California’s definitional boundary is real, but it is not the whole legal landscape

SB 1223 expressly excludes information inferred from nonneural information from its definition of neural data. [82]

However, California AB 1883 separately addresses AI-enabled workplace surveillance that recognizes, infers, or predicts emotional states. The official text shows approval on September 30, 2026, and includes safety language and narrowly described federally connected operational exemptions. It should therefore not be summarized as an exception-free ban. [119]

The useful distinction is:

- Neural-data classification concerns what information falls within a defined category.
- A prohibited-use rule can regulate emotional inference regardless of whether its inputs satisfy that category.

Cybercongress should use both approaches.

### C. Colorado requires close attention to the enacted wording

Colorado HB24-1058 places neural data within biological data, whose definition contains identification-purpose language. Its separate neural-data definition covers measurements of central or peripheral nervous-system activity processed with device assistance. [120][122]

This supports concern about how the definitions interact. It does not justify treating every non-identification use as definitively exempt without considering authoritative interpretation and other applicable provisions.

### D. Federal proposal is not federal enactment

The official record for S.2925 identifies the MIND Act as introduced September 29, 2025, and referred to the Senate Commerce Committee. Its stated purpose includes directing an FTC study of neural-data governance. The reviewed record does not show enactment. [112][113]

Use it as evidence of a legislative proposal, not an existing nationwide protection.

### E. UNESCO provides more precise coverage than a neural-only model

The final UNESCO Recommendation explicitly addresses indirect neural data and nonneural data allowing mental-state inferences, including typing dynamics, voice analysis, eye tracking, and other sources. It also addresses the whole lifecycle, including maintenance, termination, disposal, and recycling. [126]

Its consent and data-protection provisions include purpose limitation, minimization, access, correction, erasure, and suspension of processing. The text recognizes a life-threatening medical-emergency exception and situates its recommendations within applicable international frameworks. [126]

The official final text records adoption on November 11, 2025. That is the date to use when citing the instrument itself, rather than the November 12 date repeated in some summaries. It remains a recommendation, not a self-executing global statute. [126]

### F. The chemical-weapons gap is not a complete absence of prohibitions

The OPCW’s December 1, 2021 decision states that aerosolized CNS-acting chemicals are inconsistent with law enforcement as a purpose not prohibited under the Chemical Weapons Convention. [128]

But the Convention already prohibits development, production, acquisition, stockpiling, retention, transfer, and use of chemical weapons. A narrow decision about aerosolized law-enforcement use must not be read as permission for weapon development through other routes. [143]

The stronger governance argument is:

“Clarify disputed applications, strengthen implementation, and prevent relabeling prohibited weapon purposes as legitimate activity.”

That is more accurate than saying the relevant lifecycle prohibitions are simply missing.

### G. Product roadmaps are not demonstrations of superintelligence

Synchron’s March 19, 2025 announcement described a roadmap for Chiral and future training on neural data. It also described a BCI demonstration involving environmental control. The announcement’s future-oriented language does not establish unrestricted thought reading, superintelligence, or general access to private beliefs. [154]

The threat model should distinguish:

- Demonstrated functions.
- Company claims and development plans.
- Scientifically plausible future risks.
- Speculative scenarios.

Strong rights protections do not require overstating current capabilities.

## 3. Replace a single “neural data” category with a layered protection model

The proposed framework should distinguish four categories.

| Category | Definition | Principal safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Neural measurements | Recorded observations of nervous-system structure, activity, or function | Strict collection, security, purpose, and access controls |
| Mental-state inferences | Claims or estimates about thoughts, feelings, intentions, preferences, or cognitive conditions, regardless of input source | Protection against unauthorized inference and consequential misuse |
| Integrated private records | Private assistant exchanges, external memory records, relevant buffers, and personal configurations | Confidentiality, contextual use limits, integrity, and continuity |
| Consequential decisions | Actions affecting liberty, employment, education, healthcare, housing, benefits, or civic access | Reasons, contestability, evidence standards, and independent review |

This prevents two errors:

- Treating every neural measurement as a literal thought.
- Treating a behavioral inference as harmless because no neural sensor was used.

### Proposed definition: protected mental information

“Protected mental information means personal information that reveals, purports to reveal, or is processed to infer a person’s private mental states, deliberation, intentions, memories, beliefs, affective conditions, or cognitive characteristics.”

“Purports to reveal” is important. A false inference can still lead to exclusion, coercion, or reputational injury.

Protection must not depend on the inference being scientifically correct.

### Group privacy and persistent inference

The framework should also prohibit:

- Inferring sensitive characteristics about a community and applying them to individual members.
- Reconstructing a deleted profile from retained derivatives for a prohibited purpose.
- Repackaging a restricted inference as a neutral score.
- Using communication style or assistance dependence as a proxy for credibility, loyalty, or dangerousness.

Deleting a source record and stopping future inference are separate obligations. Both need explicit rules.

## 4. Define rights through enforceable tests

The following are proposed Cybercongress tests, not statements of a uniform existing legal standard.

### Mental privacy

Protected interest:
Control over access to and inference of private mental information.

Violation test:
An actor collects, derives, obtains, discloses, or uses protected mental information outside an authorized and legally permitted purpose.

Physical injury is not a prerequisite.

### Cognitive liberty

Protected interest:
Freedom to choose whether and how to use technologies affecting mental experience, subject to others’ equal rights.

Violation test:
An actor materially interferes with that choice through coercion, deception, unauthorized intervention, or a penalty that makes refusal practically unavailable.

Persuasion is not automatically manipulation.

Relevant factors include:

- Whether the source and purpose are disclosed.
- Whether the person can meaningfully refuse.
- Whether vulnerabilities are deliberately exploited.
- Whether the intervention bypasses or undermines reflective choice.
- Whether essential access is conditioned on acceptance.

### Mental integrity

Protected interest:
Freedom from unauthorized intervention in mental functioning.

Violation test:
An actor causes or attempts an unauthorized intervention that materially affects cognition, affect, perception, memory, or volitional control.

The framework should provide preventive relief before irreversible injury. It should not require visible tissue damage.

### Psychological continuity

Protected interest:
The person’s authority over materially identity-relevant changes to their integrated support arrangements.

Violation test:
A third party imposes an undisclosed or unauthorized material change affecting private records, assistance behavior, communication, or supported decision-making in ways relevant to the person’s continuing agency.

Not every software update or change in mood constitutes identity alteration.

Review factors include:

- Consent and disclosure.
- Magnitude and duration.
- Reversibility.
- Effects on communication and choice.
- The person’s own account of the change.
- Availability of supported transition or recovery.

### Equitable access

Protected interest:
Effective access to beneficial technology and equivalent participation without forced augmentation.

Separate the duties:

1. Do not discriminate.
2. Provide necessary accessible pathways.
3. Adopt transparent resource-allocation rules.
4. Address affordability and continuity.
5. Preserve the freedom not to augment.

Equal standing does not imply an unlimited public entitlement to every enhancement.

UNESCO’s final text supports equitable access while prioritizing beneficial medical and assistive applications and underserved populations. [126]

## 5. Anti-weaponization should follow purpose, effect, and coercion

Cybercongress should not classify an entire technology family as a weapon merely because it can be misused.

That could undermine access to therapeutic and assistive systems.

Instead, evaluate:

- Intended purpose.
- Actual and reasonably foreseeable effects.
- Target population.
- Degree of coercion.
- Ability to refuse or stop.
- Institutional power imbalance.
- Applicable legal prohibitions.

### Proposed prohibited purposes

No actor may use an integration or protected mental information to:

- Torture or impose cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment.
- Compel ideological or behavioral conformity.
- Extract purported truth, intention, or belief through prohibited coercion.
- Enroll a person’s integration in unauthorized surveillance or operational work.
- Manipulate civic choices through undisclosed interference.
- Punish a person by deliberately disabling essential functions.
- Force computational labor or resource sharing.
- Convert augmentation status into presumed criminality.

These prohibitions do not depend on calling a product a weapon.

### Protect continuity during incident response

A generic “kill switch” can itself become a coercive control.

For access-critical or bodily-support systems, distinguish:

- Stopping optional collection.
- Blocking unauthorized transmission.
- Pausing a nonessential feature.
- Disabling an essential bodily or communication function.

The proposed default is to stop unauthorized processing while preserving essential assistance wherever technically feasible.

This document does not authorize changes to any real-world device.

## 6. Judicial firewalls: some practices remain prohibited even with a warrant

A warrant, consent, and scientific validity answer different questions.

- A warrant concerns legal authority for a specified intrusion.
- Consent concerns the person’s authorization.
- Scientific validity concerns whether the proposed interpretation is reliable.
- An absolute prohibition determines that a practice must not occur at all.

None substitutes for the others.

### Proposed judicial rules

1. A warrant shall not authorize a practice independently prohibited as torture, coercive thought extraction, or another absolute violation.
2. Consent shall not be inferred from detention, silence, physiological activity, or predicted intention.
3. Refusal shall not itself establish guilt or dangerousness.
4. A signal shall not be treated as a direct, self-authenticating statement of thought, truthfulness, or intent.
5. Any permitted evidence must satisfy applicable relevance, reliability, disclosure, and challenge requirements.
6. Independent legal advice and accessible communication must be available where participation is requested.
7. Illegally obtained information must trigger the exclusion and remedy rules expressly adopted by the jurisdiction.

The specific details of an exclusionary rule require jurisdictional drafting. They should not be presented as already identical worldwide.

Most importantly:

“Predicted intent is not authorization.”

A preparatory signal, model confidence score, or inferred preference cannot authorize collection, disclosure, intervention, or an external action.

## 7. Fiduciary engineering: replace a utility slogan with reviewable obligations

The proposed condition “user utility minus third-party utility is nonnegative” does not prove loyalty.

It leaves major questions unanswered:

- Who defines utility?
- How is it measured?
- Are values comparable?
- Can a privacy violation be offset by convenience?
- What happens when a model predicts preferences incorrectly?

A stronger architecture combines non-offsettable constraints with accountable optimization.

### Non-offsettable constraints

A system shall not:

- Perform an unauthorized external action.
- Disclose information to an unauthorized recipient.
- Treat inferred mental content as consent.
- Accept third-party steering that conflicts with declared duties.
- Sacrifice essential access to gain commercial advantage.
- Convert a privacy refusal into an adverse eligibility score.

An aggregate benefit cannot compensate for violating one of these boundaries.

### Differential privacy needs a complete specification

The standard approximate differential-privacy condition is:

Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ exp(ε) Pr[M(D′) ∈ S] + δ

It applies to neighboring datasets and a randomized mechanism, not simply to arbitrary input-output pairs. The definition of neighboring datasets, privacy parameters, release process, and composition across releases all matter. [145]

For longitudinal neural information, a design must state whether it protects:

- One measurement.
- One session.
- One person’s full contribution.
- Another explicitly bounded unit.

Protecting one sample is not automatically equivalent to protecting an entire person’s history.

Differential privacy also does not establish consent, clinical safety, loyalty, or freedom from manipulation.

### SafeAnswers and local processing

SafeAnswers supports returning computed answers rather than routinely exporting raw data. [109]

A production system still needs:

- Restricted execution.
- Authorized queries.
- Output minimization.
- Controls against repeated-query reconstruction.
- Accessible explanations and refusal.
- Independent review of sensitive releases.

“Only answers leave” is an architectural goal, not proof that no sensitive information leaves.

## 8. Proposed enforcement article

### Article — Protected Mental Agency and Integrated Continuity

1. Equal standing shall not depend on augmentation, non-augmentation, communication medium, or claimed integration level.

2. Protected mental information shall receive safeguards based on its sensitivity and use, regardless of whether its source is neural, behavioral, conversational, or computational.

3. No inferred intention, preparatory signal, model output, or private assistant exchange shall constitute authorization for a consequential action.

4. Coercive thought extraction, involuntary behavioral conformity, unauthorized intervention, and nonconsensual operational extraction are prohibited.

5. Essential integration functions shall not be deliberately withdrawn to punish refusal of unrelated collection, disclosure, labor, or participation.

6. Consent shall be specific, accessible, informed, purpose-bound, and withdrawable for future optional processing. Continuing protection shall not depend on continuing consent.

7. Material changes to access-critical systems shall receive notice, continuity planning, and accessible review.

8. Institutions shall audit their own decisions for augmentation-based discrimination without creating public augmentation registries.

9. Participants shall receive understandable reasons, correction opportunities, independent review, and effective remedies.

10. Public transparency shall concern institutional practices and recipient categories. It shall not expose a participant’s private relationships, integration details, or sensitive recipient history.

11. CyberRank shall remain voluntary project vocabulary and governance guidance, not a diagnosis, verification status, civic ranking, or eligibility mechanism.

12. Beneficial research, assistive access, repair, and chosen integration shall not be prohibited merely because related technologies have dual-use potential.
