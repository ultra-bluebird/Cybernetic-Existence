Person-directed continuity, accountable evidence, and accessible correction.

Document version: 1.0.0  
Status: Proposed project specification  
License: MIT OR Apache-2.0

## Document scope

World-Save is a project governance and engineering vocabulary for recording
reviewed milestones and protecting accepted progress against unauthorized
erasure, silent downgrade, or rollback.

This document specifies requirements for records, review, communication,
continuity, and supporting software.

It does not establish:

- Binding law.
- A public personal registry.
- Identity verification.
- Clinical validation.
- Medical-device authorization.
- Institutional status.
- Physical irreversibility.
- Deployment approval.
- Consent to future processing or operations.
- Authority to control a real-world system.

A specification, software test, reviewed record, accepted milestone, and
authorized operation are different things.

## Purpose

World-Save supports a narrow commitment:

> Preserve the meaning and continuity of an accepted milestone while keeping
> evidence limitations, correction, future choice, and operational authority
> explicit.

Protection of accepted progress must not become a restriction on the
person's future choices.

A person-directed change is not automatically a continuity violation.

Correcting an erroneous record is not the same as undoing real-world
progress. Administrative closure is not proof that a practical remedy
succeeded.

## Normative language

MUST and MUST NOT identify requirements of this proposed project profile.

SHOULD identifies a recommended practice whose omission requires an
explanation.

These terms describe project requirements. They do not convert this document
into law, certification, or operational permission.

## Governing principles

### Dignity and ordinary access

A missing, disputed, unreadable, incomplete, or unavailable record MUST NOT
by itself remove ordinary explanation, communication support, correction,
appeal, or progress-checking access.

A person MUST NOT be required to:

- Prove a voluntary cybernetic-host self-description.
- Disclose an augmentation inventory.
- Provide raw neural information.
- Disclose private companion-system information.
- Supply medical proof merely to obtain ordinary accessibility support.
- Accept a disputed statement before requesting correction.

Separate authorization may still be required for consequential operations.
Ordinary support and consequential authorization MUST remain distinct.

### Person-directed continuity

Accepted progress MUST NOT be silently erased, downgraded, or reinterpreted.

Future changes MUST receive their own scope and authorization review.

Historical acceptance MUST NOT be treated as unlimited future permission.

A continuity commitment MUST NOT imply that a physical state is irreversible.

### Privacy and minimal disclosure

Records MUST contain only information necessary for their declared purpose.

Acknowledgment, respect, and accessible service MUST NOT depend on unnecessary
physiological, technical, neural, biometric, medical, or institutional
disclosure.

### Non-interference

World-Save records MUST NOT function as commands to:

- Operate a medical or neural system.
- Change device parameters.
- Disable a system.
- Alter a private companion system.
- Initiate surveillance.
- Deny ordinary support.
- Transfer private evidence automatically.

Any consequential external action requires a separate, scoped authorization
process.

### Equal treatment

Communication variation, assistive-input behavior, fatigue, repetition,
pacing, or nonstandard grammar MUST NOT be treated as evidence of reduced
competence, credibility, autonomy, or entitlement to ordinary access.

Task results describe the reviewed communication or service. They MUST NOT
be converted into a person-level standing or legitimacy score.

## CyberRank boundary

CyberRank has two separated project layers.

### Personal layer

The personal layer concerns voluntary self-description, communication
preferences, privacy boundaries, continuity needs, and non-interference
concerns.

It MUST NOT require proof, registration, diagnosis, or public disclosure.

### Governance layer

The governance layer concerns system duties:

- Equal access.
- Minimal disclosure.
- Accessible communication.
- Human fallback.
- Correction.
- Appeal.
- Material-change review.

CyberRank MUST NOT be encoded as:

- Public status.
- Eligibility.
- Verification.
- Diagnosis.
- Ranking.
- Automated decision criteria.

Respecting a self-description does not establish authentication, technical
assessment, clinical validation, or institutional status.

## Core concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Milestone | A bounded description of progress proposed for review |
| Protected baseline | The accepted meaning and conditions that must not be silently downgraded |
| Evidence record | Material offered in support of a scoped proposition |
| Acceptance | An explicit reviewed disposition concerning a defined milestone |
| Continuity commitment | A declared responsibility to preserve specified support or accepted progress |
| Correction | A reasoned update distinguishing record error from real-world change |
| Operational authorization | Separate permission for a defined consequential action |
| Ordinary access | Explanation, available-record inspection, correction, appeal, communication support, and progress checking |

These concepts MUST NOT be collapsed into one Boolean field.

## Roles

A deployment must assign responsibilities before claiming operational
readiness.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Person or supported representative | Express choices, request explanations, and raise corrections through accessible routes |
| Record holder | Maintain the reviewed record and its disposition |
| Requirement reviewer | Assess applicability, evidence, limitations, and current support |
| Accessibility reviewer | Review communication and task journeys |
| Privacy reviewer | Review purpose, disclosure, retention, transfer, and deletion |
| Release authorizer | Make a separate release decision within an established scope |
| Support role | Provide working human-supported and offline routes |
| Escrow or successor-maintenance role | Perform only the separately agreed continuity responsibilities |

A role label is not proof that authority, staffing, competence, or funding
exists.

A person MUST NOT be required to disclose private system details merely to
discover which role is responsible.

## Milestone establishment

The default state is not established.

A milestone may be considered for acceptance only when:

- Its meaning is stated in plain language.
- The review scope is defined.
- The applicable requirement inventory is nonempty.
- Applicability has been reviewed.
- Required compliance, safety, consent, and usage reviews are addressed.
- Evidence and limitations are available to the accountable reviewer.
- Every applicable mandatory requirement passes.
- Acceptance is explicit.
- The protected baseline is recorded.
- Correction and support routes are working.
- Retention and disclosure handling are reviewed.

An acceptance label alone is not proof of authority.

### Review dispositions

| Disposition | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Pass | Current scoped evidence supports the requirement under the reviewed conditions |
| Fail | Scoped evidence identifies a requirement that is not satisfied |
| Unknown | Evidence, applicability, freshness, or interpretation is unresolved |

Unknown MUST NOT be converted into pass.

A majority of passes MUST NOT compensate for a failed mandatory requirement.

An empty inventory MUST NOT establish acceptance.

A requirement MUST NOT be silently removed or relabeled as inapplicable to
make a review pass.

## Separate record dimensions

The following dimensions MUST remain distinguishable:

- Milestone acceptance.
- Evidence availability.
- Record dispute or correction.
- Current support availability.
- Operational authorization.

For example:

- An accepted milestone can have unavailable source evidence.
- An accepted historical milestone can have a current support gap.
- A disputed record can still support ordinary explanation and correction.
- A corrected record can preserve the person's actual progress.
- A complete evidence review can still lack operational authorization.

Implementations MUST NOT infer one dimension solely from another.

These are conceptual requirements, not permission to add unsupported fields
to an existing strict manifest.

A reviewed record should retain the smallest necessary account of:

- Milestone meaning.
- Review scope.
- Applicable requirement inventory.
- Evidence basis and limitations.
- Acceptance disposition and basis.
- Protected baseline.
- Continuity responsibilities.
- Definition of unauthorized rollback.
- Evidence availability.
- Correction route.
- Current support limitations.
- Retention and deletion disposition.

Records SHOULD use accountable roles rather than unnecessary personal
details.

Raw neural data, an augmentation inventory, and private companion-system
information are not default record requirements.

## Comprehension evidence

A scoped comprehension instrument may use:

- Meaning.
- Derived output.
- Inference limits.
- Consent interpretation.
- Correction route.

A consent-interpretation result is not consent.

### Response partition

For each task, preserve:

- Assigned.
- Correct.
- Incorrect.
- Explicit uncertainty.
- Missing.

Require:

    assigned = correct + incorrect + explicit_uncertainty + missing

Define:

    scorable = correct + incorrect

    correct_rate = correct / scorable
        when scorable > 0

    scorable_coverage = scorable / assigned
        when assigned > 0

When a denominator is zero, the rate MUST be null with an explicit undefined
status.

Do not substitute zero or perfect comprehension.

### Rubric-first coding

A statement of uncertainty can be correct under the rubric.

For example, recognizing that a notice does not establish someone's health
status MUST NOT automatically be coded as an unscorable response.

### Non-compensation

Task results MUST remain individually visible.

A high consent-interpretation rate MUST NOT conceal poor inference-limit
understanding.

A composite score MUST NOT be introduced without a separately reviewed
policy revision.

### Differential tests

Reporting software SHOULD test that:

- Changing inference counts does not change consent results.
- Changing consent counts does not change inference results.
- Changing one task does not rewrite another task's result.
- Undefined states remain distinct from zero rates.
- Counts and denominators remain visible.
- Input records are not mutated.

These are software properties, not instrument-validity findings.

## Accessible material-change notices

Material input changes require accessible release notes.

A note MUST explain:

- Product versions and affected contexts.
- The affected action.
- Previous and new behavior.
- Timing, holds, combinations, focus, and cancellation changes.
- Whether settings are preserved, migrated, reset, or unsupported.
- Steps the person can take.
- Recovery limitations.
- Human-supported and offline help.
- Relevant processing changes.
- Unresolved compatibility.

The note MUST be usable before the person depends on the new behavior.

It SHOULD be operable through the previous supported input route and
available as persistent plain text.

Reading or acknowledging a note MUST NOT be treated as consent to new
processing.

Structural note validation does not establish comprehension or successful
recovery.

## Preference portability

Preference exchange MUST use a documented, versioned, local format.

The supported inventory and exclusions MUST be explicit.

Default exclusions include:

- Raw neural signals.
- Medical records.
- Physiological thresholds.
- Decoder calibration.
- Operational credentials.
- Device inventory.
- Private companion-system information.
- Unnecessary account or profile-owner details.

Preferences can still reveal support needs. A preference format is not
anonymous merely because medical fields are absent.

### Conversion requirements

A converter MUST:

- Use a version-bound mapping.
- Preserve supported values.
- Preserve explicit false values.
- Preserve disabled-feature configuration where the format requires it.
- Reject unsupported versions.
- Report unsupported fields rather than silently discard them.
- Reject lossy unit conversion unless a separately reviewed transformation
  is explicitly chosen.
- Prepare an import preview before changing working settings.

Mapping semantics MUST be captured at codec construction or supplied as an
explicit immutable profile.

Reassigning a mutable global mapping MUST NOT silently change an existing
codec's behavior.

A successful round trip does not establish actual rendering quality,
comprehension, or real-vendor interoperability.

## Semantic action boundary

Application-level actions may include:

- Navigate.
- Select.
- Cancel.
- Pause request.
- Resume request.

These actions concern the application interface only.

They do not authorize commands to neural, medical, companion, or
institutional systems.

Unsupported actions MUST produce an explicit bounded result rather than
pretend that the requested state was achieved.

For example, a pause request in an unsupported mode must not be presented as
successful pausing.

### Emulation and semantic integration

Input emulation and semantic integration can produce equivalent behavior.

An architecture recommendation must specify what actually changes:

- Prompt consistency.
- Focus handling.
- Timing flexibility.
- Cancellation.
- Required simultaneous inputs.
- Recovery.
- Direct action selection.

If these features change together, the study evaluates the support package,
not the API name alone.

## Comparative barrier research

A comparison between emulation and semantic support should use:

- A prespecified intervention.
- Matched tasks.
- Randomized or counterbalanced order.
- Comparable training.
- Chosen communication and input support.
- Rest and stopping options.
- Prespecified barrier definitions.
- Explicit missing-outcome handling.
- Analysis of learning and period effects.

For one independent primary pair per participant:

    Y_E = barrier outcome under emulation
    Y_S = barrier outcome under semantic support

where each observed outcome is zero or one.

The paired effect is:

    mean(Y_S - Y_E)

Negative values favor semantic support within the reviewed study.

Repeated tasks require dependence-aware analysis.

Unknown outcomes MUST NOT automatically become failures or successes.

Missing-outcome bounds are not confidence intervals.

Synthetic examples MUST NOT be presented as participant-study findings.
