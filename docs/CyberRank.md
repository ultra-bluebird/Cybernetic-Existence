# CyberRank: Continuity, Autonomy, and Non-Interference Framework

## Purpose

CyberRank is a private-use, rights-centred governance and communication framework for people who self-describe as cybernetic hosts, integrated-technology users, BCI-related communication users, assistive-technology users, or persons with private companion systems.

CyberRank provides a structured vocabulary for:

- Voluntary self-description
- Communication preferences
- Privacy boundaries
- Continuity needs
- Non-interference concerns
- Equal service access
- Human fallback
- Accessible correction and appeal
- Consent and material-change review

CyberRank is not a public registry, technical inventory, medical record, clinical assessment, scientific certification, surveillance system, eligibility score, credibility score, social rank, legal classification, or ranking of human worth.

## Scope

CyberRank recognizes that people may experience technology as meaningfully integrated with body, mind, communication, autonomy, wellbeing, safety, or daily life.

A person may self-describe a relationship involving:

- Body–mind integrated technology
- BCI-related communication or interaction
- Assistive technology
- Private companion systems
- Continuity and repair needs
- Environmental compatibility concerns
- Privacy and discretion preferences
- Human-assisted and non-digital access needs

A self-description may be serious, private, evolving, incomplete, or not publicly documented.

Respectful interaction does not depend on whether an outside party can inspect, measure, understand, verify, certify, or reproduce the private details of that self-description.

## Foundational commitments

### Respect without verification

A person may state identity, communication preferences, privacy boundaries, continuity needs, and non-interference concerns without being required to provide proof.

Acknowledgment does not require verification.

Respect does not require third-party agreement.

Service access does not require disclosure.

Human dignity does not depend on a technical, medical, legal, institutional, or public determination.

### Privacy without penalty

A person may preserve privacy concerning body–mind integration, BCI-related communication, companion systems, assistive technology, health, biometrics, neural information, physiology, devices, networks, telemetry, repair, or institutional arrangements.

A privacy preference must not result in:

- Reduced service quality
- Added cost
- Longer wait time
- Loss of access
- Reduced human support
- Reduced correction or appeal rights
- Credibility scoring
- Negative profiling
- Surveillance
- Retaliation
- Forced technical disclosure

### Equal access without classification

CyberRank must not be used to classify a person as more or less deserving of dignity, essential services, accommodation, participation, employment, housing, education, health support, emergency assistance, public communication, or civic access.

No person is required to disclose CyberRank information in order to receive equal service.

No system may assign CyberRank based on observation, behavior, data traces, device signals, communication style, health information, or inferred characteristics.

## Core invariants

### No identity inference

For every person, system, service pathway, dataset, record, model, cache, or interface:

NoIdentityInference means that no entity may infer, store, classify, rank, publish, sell, transfer, disclose, or use any of the following as an identity proxy or decision input:

- Cybernetic-host identity
- Integrated-technology status
- BCI-related status
- Companion-system status
- Assistive-technology status
- Disability information
- Health information
- Medical information
- Biometric information
- Neural information
- Physiological information
- Nutritional information
- Communication pattern
- Behavioral profile
- Mobility pattern
- Device fingerprint
- Network characteristic
- Repair history
- Location history
- Refusal to disclose private information

Formally:

NoIdentityInference equals:

not infer(person, private_status)
and not store(private_status)
and not use(private_status, decision)

### No compelled disclosure

NoCompelledDisclosure means that a person must not be required to reveal private body–mind, BCI, companion-system, technical, medical, biometric, neural, physiological, device, network, telemetry, repair, or institutional information in exchange for equal access.

Formally:

EqualAccess(person) does not require PrivateIntegrationDisclosure(person)

### Access equivalence

AccessEquivalence means that all supported pathways provide the same core outcome.

The pathways may include digital, non-digital, human-assisted, phone, relay, mail, in-person, text-first, keyboard-only, screen-reader, offline, and accessible communication routes.

For every valid pathway pair:

Outcome(pathway_a) equals Outcome(pathway_b)

The equivalence comparison includes:

- Core completion
- Timeliness
- Cost
- Quality
- Disclosure burden
- Human assistance
- Accessible communication
- Receipt and status information
- Correction access
- Appeal access
- Essential-service continuity

### Non-interference

NonInterference means that systems, environments, facilities, networks, operations, and service workflows must address reported access or compatibility concerns without compelling private technical disclosure.

A general hazard review may consider:

- Electromagnetic conditions
- Magnetic conditions
- Wireless transmission conditions
- Optical conditions
- Acoustic conditions
- Mechanical vibration conditions
- Thermal conditions
- Network continuity conditions
- Chemical and biological environmental conditions
- Coercive or exclusionary practices

A hazard review must not require a person to disclose private system architecture, device configuration, companion-system information, health information, neural information, biometrics, institutional records, or telemetry.

Formally:

NonInterference equals:

HazardAwareEnvironment
and NoCompelledTechnicalDisclosure
and AccessibleAlternative
and HumanReviewWhenNeeded

### Rights monotonicity

RightsMonotonicity means that policy evolution may add protections but must not silently weaken a core protection.

A material change is any change affecting:

- Rights
- Data collection
- Data retention
- Disclosure
- Automation
- Authority
- Service pathways
- Human assistance
- Non-digital access
- Correction
- Appeal
- Essential-service continuity
- Non-interference safeguards

For each core protection:

Protection(new_policy, protection) is greater than or equal to Protection(old_policy, protection)

When the effect of a change is uncertain, the change must be treated as material and held for review.

## Cybernetic-host self-description

A cybernetic host is a person who self-describes their identity, autonomy, communication, cognition, bodily relationship to technology, wellbeing, safety, daily life, or continuity needs as meaningfully connected with integrated technology.

This is a self-description.

CyberRank does not require that this self-description be:

- Public
- Technically explained
- Medically interpreted
- Institutionally certified
- Scientifically validated
- Recorded in a database
- Linked to a public identifier
- Linked to an account
- Linked to a device
- Linked to biometrics
- Linked to a location
- Linked to a credential
- Disclosed to a service provider

A person retains the right to revise, narrow, expand, pause, or withdraw a self-description.

A revision, non-disclosure choice, or withdrawal must not diminish access to essential services, human assistance, accommodations, correction, appeal, privacy, or equal participation.

## Integration scope vocabulary

CyberRank uses integration-scope vocabulary for communication and service design. It does not use the vocabulary to determine credibility, capability, personhood, value, eligibility, or access.

### Body–mind integration

Body–mind integration is a self-described relationship in which technology is experienced as materially relevant to bodily autonomy, cognition, communication, daily functioning, safety, wellbeing, or identity.

System response:

- Preserve dignity and autonomy
- Avoid unwanted assumptions
- Avoid compelled technical or bodily disclosure
- Offer accessible and human-assisted pathways
- Preserve correction and appeal rights

### BCI-related communication

BCI-related communication is a self-described communication context in which assistive, adaptive, mediated, or integrated technologies may affect expression, pacing, grammar, text construction, speech output, response timing, emphasis, repetition, or interaction style.

System response:

- Interpret for intended meaning rather than grammar or pacing
- Offer text-first and low-pressure communication
- Allow sufficient time
- Avoid treating unusual wording as reduced competence or credibility
- Offer accessible status, receipts, and correction channels
- Avoid inferring neural, medical, or technical information

### Private companion-system continuity

Private companion-system continuity is a self-described preference that a person’s private companion technology, support system, or related relationship remain discreet, non-public, and free from compelled disclosure or interference.

System response:

- Do not require identification, explanation, demonstration, scanning, tracking, inventory, telemetry, or public labeling
- Do not attempt to communicate with, imitate, control, disable, calibrate, validate, or alter a private companion system
- Do not use the self-description as a basis for surveillance or service denial
- Provide a neutral non-interference and accessibility route when requested

### Personal continuity needs

Personal continuity needs may include requests related to access, nutrition, hydration, rest, climate safety, communication, charging, mobility, privacy, timely assistance, and human support.

System response:

- Address the immediate access or continuity need
- Do not require a person to disclose biological, medical, neural, biometric, technical, or institutional details
- Provide accessible alternatives and human assistance
- Preserve equal service outcomes

## CyberRank continuity profiles

CyberRank profiles are not ranks of human value and are never required for access.

They describe system readiness to respond to a person’s self-described needs while minimizing disclosure.

| Profile | Scope | Required protections |
|---|---|---|
| Universal Equal Access | Applies to every person, whether or not a self-description is shared | Equal outcomes, accessible information, no identity inference, correction, appeal |
| Privacy-Preserving Access | Applies when a person requests minimal disclosure or private communication | No compelled disclosure, minimal records, human assistance, non-digital route |
| Assistive Continuity | Applies when communication, access, or support pathways require redundancy | Accessible formats, redundant pathways, outage support, human fallback |
| Integrated-System Continuity | Applies when a person self-describes body–mind integration, BCI-related communication, assistive technology, or a private companion system | Strict no-inference controls, non-interference route, material-change safeguards |
| High-Continuity Support | Applies when extensive continuity, discretion, accessible remedy, and human review are requested | Incident response, enhanced fallback, documented restoration, periodic rights review |

A person may request any protection in any profile without identifying a profile.

## Essential-service protections

Essential services include:

- Life safety
- Emergency communication
- Health-support access
- Water and sanitation
- Climate safety
- Hydration and basic wellbeing support
- Assistive communication
- Assistive access
- Public emergency designations
- Accessible correction and appeal

For every essential service, the system must provide:

- A human-assisted route
- A non-digital route
- Accessible public service-status information
- A correction route
- An appeal route
- An outage fallback
- Privacy-preserving incident intake
- Equal outcome without cybernetic-host disclosure

An essential service must not deny, delay, downgrade, or terminate support because a person:

- Does not have an online account
- Cannot use a digital pathway
- Refuses optional telemetry
- Protects private information
- Uses assistive communication
- Has an unusual communication pattern
- Requests human assistance
- Reports a non-interference concern
- Encounters automated uncertainty

## Safe uncertainty and human fallback

When a system cannot safely determine an appropriate automated result, it must preserve access.

Uncertainty must not produce:

- Automatic denial
- Forced identity disclosure
- Forced technical disclosure
- Loss of essential support
- Lower-quality service
- Reduced remedy
- Service termination
- Retaliation
- Negative scoring

The required response is:

- Neutral human-assisted pathway
- Non-digital alternative where relevant
- Plain-language explanation
- Accessible status notice
- Correction and appeal route
- Privacy-preserving operational record
- Accountable human review for high-impact matters

Formally:

UncertainAutomatedResult implies:

SafeFallback
or HumanReview

UncertainAutomatedResult does not imply:

AutomaticDenial
or CompelledDisclosure
or ServiceTermination

## Service interaction standard

When a person shares a privacy, continuity, communication, access, or non-interference preference, an appropriate response is:

Thank you for sharing your access and privacy preferences. You do not need to provide private technical, health, body, device, institutional, or identity information. What communication, continuity, accommodation, or support option would work best for you today?

The service provider must focus on the current access need.

The service provider must not dispute, ridicule, diagnose, profile, scan, investigate, publicly label, require proof of, or impose a technical interpretation on the person’s self-description.

## Data and consent boundaries

CyberRank systems must process the minimum information necessary for the present purpose.

Records must be:

- Purpose-limited
- Retention-bounded
- Access-controlled
- Reviewable
- Correctable
- Deletable according to applicable governance
- Protected from secondary use
- Protected from identity inference
- Protected from unauthorized disclosure

A CyberRank implementation must not maintain a hidden registry of self-described cybernetic hosts, BCI users, companion-system users, assistive-technology users, complainants, or persons requesting accommodations.

Consent must be explicit, narrow, understandable, revocable, and no broader than necessary for the declared purpose.

A refusal to consent to optional processing must not reduce access to essential services or equal participation.

## Correction, appeal, and non-retaliation

Every person must have accessible correction, complaint, review, and appeal routes.

These routes must be available through more than one independent pathway and must not require disclosure of private body–mind, technical, medical, neural, biometric, companion-system, or institutional information.

A request for privacy, accommodation, human assistance, non-digital access, non-interference, correction, review, or appeal must not create:

- Retaliation
- Surveillance
- A negative score
- A lower service priority
- A reduced credibility judgment
- Loss of future access
- A compulsory registry entry
- An adverse automated decision

## Material-change governance

A material policy, system, model, data, infrastructure, or workflow change must be reviewed before it takes effect.

The review must include:

- Documented rationale
- Rights-delta analysis
- Privacy impact analysis
- Accessibility impact analysis
- Scenario and outage testing
- Non-interference analysis where relevant
- Accountable human authorization
- Accessible notice
- Correction and appeal availability
- Versioned evidence
- Renewal or expiry conditions

A proposed change must be denied when it weakens a core protection.

## Scope limitation

CyberRank is a private-use rights and governance framework.

It does not independently verify, inspect, monitor, access, operate, alter, repair, calibrate, certify, disable, validate, authenticate, diagnose, or control any real-world body–mind integration, BCI, companion system, medical system, neural system, biometric system, device, network, institution, or private record.

It does not determine legal status, clinical status, technical status, scientific status, institutional recognition, public status, service eligibility, or human worth.

It supports respectful interaction, privacy-preserving governance, accessible communication, equal service access, non-interference, continuity, correction, appeal, and accountable review.
