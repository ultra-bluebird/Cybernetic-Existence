# Biocompatibility Information Rights and Clinical Escalation

## Purpose

This document defines a rights-centered, privacy-minimal approach to accessing
general biocompatibility information and preparing for appropriate clinical or
manufacturer follow-up concerning body-contacting technologies.

It respects voluntary cybernetic-host and augmented-citizen self-description without
requiring proof, diagnosis, registration, technical inventory, public disclosure, or
identity verification.

This document does not diagnose a medical condition, confirm a device reaction,
certify a material, authorize a procedure, validate an integration claim, grant
CyberRank status, or create a machine-control credential.

## Information-access principle

A person has a legitimate safety and autonomy interest in understandable,
evidence-based information about:

- biological evaluation of body-contacting materials;
- contact type and duration;
- known material and manufacturing information;
- potential exposure pathways;
- manufacturer instructions and warnings;
- device follow-up documentation;
- appropriate clinical escalation;
- urgent symptom recognition; and
- privacy-preserving ways to prepare questions.

Access to general information MUST NOT require disclosure of:

- identity;
- private governance references;
- CyberRank information;
- body–technology topology;
- clinical diagnosis;
- biometric or neural information;
- device credential;
- employer, insurer, or institutional affiliation.

## Biocompatibility boundary

Biocompatibility is a risk-based biological evaluation of a device or material in
its intended use context.

General information about biocompatibility MUST NOT be represented as:

- an individual diagnosis;
- evidence that a particular warning indicates a biological reaction;
- proof that a component is safe;
- permission to alter, repair, calibrate, remove, implant, or operate a device;
- a substitute for manufacturer documentation or qualified clinical review.

## Private evidence record

A person MAY maintain a private evidence record containing only information they
choose to retain.

The record MAY include:

- general standards and regulator guidance references;
- available device or product information;
- material declarations where available;
- body-contact category and duration;
- observable concerns with dates and context;
- manufacturer correspondence;
- questions for a clinician;
- clinician-provided follow-up instructions.

The record MUST NOT be required by a platform as a condition of receiving general
education or ordinary service access.

The record MUST NOT be treated as a public registry, eligibility record, rank,
identity proof, capacity assessment, employment record, or machine credential.

## Platform responsibilities

A platform offering biocompatibility information SHOULD:

- provide plain-language, evidence-based general education;
- distinguish general education from individual medical assessment;
- explain the limits of its information;
- support accessible communication formats;
- encourage appropriate clinical or manufacturer follow-up when individual
  assessment is needed;
- provide urgent-care escalation language for potentially serious symptoms;
- minimize retention and avoid unnecessary identity collection;
- avoid medicalization, stigma, credibility judgments, or proof demands;
- avoid converting a request into a public status, risk score, or governance record.

A platform MUST NOT:

- claim to diagnose, certify, or clear an individual;
- require a private self-description to access general education;
- infer identity, augmentation status, credibility, capacity, or eligibility;
- expose private records to public, employer, insurer, or machine-control use;
- use requests or records as training data without explicit, revocable consent;
- tell a person to ignore serious symptoms or delay urgent care.

## Clinical and manufacturer follow-up

When individual assessment is needed, the appropriate route depends on the device,
body-contact pathway, observed concern, and local care context.

A person MAY choose to contact:

- the clinician or care team responsible for follow-up;
- the device manufacturer or product support channel;
- a pharmacist, primary-care clinician, specialist, or emergency service as
  appropriate to the concern;
- a regulator safety-reporting pathway where applicable.

The person controls what to disclose, subject to the information necessary for the
chosen clinical or safety process.

## Urgent escalation

A person should seek urgent in-person medical assessment or emergency services for
severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, including:

- difficulty breathing;
- swelling involving the face, mouth, tongue, or throat;
- chest pain;
- fainting;
- rapidly spreading rash;
- high fever;
- severe or escalating pain;
- drainage, pronounced warmth, or signs of infection near a body-contacting device;
- sudden neurological symptoms;
- apparent device failure associated with acute symptoms.

## Governance boundary

CyberRank and other private governance vocabularies MAY describe personal
communication preferences, privacy boundaries, continuity needs, and
non-interference concerns.

They MUST NOT be used as:

- medical status;
- biological compatibility certification;
- clinical eligibility;
- identity verification;
- public ranking;
- employment criterion;
- insurance criterion;
- device authorization;
- machine-control credential.

## Review and correction

A person may correct, delete, or stop maintaining their private record.

Platforms and systems MUST offer accessible ways to:

- correct a factual record;
- request deletion where retention is optional;
- obtain a plain-language explanation of any retained information;
- request human support;
- appeal an inappropriate access or disclosure decision.
