# ScrollDepthCompanion and ReverseDiscovery Research Contract

## Primary promises

ScrollDepthCompanion:
Give an adult a private, local-only view of their own visible browsing activity
without sending the data to Hearthline, creating a behavior profile, prompting
engagement, inferring a private condition, or changing discovery results.

ReverseDiscovery:
Let an adult publish a time-bounded, audience-scoped discoverable invitation,
story, activity, room-presence prompt, or interest-led connection prompt on
their own terms without creating a public availability signal, a popularity
score, a service listing, a payment path, or an obligation to reply, meet,
host, travel, disclose, or become intimate.

## ScrollDepthCompanion constraints

- The companion is disabled or session-only by default.
- It is browser-local, network-disabled, user-visible, user-deletable, and
  never transmitted.
- It records only local aggregate action counts selected by the user.
- It stores no profile identity, counterpart identity, query, message content,
  recipient, scroll coordinate, dwell time per person, keystroke behavior,
  pointer behavior, location, device fingerprint, assistive-technology signal,
  private preference, or inferred state.
- It does not issue automatic wellness, recovery, fatigue, mental-health,
  relationship, desirability, or behavioral judgments.
- It does not auto-prompt breaks, limit access, reduce discovery, alter
  recommendations, or influence any platform feature.
- It has keyboard, screen-reader, text resize, high-contrast, reduced-motion,
  and text-first equivalents.
- Local persistence, if added, requires explicit retention choice and no
  network-capable storage adapter.

## ReverseDiscovery constraints

- A reverse-discovery publication is authored, scoped, activated, revised,
  expired, and revoked only by its owner.
- Publication types are limited to story invitation, interest prompt, public
  activity invitation, room presence invitation, conversation prompt,
  friendship-first prompt, and virtual-first prompt.
- Every publication has a visibility scope, contact policy, and expiry.
- Public and local discovery use mutual-interest-required contact by default.
- Publication is an invitation to discover or respond, not a promise of
  availability, consent, meeting, touch, intimacy, hosting, transport, or
  continued contact.
- Publications cannot include money, rates, deposits, payment links, crypto,
  gifts conditioned on intimacy, lodging, housing, transport, labor, work,
  debt relief, drugs, services, or another value-for-intimacy path.
- Publications cannot publish a home address, workplace address, live location,
  private routine, financial need, private health context, recovery context,
  or private identity information.
- Publishing cannot create a desirability, popularity, engagement, response,
  influence, availability, or visibility score.
- Publication response paths are mutual-interest, context-reply, room-only, or
  invitation-request flows; unrestricted public direct messages are excluded.
- Expiry, revision, and revocation remove a publication from discovery,
  ordinary retrieval, caches, and indexes through defined deletion paths.
- No reverse-discovery publication is used as a payment, commercial service,
  housing, travel, or resource-exchange listing.

## Always-stop conditions

- Companion data leaves the device, enters a log, is synchronized, is used for
  analytics, or alters platform behavior.
- Companion data is used to infer fatigue, addiction, recovery, disability,
  health, sexuality, loneliness, financial status, vulnerability, or a
  behavioral score.
- A companion becomes an engagement nudge, productivity tracker, usage cap, or
  platform-defined wellness judgment.
- A reverse-discovery publication reveals or enables inference of home, work,
  exact location, routine, financial need, housing need, health, recovery,
  private identity, or real-time availability.
- A publication allows unrestricted unsolicited direct messages by default.
- A publication creates a popularity, response, desirability, exposure, or
  ranking effect.
- Expired or revoked publications remain in discovery, search, caches, or
  ordinary product retrieval paths.
- Publication content supports payment, conditional gifts, transport,
  housing, lodging, debt, work, drugs, services, or any value-for-intimacy
  arrangement.
- A supported access point lacks an equivalent accessible path to create,
  inspect, revise, publish, pause, expire, revoke, or delete a publication.
- An individual report of unwanted contact or exposure is dismissed because
  aggregate reverse-discovery engagement appears positive.

## Permitted measures

- Synthetic tests demonstrating absence of network requests from the companion.
- Local deletion and expiry verification using synthetic entries.
- Aggregate, non-account-linked publication expiry and revocation completion
  checks.
- Accessibility task-completion outcomes using synthetic publications.
- Explicit participant feedback about publication clarity, contact policy,
  visibility control, expiry, revocation, and unwanted-contact risk.

## Prohibited measures

- Companion-content collection.
- Scroll-depth or dwell-time analytics.
- Engagement optimization.
- Publication popularity, click-through, response-rate, or conversion ranking.
- Query-history profiling.
- Sensitive identity, health, recovery, finance, housing, sexuality, location,
  capacity, or vulnerability inference.
- Publication-based discovery boost.
- Automated identity or intent classification.
