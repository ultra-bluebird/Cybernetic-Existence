# FreedomProtectionSignal and IdentityNonDisclosureShield Research Contract

## Primary promises

FreedomProtectionSignal:
Document and enforce that every Hearthline user may disclose, decline, pause,
withdraw, leave, remain silent, change their mind, keep information private,
use an accessible access path, and request correction or appeal without needing
to prove an identity, circumstance, diagnosis, experience, access need, or
reason.

IdentityNonDisclosureShield:
Prevent Hearthline from requiring, inferring, storing, querying, ranking,
advertising from, profiling from, or using undisclosed identity categories and
sensitive private circumstances while preserving voluntary, scoped,
recipient-controlled self-description.

## FreedomProtectionSignal constraints

- The Signal is a platform documentation and enforcement object, never a user
  badge, status, achievement, reputation, trust marker, or profile field.
- Decline, silence, pause, withdrawal, block, cancellation, privacy choice,
  accessibility preference, low-bandwidth use, and refusal to disclose do not
  reduce a user's worth, desirability, ordinary discovery eligibility, or
  right to use Hearthline.
- A profile, match, message, invitation, meeting plan, shared preference, or
  prior interaction does not create standing consent or future entitlement.
- Critical controls are immediate, accessible, explainable, and do not require
  a reason.
- The Signal makes only truthful platform-controlled guarantees and explains
  that Hearthline cannot prevent off-platform copying, screenshots, memory, or
  third-party behavior.

## IdentityNonDisclosureShield constraints

- Identity-category disclosure is voluntary unless narrowly required by law or
  essential security operations, and any such requirement must be separately
  documented, minimized, accessible, correctable, and reviewable.
- Hearthline does not infer sensitive identity, health, recovery, financial,
  housing, employment, sexuality, capacity, vulnerability, private intimacy,
  augmentation, assistive-technology, or communication categories from
  language, images, voice, metadata, behavior, timing, social graphs, device
  data, location patterns, or refusal to disclose.
- Undisclosed categories cannot be stored, queried, filtered, ranked,
  advertised, used for moderation scoring, used for eligibility, or used to
  change feature access.
- There is no profile-completion penalty or non-disclosure penalty.
- Voluntary self-description is user-controlled, scope-limited, revocable
  where technically feasible, and excluded from discovery, ranking,
  advertising, analytics, and model training unless the user separately,
  clearly, and currently authorizes a narrow purpose.
- Inference-guard audit events contain no user identity, raw input, profile,
  session, device, location, message, or derived sensitive category.
- The Shield is enforced through schemas, APIs, query builders, model-feature
  review, data-lineage review, logging review, and human governance.

## Always-stop conditions

- A user must disclose identity, health, recovery, sexuality, financial,
  housing, employment, disability, access need, relationship history, body
  data, private intimacy, augmentation, or another sensitive category to use
  an ordinary connection, privacy, safety, appeal, or accessibility feature.
- A refusal, silence, privacy choice, access preference, or withheld identity
  reduces ordinary discovery eligibility, feature access, rank, visibility, or
  service quality.
- The system infers or stores a sensitive category from content, behavior,
  metadata, timing, device, location, images, voice, social graph, or a
  disclosure refusal.
- Identity or sensitive-category data reaches discovery, ranking,
  recommendation, advertising, analytics, model training, moderation scoring,
  eligibility, payment, or external-action systems outside the expressly
  authorized scope.
- Inference-guard records contain user identity, raw content, device data,
  location, profile, session, or derived sensitive category.
- A critical freedom control lacks a direct or equivalent accessible path.
- An individual report of identity, privacy, dignity, or access harm is
  dismissed solely because aggregate controls appear compliant.

## Permitted measures

- Synthetic schema, API, query, feature-registry, and data-lineage scan results.
- Aggregate-only inference-guard event counts with minimum retention and
  small-cell suppression.
- Accessibility task-completion tests using synthetic accounts and scenarios.
- Voluntary feedback about whether freedom guarantees are clear and whether
  non-disclosure feels respected.
- Human-review correction and appeal outcomes in aggregate.

## Prohibited measures

- Identity completion rates.
- Identity inference accuracy.
- Sensitive-category prediction.
- Disclosure refusal profiling.
- Identity-targeted advertising.
- Identity-based ranking or filtering.
- Access-preference profiling.
- User-level inference-guard logs.
- Private-message, image, voice, or behavior analysis for category extraction.
