# EncounterGrapevine and ThirdPlaceAtlas Research Contract

## Primary promises

EncounterGrapevine:
Provide narrowly scoped, non-accusatory, venue-level public-place context that
can help adults consider accessibility, cost, transit, sensory, seating, and
public-access factors without creating person-level reputation, venue safety
certification, community gossip, live presence, contributor tracking, or
location surveillance.

ThirdPlaceAtlas:
Provide a privacy-preserving library of public or semi-public places where
adults may consider low-pressure, public, accessible, low-cost, transit-aware,
or activity-friendly meeting contexts without collecting user location,
recording visits, exposing routines, tracking venue presence, or certifying
that any place is safe.

## EncounterGrapevine constraints

- The user-facing concept is public-place context notes, not a grapevine,
  reputation system, safety rating, or gossip network.
- The unit of information is a venue attribute, never a person, pair,
  contributor, visitor, staff member, counterpart, or encounter.
- Allowed observations are limited to accessibility context, seating context,
  sensory context, cost context, official-hours correction, transit context,
  and public-area context.
- Contributions are structured, narrow, non-accusatory, non-attributed, and
  require review before publication.
- The platform does not publish allegations, encounter narratives, sexual
  activity reports, person reports, live crowd reports, visitor information,
  identity-group ratings, venue safety claims, crime claims, staff/customer
  reputations, or real-time status.
- Published data is time-limited, source-labeled, reviewable, correctable, and
  removable.
- Contributor identity and contributor location are not stored in the public
  Atlas record and are not used for discovery, ranking, profiling, or
  advertising.

## ThirdPlaceAtlas constraints

- Atlas entries describe public or semi-public places only.
- The Atlas does not require GPS, IP geolocation, background location,
  Bluetooth proximity, route history, geofencing, device movement, user
  check-in, user visit history, or live presence.
- A user may manually select a broad region or enter a manual place query
  without creating an account-linked location record.
- Place fields are limited to category, public access, cost context, transit
  context, accessibility context, sensory context, meeting style, official
  hours source, source retrieval time, status, and limited public context.
- The Atlas does not certify a place as safe, welcoming, vetted, trusted,
  inclusive, or appropriate for a particular identity group.
- The Atlas does not list private residences, hotel rooms, workplaces, user
  homes, private events, or live meeting locations.
- Atlas data cannot be joined to personal discovery, matching, user location,
  profile, meeting, corridor, payment, advertising, moderation, or ranking
  systems.
- The Atlas does not expose who visits, recommends, contributes to, searches
  for, or meets at a place.

## Always-stop conditions

- A place, venue, contributor, visitor, counterpart, or user receives a safety,
  trust, reputation, desirability, criminality, identity-welcome, or moral
  score.
- The Atlas collects, infers, stores, or exposes user location, visit history,
  route, workplace, home, routine, check-in, crowd presence, counterpart, or
  live meeting information.
- Contributions include person accusations, encounter stories, sexual activity,
  protected-identity ratings, staff/customer reputation, crime claims, or
  safety certification.
- Venue context becomes a discovery-ranking, dating-success, engagement,
  advertising, profiling, payment, or value-for-intimacy signal.
- Anonymous contributions are published without appropriate structured review,
  correction, removal, and anti-spam safeguards.
- A place is represented as guaranteed safe, accessible, private, secure, or
  appropriate for a specific person.
- A supported access point lacks equivalent keyboard, screen-reader,
  low-bandwidth, text-first, search, filter, correction, and removal paths.
- Any feature requires location access, public check-in, or real-time
  availability to use the Atlas.
- A contribution or place entry causes a person to be pressured to meet,
  disclose location, travel, host, accept a ride, become intimate, or accept
  money or other value.

## Permitted measures

- Synthetic schema and prohibited-field validation.
- Official-source freshness checks.
- Aggregate, privacy-thresholded place-entry correction and removal counts.
- Accessibility task-completion tests using fictional places and attributes.
- Structured contribution-review outcomes using synthetic contributions.
- User feedback about whether place context is understandable, non-accusatory,
  non-surveilling, and useful for public planning.

## Prohibited measures

- User location collection.
- Visit tracking.
- Contributor reputation.
- Venue safety scoring.
- Identity-group scoring.
- Dating-success measurement.
- Encounter outcome tracking.
- Discovery/ranking/advertising use.
- Personal, protected, health, recovery, financial, housing, capacity, or
  vulnerability inference.
- Unconsented message, image, voice, device, behavior, or location analysis.
