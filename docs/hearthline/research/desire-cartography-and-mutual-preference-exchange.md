# DesireCartography and MutualPreferenceExchange Research Contract

## Primary promises

DesireCartography:
Provide an optional, local-first, user-authored private reflection space where
an adult may record, revise, export, share deliberately, or delete their own
current desires, curiosities, boundaries, pace, affection preferences, privacy
preferences, rest, uncertainty, and non-disclosure without creating a public
inventory, matching input, identity inference, score, profile field, consent
record, or platform behavioral dossier.

MutualPreferenceExchange:
Provide a mutually opened, structured, asynchronous, revisable, revocable,
temporary, non-scoring context in which two adults may independently choose
whether to share selected current discussion preferences without turning
similarity or difference into a verdict, a compatibility score, a consent
record, a checklist of promised activity, a requirement to negotiate, or a
condition for meeting, intimacy, or continued contact.

## DesireCartography constraints

- Cartography is local-first. The default is disabled or session-only.
- Entries are private to the author unless the author explicitly exports or
  manually shares a selected excerpt.
- Hearthline does not provide automatic counterpart sharing, social sharing,
  discovery sharing, matching use, profile use, recommendation use, advertising
  use, analytics use, model-training use, or moderation use.
- Entries contain no automatic counterpart, profile, room, conversation,
  meeting, location, address, workplace, schedule, identity, health, recovery,
  sexuality, financial, housing, employment, body, or encounter reference.
- The object does not infer or calculate desire level, orientation, gender,
  role, health, recovery, trauma, relationship readiness, compatibility,
  consent, confidence, wellness, desirability, risk, or future behavior.
- Users may record uncertainty, contradiction, no desire, no label, rest,
  privacy, no change, a changed mind, or a preference not to answer.
- Local persistence is optional, encrypted where supported, user-controlled,
  network-isolated, and immediately deletable.
- Hearthline does not promise that a user-controlled export or manually shared
  excerpt can be recalled from another application or person.

## MutualPreferenceExchange constraints

- The exchange starts only when both adults separately choose to open it.
- Participants draft independently before revealing selected cards.
- Every card is optional, current, revisable, revocable, recipient-scoped, and
  may use blank, `not_sure`, `depends`, `not_discussing_now`, or
  `prefer_not_to_share`.
- Initial cards are limited to conversation pace, boundary conversation style,
  affection discussion, privacy preference, substance boundary, follow-up
  preference, difference acknowledgment, and no-disclosure.
- The exchange presents only `shared`, `different`, `not_shared`, and
  `not_discussing` context labels.
- The exchange does not show a grid score, percentage, alignment label,
  compatibility verdict, recommendation, warning, red/green flag, readiness
  status, medical statement, consent record, legal statement, or real-world
  permission.
- Difference is valid information. Either participant may continue, pause, or
  end the exchange without explanation.
- Shared preferences do not imply consent to touch, sexual activity, meeting,
  private venue, image exchange, travel, lodging, transport, aftercare, money,
  gifts, work, services, or future contact.
- The exchange cannot unlock a platform capability or alter eligibility,
  discovery, ranking, advertising, moderation, or any person-level score.
- The exchange is short-lived, has no automatic revival, and ordinary platform
  access ends on expiry or revocation.
- Hearthline does not claim that expiry or revocation can prevent off-platform
  copying, screenshots, manual transcription, memory, or recipient retention.

## Always-stop conditions

- Cartography content leaves the device without explicit user action, enters
  logs, is synchronized, is used for analytics, or changes platform behavior.
- Cartography becomes automatically linked to a person, profile, message, room,
  meeting, location, or discovery object.
- Any desire reflection becomes a score, match input, identity inference,
  profile field, behavior prediction, consent record, or public inventory.
- The exchange pressures a participant to reveal, mirror, negotiate, agree,
  meet, travel, host, receive a ride, provide care, share an image, become
  intimate, or continue contact.
- The exchange produces a verdict, percentage, score, recommendation, consent
  implication, real-world permission claim, readiness label, or compatibility
  classification.
- A blank, pause, revocation, non-disclosure choice, difference, or exit
  reduces discovery, feature access, profile visibility, ranking, or ordinary
  service access.
- Exchange data reaches discovery, ranking, advertising, analytics, model
  training, moderation scoring, eligibility, or profile fields.
- A user cannot access an equivalent keyboard, screen-reader, low-bandwidth,
  asynchronous, text-first, pause, revoke, expiry, and exit path.
- A claim of encryption, confidentiality, ephemerality, deletion, revocation,
  or unrecoverability exceeds verified implementation evidence.
- A value-for-intimacy path involving money, gifts, housing, lodging,
  transportation, work, debt, drugs, goods, services, or another valuable
  consideration is introduced.

## Permitted measures

- Synthetic local-only network-isolation tests for Cartography.
- Synthetic state-machine tests for independent drafting, reveal, pause,
  revision, revocation, expiry, and no automatic revival.
- Accessibility task-completion tests using fictional accounts and cards.
- Voluntary feedback about pressure, clarity, difference, pause, privacy,
  accessibility, and revocation using fictional scenarios where preferred.
- Security review outcomes using synthetic declarations only.

## Prohibited measures

- Desire-content analysis.
- Sexual preference inference.
- Compatibility scoring.
- Alignment percentage.
- Consent prediction.
- Identity, health, recovery, sexuality, financial, housing, capacity,
  vulnerability, or relationship-readiness inference.
- Discovery, ranking, advertising, moderation, eligibility, or behavioral
  profiling effects.
- Off-platform outcome prediction.
- Unconsented private-message, image, voice, or behavior analysis.
