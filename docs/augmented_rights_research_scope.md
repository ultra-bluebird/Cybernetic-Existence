# Augmented Rights Research Scope

## Purpose

This document defines a research and governance scope for equal access,
privacy, autonomy, accessible communication, correction, appeal, continuity,
and non-interference protections for people who voluntarily describe their
relationship with assistive, cybernetic, medical, communication, or
body-integrated technologies.

The document does not create a legal status, identity class, public registry,
medical diagnosis, device certification, institutional affiliation, or
eligibility category.

## Normative research anchors

The research maps protections to authoritative human-rights and accessibility
sources, including dignity, autonomy, equality, non-discrimination, physical
and mental integrity, privacy, access to assistive technologies, accessible
information, participation, and effective remedy.

Applicable medical-device, health, privacy, consumer-protection, workplace,
accessibility, and product-safety requirements remain independent and must be
evaluated within their jurisdictional and factual context.

## Core governance commitments

1. Respect voluntary self-description without requesting proof, diagnosis,
   inventory, or disclosure of technical or medical details.
2. Provide accessible communication, human assistance, correction, and appeal
   without requiring a credential, cryptographic proof, or technical access.
3. Apply data minimization, purpose limitation, retention limits, and
   correction mechanisms to all identity- or accessibility-related records.
4. Do not infer augmentation, health, disability, neural status, credibility,
   institutional affiliation, or eligibility from communication mode, metadata,
   use of assistive technology, or refusal to disclose.
5. Keep all automated outputs advisory and reviewable. They cannot directly
   operate devices, infrastructure, medical systems, or external services.
6. Separate personal preferences from governance duties. A voluntary preference
   statement may request privacy or accessibility; it does not authorize
   classification, surveillance, or automated decision-making.
7. Require human review for material decisions, disputed records, policy
   exceptions, and accessibility barriers.

## Technical interoperability

Voluntary preference records may use W3C Verifiable Credentials and
privacy-preserving presentations when the holder chooses them. Implementations
must support short-lived, audience-bound, minimal-disclosure presentations and
must provide an equivalent non-cryptographic pathway.

No proof system may be required to receive equal service, respectful treatment,
accessible communication, correction, appeal, or human assistance.

## Research questions

- Which existing rights and accessibility protections apply to specific
  documented service barriers?
- What data is strictly necessary to provide an accommodation or preserve a
  correction path?
- How can platforms minimize correlation and avoid retaining preference records
  longer than necessary?
- Which independent legal and technical reviews are required before a system
  makes a material decision?
