# Cybernetic-Host Friendly Business Certification Rubric

## Purpose

The Cybernetic-Host Friendly Business Certification is a voluntary, third-party-auditable recognition program for local businesses and public-facing organizations.

It recognizes practical commitments to:

- Physical accessibility.
- Accessible communication.
- Sensory accommodation.
- Non-discrimination.
- Privacy-respecting service.
- Staff readiness.
- Assistive and augmentative technology inclusion.
- Accessible grievance and continuous improvement.

It is not a legal compliance certificate, medical endorsement, device certification, government license, safety certification, or guarantee that every access need can be met in every circumstance.

A business may participate without asking customers, workers, or visitors to disclose whether they are cybernetic hosts, disabled, use assistive technology, or have health-related needs.

Businesses that serve the public may have obligations under applicable disability-rights and public-accommodation law. In the United States, ADA Title III covers many public accommodations and requires equal opportunity to access offered goods and services, while accessibility duties may include standards for new construction and alterations and barrier removal where readily achievable. [245][248][250]

## Eligibility Principles

To seek recognition, a business must:

- Serve the public or provide public-facing services.
- Provide a named accessibility and inclusion contact.
- Publish a plain-language non-discrimination and accommodation statement.
- Maintain an accessible feedback and grievance path.
- Allow third-party review of relevant public-facing conditions and records.
- Not require customers to disclose cybernetic, health, disability, neural, biometric, or augmentation data to receive ordinary service.
- Not use self-declared cybernetic-host status for pricing, access restriction, surveillance, employment screening, or behavioral profiling.
- Agree to corrective-action follow-up when an audit identifies material gaps.

## Scoring Dimensions

The total score is one hundred points.

| Dimension | Points | Evidence examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Physical access | 30 | Step-free access, route continuity, door usability, restroom access, seating, counter access, wayfinding, emergency egress information |
| Communication and information access | 20 | Accessible website and menu, readable signage, captions, plain language, alternative formats, phone and in-person contact paths |
| Sensory and environmental accommodation | 15 | Adjustable lighting or lower-sensory option, quiet-service timing, fragrance-aware practices where feasible, clear advance information |
| Non-discrimination and privacy | 15 | Written policy, no mandatory disclosure, no adverse treatment, data-minimization practice, accessible grievance process |
| Staff training and service readiness | 15 | Recurring training, respectful communication, assistive-technology handling boundaries, de-escalation, escalation contact |
| Community accountability | 5 | Community feedback, corrective-action record, independent audit cooperation, accessible public status information |

## Mandatory Gates

A business cannot receive a certification tier if any gate fails:

- No written non-discrimination and accommodation statement.
- No accessible contact or grievance path.
- Mandatory disclosure of disability, cybernetic-host status, augmentation, health, or device information for ordinary service.
- Use of biometric, neural, device, or augmentation information for adverse customer treatment.
- Material inaccessible public entry route without disclosure and a documented, accessible alternative where feasible.
- Refusal to permit independent audit of claimed certification evidence.
- Material unresolved accessibility or discrimination complaint without documented response.

## Tier Thresholds

| Tier | Score | Additional conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| Bronze | 55–69 | Mandatory gates pass; improvement plan published |
| Silver | 70–84 | Mandatory gates pass; accessible feedback loop and staff training evidence verified |
| Gold | 85–100 | Mandatory gates pass; recurring independent audit, strong sensory and communication access, documented corrective-action effectiveness |

A score below 55, or any mandatory-gate failure, results in `not_certified` or `review_required`. It does not imply that a business is unlawful; it means the voluntary program lacks sufficient verified evidence for its own recognition threshold.

## Physical Accessibility Detail

Physical access review considers:

- Arrival and parking or transit connection.
- Continuous accessible route from public way to entrance.
- Step-free entry or a clearly identified equivalent access route.
- Door opening, maneuvering, and activation usability.
- Counter, seating, restroom, and waiting-area access.
- Route width, surface continuity, lighting, and wayfinding.
- Emergency exit and evacuation information.
- Maintenance response for ramps, automatic doors, tactile cues, elevators, and accessible restroom equipment.

The review should use the applicable jurisdictional standard and qualified access assessor rather than treating a generic score as proof of legal compliance.

## Communication and Information Detail

Communication evidence may include:

- Plain-language service information.
- Accessible digital content aligned with applicable accessibility standards.
- Captions, text alternatives, readable contrast, keyboard access, and screen-reader compatibility.
- Alternative ordering, booking, feedback, and support pathways.
- In-person, phone, and non-app access.
- Clear notice of available accommodations and how to request help.
- Accessible emergency, evacuation, and service-change communication.

W3C accessibility standards apply to web and mobile content and can also inform accessibility for non-web information and communications technologies. [247]

## Sensory Accommodation Detail

The program recognizes practical, non-medical sensory accommodations:

- Clearly stated quiet periods.
- Lower-stimulation seating or service area where feasible.
- Adjustable music or lighting on request where feasible.
- Fragrance-aware cleaning or notice of strong environmental conditions.
- Clear advance notice of sensory conditions.
- Staff ability to provide written, spoken, or step-by-step service information.
- Space for assistive or augmentative technology without unnecessary scrutiny.

No participant must disclose a diagnosis, augmentation, sensory profile, or reason for requesting a broadly available accommodation.

## Non-Discrimination and Privacy Detail

The business must demonstrate:

- A public statement of equal service and non-discrimination.
- No surcharge for ordinary accessibility accommodation.
- No adverse use of self-declared cybernetic-host or assistive-technology information.
- No mandatory biometric, neural, device, or health disclosure for baseline service.
- Data minimization for any optional service preference.
- An accessible, non-retaliatory complaint process.
- A documented response standard and corrective-action workflow.

## Staff Training Detail

Training must cover:

- Respectful, person-directed communication.
- Avoidance of assumptions about capability, identity, or medical status.
- How to offer assistance without touching a person or device without permission.
- How to communicate available access options.
- How to respond to malfunctioning accessibility equipment.
- Privacy and non-disclosure requirements.
- Escalation to a named accessibility contact.
- Complaint, correction, and service-recovery process.

## Third-Party Audit Process

The independent auditor must:

- Use an accessible audit protocol.
- Inspect evidence against each scoring dimension.
- Conduct a site walkthrough where appropriate.
- Review policy, training, and grievance evidence.
- Test public-facing access and communication pathways.
- Collect voluntary feedback without requiring sensitive identity disclosure.
- Produce a plain-language report.
- Identify evidence gaps, corrective actions, and re-audit timeline.
- Disclose conflicts of interest.
- Avoid collecting or publishing customer identity, augmentation status, health information, biometrics, or neural data.

Audit evidence is graded:

| Evidence status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Independently observed, tested, or documented |
| Partially verified | Some evidence present; corrective action or follow-up required |
| Self-attested | Business statement awaiting independent verification |
| Not available | No sufficient evidence provided |

Only verified and partially verified evidence may contribute to a final tier. Self-attested evidence can support an improvement plan but cannot by itself produce Gold status.

## Certification Validity

- Bronze recognition is valid for twelve months.
- Silver recognition is valid for twelve months with a mid-cycle accessibility update.
- Gold recognition is valid for twelve months with a mid-cycle independent follow-up.
- Material change to public access, location, ownership, policy, accessibility equipment, or complaint status triggers review.
- Businesses may request reassessment after corrective action.
- A suspended or expired certificate must be displayed accurately in the public directory.
