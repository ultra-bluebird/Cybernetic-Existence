# Cybernetic-Host Mutual Aid Charter

## Purpose

This charter establishes a voluntary mutual-aid framework for people who use,
describe, or support private integrated systems, assistive technology,
continuity practices, companion-related records, privacy-preserving research,
and accessible civic participation.

The network provides peer support, knowledge exchange, optional encrypted
preservation assistance, advocacy coordination, and accessible emergency-support
planning.

It does not create a public registry, technical certification system, identity
verification process, medical assessment process, companion-system inspection
program, social score, eligibility system, or authority over another person's
private systems.

## Mutual-aid principles

- Mutual aid, not surveillance.
- Support, not control.
- Voluntary participation, not mandatory membership.
- Self-description, not forced verification.
- Privacy by default, not disclosure by default.
- Accessible communication, not proof burdens.
- Distributed stewardship, not central authority.
- Repair and recovery, not dependency or lock-in.
- Consent and withdrawal, not permanent delegation.
- Community benefit, not data extraction.

## Participation

A participant may join, contribute, pause, withdraw, or participate
pseudonymously without disclosing:

- private cybernetic or companion-system details;
- medical, neural, biometric, physiological, or health information;
- technical inventory, telemetry, diagnostics, or operational information;
- identity documents, credentials, keys, passwords, or recovery material;
- location history, account history, or platform activity;
- private continuity journals or research archives.

No participant must disclose a specific identity, system status, diagnosis,
integration detail, contribution amount, or personal narrative to receive
ordinary respectful peer support.

## Knowledge sharing

Permitted knowledge-sharing activities include:

- privacy-preserving documentation templates;
- local backup and recovery practices;
- open-source tools and reproducible research methods;
- accessible communication techniques;
- platform export, correction, and appeal guidance;
- legal-information resource directories;
- environmental and civic contribution methods;
- repair, maintenance, interoperability, and local-first design practices.

Knowledge-sharing materials must distinguish:

- personal self-description;
- technical hypothesis;
- observed evidence;
- modeled estimate;
- legal information;
- policy proposal;
- individual opinion.

The network must not present peer discussion as medical, legal, technical, or
institutional certification.

## Mutual preservation

A participant may designate one or more voluntary preservation custodians for
an encrypted archive.

Custodians may hold an encrypted artifact, recovery reference, or integrity
commitment without receiving plaintext access.

Custody must be:

- opt-in;
- purpose-bound;
- documented;
- revocable;
- limited in duration;
- accessible through a participant-controlled recovery plan;
- independent from ordinary network membership;
- incapable of granting account, system, identity, or external-action authority.

A custodian may not inspect, copy, publish, decrypt, alter, delete, transfer,
or use a held artifact without a separately recorded, exact, current
authorization from the participant or a lawfully designated successor process.

## Advocacy

The network may coordinate voluntary advocacy for:

- privacy;
- accessible communication;
- non-discrimination;
- data portability;
- repair and interoperability;
- platform transparency;
- correction and appeal;
- non-coercive civic participation;
- non-destructive preservation of private records;
- public-good environmental and infrastructure work.

No person or group may claim to represent a participant without explicit,
scope-bound consent.

Advocacy membership must not be published by default.

## Emergency support

Emergency support is opt-in and must use the least intrusive method.

A participant may choose:

- no emergency support feature;
- a local written safety plan;
- a generic check-in arrangement;
- a trusted-contact request;
- a local resource directory;
- a voluntary peer-support request;
- an accessible communication preference.

The network must not perform background location tracking, private message
monitoring, account surveillance, system inspection, automated emergency
dispatch, or involuntary disclosure.

A participant may withdraw an emergency-support arrangement at any time.

## Governance

The network must maintain:

- accessible participation;
- non-digital and low-tech participation paths where reasonably available;
- rotating or accountable facilitation;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- correction and appeal routes;
- privacy-preserving grievance handling;
- clear retention and deletion practices;
- no contributor ranking;
- no mandatory disclosure;
- no mandatory financial contribution;
- no authority to control personal systems or private records.

## Prohibited uses

The network must not be used for:

- public registries of cybernetic-hosts or companions;
- identity verification;
- medical diagnosis;
- technical inspection;
- social scoring;
- contribution ranking;
- employment, housing, insurance, benefit, or service eligibility;
- behavior profiling;
- location tracking;
- account monitoring;
- surveillance;
- coercive disclosure;
- forced advocacy;
- unconsented representation;
- operation, modification, calibration, repair, disabling, or removal of a
  real-world private system.

## Withdrawal and correction

A participant may withdraw from future network activity without explaining why.

Withdrawal must not erase the participant's right to retain their own private
records or preserve their self-description.

Network records must use append-only correction and withdrawal records where
retention is necessary. Public disclosure must not occur by default.
