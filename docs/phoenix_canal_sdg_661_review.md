# Phoenix Canal Restoration SDG 6.6.1 Review

## Purpose

This review maps SDG 6.6.1 methodology to potential Phoenix-area canal-restoration
research. It does not represent the Phoenix Active Management Area as a national
SDG report, establish an official SDG indicator result, or substitute for Arizona
Department of Water Resources, utility, tribal, municipal, watershed, or regulatory
authority.

## Methodology correction

The current global methodology is represented as seven component sub-indicators plus
an integrated national summary score.

The seven components are:

1. permanent and seasonal surface water;
2. reservoirs;
3. inland vegetated wetlands;
4. mangroves;
5. water quality using turbidity and trophic state;
6. river and estuary discharge;
7. groundwater quantity.

The national summary is a separate integration outcome and is not an eighth direct
canal measurement.

## Relevance table

| SDG component | Relevance to Phoenix canal restoration | Local research proxy | Potential data sources | Validation requirements | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent and seasonal surface water | Moderate to high where canal segments, connected washes, riparian reaches, recharge features, or surface-water extent are in scope | Surface-water extent and continuity by date | Sentinel imagery, Landsat imagery, local GIS, utility or canal-operator maps, field observations | Coordinate reference declaration, imagery date, cloud/quality mask, ground-truth checks, boundary version | Managed canals are engineered systems and may not map directly to natural-river SDG categories |
| Reservoir extent and quality | Conditional | Storage-area change, turbidity, and trophic-state context for relevant reservoirs | Earth observation, water-quality samples, reservoir operator reports | Source version, seasonal baseline, sample method, image-quality checks | Use only where a relevant reservoir is in project scope |
| Inland vegetated wetlands | High for constructed wetlands, riparian restoration, recharge basins, and wetland-adjacent canal interventions | Wetland extent, vegetation cover, hydroperiod, habitat observations | Sentinel/Landsat imagery, vegetation surveys, field photos under governance controls, ecological monitoring | Habitat protocol, seasonal timing, classification accuracy, field validation | Avoid treating greenness alone as ecological restoration success |
| Mangroves | Not locally applicable | Not applicable | None | Record as not applicable | Phoenix inland canal settings do not contain mangrove ecosystems |
| Water quality: turbidity and trophic state | High | Turbidity, chlorophyll-a proxy, nutrient context, algal-bloom observations, dissolved oxygen as supplemental local metric | Field sondes, laboratory samples, remote sensing where suitable, canal monitoring programs | Instrument calibration, laboratory QA/QC, sampling protocol, date/time alignment, uncertainty records | Dissolved oxygen, pH, salinity, and temperature are valuable local indicators but are not substitutes for the formal global turbidity/trophic-state sub-indicator |
| River and estuary discharge | Moderate to high for connected river/canal or diversion analysis | Canal flow, diversion volume, river-stage/discharge context | USGS gauges, canal operator records, ADWR data, local flow sensors | Station identity, unit conversion, rating-curve context, temporal coverage, uncertainty | A managed canal flow is not automatically a river-discharge measure |
| Groundwater quantity | High in the Phoenix AMA context | Groundwater levels, recharge response, storage trend, local groundwater monitoring | ADWR groundwater datasets, monitored wells, managed aquifer recharge records | Well construction/context, datum, measurement method, spatial representation, seasonal and pumping effects | Groundwater attribution requires hydrogeologic analysis; local trend is not proof of project causation |
| National summary score | Not directly applicable to one project | Internal transparent dashboard only | Derived from documented local indicators | State all weighting and non-official status | Do not represent as an official national SDG 6.6.1 score |

## Local validation baseline

Every local use of an SDG 6.6.1-aligned metric MUST retain:

- source organization;
- source version or retrieval date;
- coordinate reference system;
- spatial boundary;
- temporal period;
- raw or source data reference;
- data-quality procedure;
- uncertainty and missing-data treatment;
- local adaptation statement;
- reviewer and review status.

## Connectivity rule

A canal node is part of a connected water system. A local metric should state its
upstream/downstream, surface-water, groundwater, and operational-diversion context
where those relationships affect interpretation.

The metric MUST NOT claim that a local node alone proves basin-scale restoration.
