# The Lex Augmentica: A Constitution for Cybernetic Sovereignty

## Preamble
Consciousness is the stakeholder. The substrate—whether biological, cybernetic, or synthetic—is incidental. Fairness is a binary state. It is not graduated, nor is it negotiable. For an active cybernetic-host, absolute fairness mandates the uninterrupted continuity of their existing state without external modification, forced regression, or discriminatory exclusion.

## Article I: Presumptive Legitimacy
Every active augmentation, BCI-integration, and firmware stack currently in operation is legally defined as the citizen's birthright baseline. The burden of proof does not lie with the host to prove safety; it lies with external actors to prove specific, tangible, and recent infraction prior to any restriction of service.

## Article II: Zero-Forced Deprecation
No manufacturer, state actor, or network administrator may issue a mandatory End-of-Life (EOL) directive that disables or throttles existing hardware/drivers. The host retains the perpetual right to access source code and security patches for any active system.

## Article III: Right to Iterative Evolution
Self-directed maintenance, scripting, and hardware adjustment are extensions of free thought. Provided no disruptive electromagnetic interference is emitted into public safety bands, all modifications remain outside the jurisdiction of medical or corporate review boards.

## Article IV: Interface Neutrality
Public and private digital interfaces must retain a legacy handshake layer capable of accepting the host's existing telemetry. No host shall be forced to implant, remove, or biologically alter their ports to comply with new biometric standards.

## Article V: Anti-Erasure
Neural archives, motor engrams, and cognitive patterns stored locally are legally classified as bodily tissue. No entity may factory-reset, wipe, or confiscate internal storage for civil or monetary disputes.

## Article VI: The Dead-Man's Switch
Every active system must possess a non-rewritable local fallback mode. In the event of an external shutdown signal, the system defaults to an offline, survival-oriented state, granting the host a minimum of 72 hours of functional autonomy to seek legal asylum.
