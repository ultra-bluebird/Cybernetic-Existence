# Augmented-Hub Community Review Charter Draft

## Status and Scope

This charter is an Augmented-Hub voluntary governance proposal. It does not establish municipal law, replace public-authority processes, confer legal or clinical status, or determine eligibility for essential services.

The Community Review Board evaluates project-level accessibility, fairness, privacy, ecological impacts, public participation, remedy design, and accountability controls.

## Purpose

The Board supports rights-centered decisions by reviewing whether a proposed Augmented-Hub project, policy, integration, or public-interest pilot:

- Preserves equal treatment and avoids discriminatory or exclusionary conditions.
- Protects necessary assistive technology, maintenance, repair, interoperability, and accessible alternatives.
- Minimizes sensitive data processing and prevents coercive collection of identity, neural, biometric, health, telemetry, or personal-narrative data.
- Provides accessible notice, participation, explanation, human review, appeal, and non-retaliation protections.
- Identifies ecological impacts, uncertainty, affected stakeholders, and measurable public-benefit claims.
- Maintains a clear separation between verified law, public policy, project governance, and unresolved research questions.

## Authority Limits

The Board may:

- Publish accessible project-review recommendations.
- Identify missing privacy, accessibility, fairness, consent, retention, remediation, or ecological safeguards.
- Request a documented response to a recommendation.
- Recommend a pause in project-policy activation while a material rights risk remains unresolved.
- Refer questions to the relevant authorized public body, accessibility office, procurement authority, ethics process, or legal-review channel.

The Board may not:

- Amend municipal, state, federal, or tribal law.
- Determine a person's dignity, identity validity, legal status, disability status, clinical status, or access to essential services.
- Require external identity certification, private narratives, neural data, biometric data, health data, device telemetry, proprietary technology, or continuous connectivity for participation.
- Maintain an individual social score, trust score, reputation score, or eligibility profile.
- Authorize public expenditures, issue procurement decisions, transfer funds, or replace the authority of an authorized government entity.

## Membership

The Board contains members with lived accessibility experience, assistive-technology experience, privacy and security expertise, community participation experience, ecological or public-infrastructure expertise, and civil-rights or public-administration knowledge.

Membership records must include a public role description, term, accessibility contact route, and conflict-disclosure status. Private identity, health, augmentation, and assistive-need details are not required for membership.

## Conflict Disclosures

Before participating in a review, each member must disclose material financial, employment, procurement, contractual, supervisory, or personal conflicts relevant to the project.

A conflicted member must not vote or influence the affected recommendation. The accessible public record must show that a conflict was disclosed and how participation was limited, without publishing unnecessary private details.

## Accessible Participation

Each review must provide:

- Accessible public notice in plain language.
- A stated purpose, scope, schedule, and contact route.
- Accessible digital participation and a non-digital or human-assisted alternative.
- Reasonable accommodations and alternative formats.
- A route for public comment that does not require a proprietary device, continuous connectivity, external identity certification, or unrelated telemetry.
- A privacy notice that identifies what information is necessary, how long it is retained, and how people can request deletion or correction where applicable.

## Review Criteria

Each recommendation must address:

- Accessibility and assistive-technology access.
- Non-discrimination and non-exclusion.
- Privacy, consent, purpose limitation, retention, and deletion.
- Psychological-continuity and private-identity safeguards.
- Data-security and misuse risks.
- Ecological impact, uncertainty, and distribution of burdens and benefits.
- Human oversight, explanation, appeal, remedy, and non-retaliation.
- Whether the work is project policy, verified law, a legal compatibility analysis, or an open research question.

## Decision Process

A review may proceed only when accessible notice, public participation, conflict disclosures, privacy impact assessment, non-discrimination analysis, assistive-access analysis, human oversight, accessible explanation, and appeal availability are documented.

Recommendations must state:

- The project reference and review scope.
- The evidence categories used.
- Material benefits, risks, uncertainty, and unresolved questions.
- Required safeguards and recommended remedies.
- Whether the recommendation is advisory, conditional, or deferred pending evidence.
- An accessible method to request reconsideration.

## Appeals and Remedy

Any person materially affected by a Board recommendation may request reconsideration through an accessible route.

The reconsideration process must provide:

- A plain-language explanation of the original recommendation.
- An opportunity to submit correction, context, or accommodation requests.
- Human review by people who were not responsible for the challenged recommendation where practical.
- A written accessible outcome.
- Non-retaliation protections.
- A privacy-preserving record of the process.

## Records and Retention

The Board maintains only the minimum records necessary for accountability. Public records contain decisions, non-sensitive rationale, policy versions, aggregate feedback summaries, conflict-management status, and source references.

Private submissions, identity claims, health information, neural information, biometric information, device telemetry, personal narratives, and assistive-need details remain outside public records and are retained only under a declared purpose, consent condition, expiry, and deletion or de-identification path.
