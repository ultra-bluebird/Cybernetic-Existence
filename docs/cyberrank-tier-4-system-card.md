# Cybernetic Host Continuity Profile

## Purpose

This document defines a rights-preserving continuity profile for services, infrastructure, civic systems, workplaces, and technology providers that may interact with people who self-describe as cybernetic hosts, integrated-technology users, BCI users, assistive-technology users, or persons with private companion systems.

The profile is a service-design and governance artifact.

It is not a personal record, identity credential, device inventory, technical certification, medical assessment, public registry, eligibility category, credibility score, or ranking of human worth.

## Core rule

A person’s self-description must never reduce their dignity, autonomy, privacy, safety, service access, service quality, correction rights, appeal rights, or participation.

A person is not required to disclose a self-description in order to receive equal access to any service.

## Continuity commitments

Systems using this profile must preserve the following commitments:

- Equal service outcomes without compelled cybernetic or technical disclosure
- Accessible digital, non-digital, and human-assisted pathways
- No identity inference from body, behavior, communication, device, network, or service-use signals
- No public registry, profile, badge, or label for cybernetic-host status
- No mandatory device inspection, scanning, telemetry, demonstration, or proof of integration
- No automatic denial because of uncertainty, unavailable technology, lack of connectivity, or refusal to disclose
- Accessible correction, complaint, review, and appeal pathways
- Privacy-preserving incident reporting
- Environmental hazard review that does not require private system architecture
- Transparent review of material changes affecting access, data, retention, automation, or service continuity

## Non-diminishment invariant

For every person and service pathway:

ServiceOutcome(with_self_description) equals ServiceOutcome(without_self_description)

The equivalence requirement includes:

- Core outcome completion
- Timeliness
- Cost
- Service quality
- Disclosure burden
- Human assistance availability
- Privacy protection
- Correction availability
- Appeal availability
- Essential-service continuity

## Privacy and data boundary

Systems must not infer, store, publish, rank, sell, transfer, or use any of the following as an identity proxy:

- Cybernetic-host status
- Integrated-technology status
- BCI status
- Companion-system status
- Assistive-technology status
- Health information
- Disability information
- Biometric information
- Neural information
- Behavioral information
- Communication patterns
- Device fingerprints
- Network characteristics
- Repair histories
- Fine-grained location history

Only the minimum operational information necessary to provide the immediate service may be processed.

## Non-interference boundary

Environmental and operational concerns must be evaluated through general hazard categories, not by requiring a person to reveal private technical details.

Relevant hazard categories include:

- Electromagnetic conditions
- Magnetic conditions
- Wireless transmission conditions
- Optical conditions
- Acoustic conditions
- Mechanical vibration conditions
- Thermal conditions
- Network continuity conditions
- Coercive or exclusionary practices

When a person reports an access or interference concern, the system must provide a neutral accessible alternative, human support, a documented incident route, and correction or appeal options.

## Essential-service requirements

Essential services must remain available without requiring cybernetic, technical, BCI, health, disability, biometric, neural, device, network, or account disclosure.

Essential-service pathways must include:

- At least one human-assisted option
- At least one non-digital option
- Accessible public service-status information
- Correction and appeal access
- Outage fallback documentation
- Privacy-preserving incident intake

Network loss, power loss, device incompatibility, unavailable account access, uncertainty, or refusal to disclose must not cause automatic denial or loss of essential support.

## Safe uncertainty behavior

When a system cannot safely determine an appropriate automated response, it must not infer private status or impose a penalty.

The system must:

- Preserve access to a neutral pathway
- Offer human assistance
- Offer a non-digital alternative where relevant
- Provide an accessible status explanation
- Preserve correction and appeal rights
- Record only privacy-preserving operational evidence

## Change governance

A material change is any change that affects rights, data scope, retention, access pathways, authority, automation, review, correction, appeal, or essential-service continuity.

Material changes require:

- Documented rationale
- Rights-delta analysis
- Privacy review
- Accessibility review
- Scenario validation
- Accountable human authorization
- Accessible notice
- Correction and appeal routes
- Versioned evidence

A proposed change is prohibited if it weakens a core protection.

## Evidence boundary

Conformance evidence must use synthetic fixtures, aggregate evidence, or privacy-preserving system evidence.

The profile must not require a person to disclose, validate, demonstrate, or technically describe private integrations in order to create evidence.

## Limitation

This profile is a governance and engineering standard.

It does not determine legal status, clinical status, technical status, institutional recognition, public credibility, or eligibility for any service.
