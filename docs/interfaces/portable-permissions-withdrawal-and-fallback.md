## Interoperability

Exchange only the minimum task information required.

Preserve:
- Permission scope.
- Refusal and withdrawal.
- Unknown or disputed findings.
- Visibility restrictions.
- Correction relationships.
- Accessibility preferences.

Do not silently discard a field whose loss weakens protection.

Imported records are not automatically current execution authority.

## Withdrawal

Distinguish:
- Received.
- Identified.
- Enforcement pending.
- Future activity stopped under the grant.
- Dependent-service confirmation pending.
- Residual-copy status unresolved.
- Remedy required.

Do not claim all copies were erased merely because a grant was revoked.

Disclose actual limits before a consequential release.

A withdrawal request must not trigger punitive loss of unrelated
essential access.

## Human fallback

Define the responsible support function, authority, accessible channels,
response obligations, escalation route, and continuity arrangements.

Support must not require use of the same inaccessible channel that failed.

Closure is not qualified restoration.

## Evidence

Interoperability requires cross-implementation tests.

Withdrawal requires enforcement and propagation evidence.

Fallback requires observed access, authority, action, and outcome.

No schema-valid response establishes these operational facts by itself.

## Privacy

No neural data, diagnosis, or augmentation inventory is required by default.

Support staff receive only information needed for the task.

Public reporting must not expose private cases or become participant ranking.
