# Cybernetic-Host Civic Contribution Framework

## Purpose

This framework supports voluntary civic contribution to environmental
monitoring, emergency communication, infrastructure maintenance, accessibility,
community support, ecological restoration, water resilience, food security, and
public-service improvement.

It treats cybernetic-host participation as one voluntary contribution pathway
among many. It does not require disclosure, proof, diagnosis, registration,
technical inspection, identity verification, or public acknowledgment of any
private system.

## Core rule

Contribution value is determined by evidence quality, relevance, uncertainty,
community benefit, and accountable use.

Contribution value is not determined by a person's body, technology,
augmentation, assistive device, private system, identity, status, payment
capacity, communication style, or willingness to disclose private information.

## Voluntary participation

All civic contributions are voluntary.

A contributor may use:

- a local digital tool;
- a public sensor;
- a community-owned monitoring kit;
- a paper form;
- a phone, relay, or staff-assisted channel;
- a community organization;
- an anonymous or pseudonymous report;
- an accessible non-digital pathway.

No person must own a device, disclose a private system, provide biometric data,
use a smartphone, accept location tracking, maintain a platform account, or
provide unpaid labor to obtain ordinary civic services or participate in public
life.

## Contribution domains

### Environmental monitoring

Permitted contribution classes include:

- air-quality and heat observations;
- water-quality and runoff observations;
- soil health and infiltration observations;
- shade, canopy, habitat, and biodiversity observations;
- waste-diversion and repairability observations;
- drought and food-security indicators.

Environmental contributions must include the method, uncertainty, source class,
and applicable calibration or quality information. They must exclude private
health, biometric, neural, private-system, and persistent location-history
data.

### Emergency communication support

Permitted contribution classes include:

- accessible alert review;
- public-resource and shelter information;
- translation, plain-language adaptation, relay, and communication support;
- non-identifying route or service availability updates;
- emergency accessibility barrier reports.

Emergency contribution does not authorize physical intervention, emergency
dispatch, surveillance, identity determination, or access to private systems.

### Infrastructure monitoring

Permitted contribution classes include:

- public repair observations;
- accessibility barrier reports;
- water leak, shade, heat, drainage, lighting, and outage reports;
- condition-monitoring observations;
- repair and maintenance documentation.

Infrastructure contribution is advisory. It does not authorize equipment
operation, utility control, enforcement, surveillance, or denial of service.

### Community support

Permitted contribution classes include:

- accessibility review;
- language and plain-language support;
- public-resource navigation;
- community feedback;
- repair documentation;
- public-space usability assessment;
- civic grievance and correction support.

## Privacy and non-surveillance

The framework prohibits:

- personal tracking;
- biometric collection;
- private-system inspection;
- augmentation inference;
- identity ranking;
- behavioral profiling;
- contributor scoring;
- social-credit systems;
- employment, housing, insurance, benefit, or service eligibility use;
- law-enforcement, immigration-enforcement, or punitive use;
- sale of contributor identity or behavioral data;
- use of civic participation as a condition of ordinary public access.

## Contribution record minimum

A contribution record may contain only:

- a non-identifying contribution reference;
- contribution domain and indicator;
- coarse place or grid-cell reference where necessary;
- bounded time range;
- method reference;
- evidence-quality class;
- uncertainty;
- declared public purpose;
- retention period;
- correction and withdrawal path.

It must not contain private system details, medical information, biometric
information, neural information, account credentials, persistent contributor
identity, or raw location history by default.

## Use limitation

Civic contribution records may be used only for the declared public purpose.

They may not be repurposed for enforcement, policing, immigration control,
fines, eligibility decisions, service denial, insurance pricing, employment
screening, housing screening, real-estate speculation, or behavioral
profiling.

## Maintenance and compensation

Volunteer participation must not replace properly funded public maintenance,
emergency response, environmental monitoring, accessibility services, or
essential infrastructure obligations.

Where an institution, business, or municipality materially benefits from
contribution-based data or labor, it should disclose funding, maintenance,
calibration, stewardship, and compensation arrangements.

## Correction, withdrawal, and appeal

Contributors may:

- correct an inaccurate record;
- withdraw future contribution consent;
- request retention and use explanation;
- report misuse;
- access an accessible human review path;
- use a non-digital grievance route where reasonably available.

Withdrawal affects future use and distribution where technically and legally
possible. Historical records should be handled through documented retention,
correction, and withdrawal-propagation procedures rather than silent deletion
or hidden reuse.

## Non-authority boundary

This framework does not create authority to:

- decide identity or status;
- verify private systems;
- operate infrastructure;
- dispatch emergency services;
- issue permits;
- enforce law;
- control physical systems;
- override community governance;
- determine eligibility for services or civic participation.

It creates a voluntary, privacy-preserving civic contribution pathway.
