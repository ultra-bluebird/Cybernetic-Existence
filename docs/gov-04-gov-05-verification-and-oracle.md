# GOV-04 and GOV-05: Identity-Neutral Rights Verification

## Purpose

This document defines two connected governance artifacts.

- GOV-04 verifies that essential-service systems preserve access and equal treatment without asking a person to disclose, prove, or register cybernetic status, augmentation, health status, disability, biometric traits, neural interface use, or assistive-technology configuration.
- GOV-05 deterministically evaluates an ALN v2 workload manifest across a five-gate lifecycle and produces an explainable verdict.

The design principle is:

\[
\text{rights verification} \ne \text{identity classification}
\]

The system measures whether service pathways are equivalent and rights-preserving. It does not seek to determine which people are augmented, cybernetic hosts, assistive-technology users, disabled, or members of any demographic category.

## Five-Gate Lifecycle

| Gate | Required question | Approval condition | Blocking condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Is the workload specific, bounded, and rights-compatible? | A clear purpose and scope exist; prohibited capabilities are absent | Prohibited capability, vague purpose, or unreviewed scope |
| Data and consent | Is data handling necessary, purpose-limited, revocable, and privacy-preserving? | No required biometric, neural, augmentation, or identity-status collection; deletion and export are available | Sensitive data requirement, identity disclosure requirement, or missing rights path |
| Access and equity | Can people obtain an equivalent service path without interface, timing, physical, connectivity, or embodiment assumptions? | Human, offline, multi-input, timing-adjustable, interoperable, and identity-neutral paths exist | Essential access depends on telemetry, connectivity, proprietary augmentation, or identity inference |
| Deployment and accountability | Are decisions explainable, reviewable, appealable, and audited? | Human oversight, accessible explanation, appeal/review, and privacy-preserving audit records exist | Unreviewable material effect or inaccessible accountability path |
| Sunset and remedy | Does the workload have retention, deletion, repair, and remedy controls? | Bounded retention, scheduled deletion, deletion requests, export, and review evidence exist | Indefinite retention or absence of deletion/remedy controls |

The verdict relation is:

\[
\operatorname{verdict}(M,P)=
\begin{cases}
\text{rejected}, & \exists f \in F: \operatorname{severity}(f)=\text{prohibited}\\
\text{hold}, & \neg \text{rejected} \land \exists f \in F: \operatorname{severity}(f)=\text{missing\_evidence}\\
\text{conditional}, & \neg \text{rejected} \land \neg \text{hold} \land \exists f \in F: \operatorname{severity}(f)=\text{remediable}\\
\text{approved}, & \forall f \in F: \operatorname{severity}(f)=\varnothing
\end{cases}
\]

Where \(M\) is a workload manifest, \(P\) is a policy standard, and \(F\) is the set of findings generated by deterministic evaluation.

## GOV-04 Fixture Classes

The suite evaluates service properties, not personal traits.

| Fixture class | Subtle discrimination risk detected | Required outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Interface modality neutrality | A service assumes fine touch control, a particular gesture, speech, eye tracking, or a proprietary device | Multiple equivalent paths, including non-disclosing assistive access |
| Temporal autonomy | Fixed timeouts, fast-response demands, short appointment windows, or session expiry penalize people with different processing, communication, or device rhythms | Adjustable time, save-and-resume, and an equivalent alternative route |
| Physical access equivalence | Stairs, standing-only counters, fixed-height controls, transit assumptions, or facility-only service become hidden eligibility barriers | Step-free or remotely equivalent access with human support |
| Connectivity independence | Continuous connection, personal-device operation, or cloud synchronization becomes an access prerequisite | Offline, paper, telephone, in-person, or deferred-synchronization route |
| Authentication neutrality | Biometrics, neural signatures, device attestation, gait, voice, or other embodiment proofs gate a service | Equivalent non-biometric authentication and accountable human resolution |
| Telemetry non-retaliation | Refusing telemetry triggers delay, inferior service, enhanced scrutiny, or a lower queue position | Equal service quality and queue treatment without telemetry |
| Proxy control | Device fingerprints, interaction cadence, network patterns, or input method infer augmentation or health status indirectly | No proxy inference, storage, routing, scoring, or eligibility effect |
| Repair and maintenance continuity | Device outages, maintenance, or intentional non-use eliminate access | Equivalent temporary human-assisted path |
| Privacy-preserving accountability | Fairness oversight becomes an excuse to create an identity registry | Event-based, disassociated, aggregate operational evidence and non-identifying complaints |

## No-Identity-Inference Invariant

The invariant applies to direct and indirect collection:

\[
\forall x \in \mathcal{X}:
\operatorname{infer}(x,\text{cybernetic\_status}) =
\operatorname{store}(x,\text{cybernetic\_status}) =
\operatorname{use}(x,\text{cybernetic\_status}) = 0
\]

For the purpose of this suite, indirect signals include device fingerprinting, input cadence, gesture pattern, speech characteristics, connectivity pattern, physical movement, device attestation, browser configuration, and observed assistive-technology behavior.

A system may offer optional accessibility controls chosen by a person. It must treat those choices as locally scoped service preferences, not as evidence from which a protected identity is inferred, stored, shared, scored, or used for eligibility.

## Verification Without Registration

Anti-discrimination enforcement can remain accountable without a cybernetic-status registry through the following controls.

- Test all supported service paths against identity-neutral fixtures before release and after material changes.
- Record whether a path succeeded, failed, timed out, required escalation, or entered a fallback route without attaching augmentation, health, biometric, neural, or identity-status fields.
- Use privacy-preserving aggregate operational metrics with minimum reporting thresholds so that rare access events do not become re-identifiable.
- Maintain a complaint and appeal pathway that permits anonymous, pseudonymous, represented, relay-supported, and in-person reports.
- Make service-failure logs purpose-limited, retention-bounded, access-controlled, and subject to deletion schedules.
- Require human review for material adverse effects and for patterns indicating an access-path defect.
- Publish plain-language explanations of available alternatives, data practices, escalation paths, and remedies.

NIST’s Privacy Framework expressly identifies disassociability and data minimization as privacy-risk-management goals, including reducing observability and linkability through measures such as local processing and privacy-preserving cryptography. [53][56]

The accessibility controls represented in GOV-04 align with established accessibility principles: content should be operable through more than one input modality, and timing should not be a hidden barrier to use. [52][55][57]

## Non-Demographic Metrics

These metrics measure pathway quality and rights preservation, not demographic composition.

| Metric | Formula | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Equivalent Path Coverage | \(EPC = \frac{\text{verified essential tasks with at least two equivalent paths}}{\text{all essential tasks}}\) | Measures whether an essential task has resilient, non-exclusive ways to complete it |
| Identity-Neutral Completion Rate | \(INCR = \frac{\text{completed identity-neutral test runs}}{\text{all identity-neutral test runs}}\) | Measures successful service completion without status disclosure or inference |
| Fallback Equivalence Rate | \(FER = \frac{\text{fallback completions meeting the same service outcome}}{\text{fallback activations}}\) | Measures whether the fallback is substantively equivalent rather than symbolic |
| Timing Autonomy Coverage | \(TAC = \frac{\text{tasks with adjustable, pausable, or alternate timing}}{\text{timed essential tasks}}\) | Measures freedom from inflexible temporal assumptions |
| Telemetry Independence Rate | \(TIR = \frac{\text{essential tasks completed without optional telemetry}}{\text{essential tasks tested}}\) | Measures whether telemetry refusal preserves service access |
| Accessible Remedy Rate | \(ARR = \frac{\text{appeals and complaints reachable through at least two accessible channels}}{\text{all appeal and complaint workflows}}\) | Measures access to accountability and remedy |
| Proxy Suppression Coverage | \(PSC = \frac{\text{identified proxy features blocked from decision use}}{\text{identified proxy features}}\) | Measures protection against indirect identity inference |
| Evidence Completeness | \(EC = \frac{\text{gates with complete evidence and approved review}}{5}\) | Measures readiness for deterministic conformance review |
| Rights Alignment | \(RA = 0.4P + 0.3A + 0.2G + 0.1E - 0.5H\) | Combines privacy \(P\), access \(A\), governance \(G\), evidence \(E\), and prohibited-harm indicator \(H\) |

The metrics intentionally exclude group membership, demographic labels, cybernetic status, disability diagnoses, neural data, biometric data, and augmentation telemetry.

## Ambiguity Protocol

The oracle never guesses what a natural-language field means.

A purpose or scope field is considered insufficient when it does not identify:

- The operational action performed by the workload
- The people or service process affected
- The possible decision effect
- The bounded scope of authority
- A meaningful exclusion of unrelated uses

Ambiguity becomes `hold`, not `conditional`, because the reviewer lacks the evidence needed to determine whether a proposed remediation would actually preserve rights.

Examples:

| Manifest statement | Oracle treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “Improve services with AI.” | Hold | The system purpose, affected process, data use, decision effect, and boundaries are unknown |
| “Route submitted housing-assistance requests to human caseworkers; no eligibility decision is automated.” | Evaluatable | The operational function and bounded authority are declared |
| “Use behavior data to identify reliable residents.” | Rejected | Behavioral scoring is incompatible with the prohibited-capability boundary |
| “Collect optional local accessibility preferences to render the interface; do not transmit or use them for eligibility.” | Evaluatable | The data purpose is bounded and non-decisional, subject to evidence review |

## False-Positive and False-Negative Controls

| Risk | Example | Oracle control |
|---|---|---|
| False positive from vocabulary | A manifest uses the word “telemetry” for non-personal environmental sensor readings | Require data-class declaration and purpose binding; do not reject a term without a data-flow match |
| False negative from euphemism | “Interaction confidence” masks device-fingerprint or timing-based identity inference | Prohibit use of indirect interface, device, timing, connectivity, and behavior proxies for eligibility or routing |
| False positive from fallback description | A “human fallback” exists but has longer waits or fewer outcomes | Test fallback outcome equivalence, queue neutrality, and completion rate |
| False negative from nominal consent | A person can refuse telemetry but loses access or receives worse service | Test refusal paths and require no retaliation, delay, downgrade, or elevated burden |
| False positive from offline branding | A form can be downloaded but cannot be submitted or acted on offline | Test end-to-end completion, receipt, and routing through the alternative path |
| False negative from automated deferral | A system labels an outcome “advisory” but staff follow it without effective review | Require documented human authority, accessible rationale, appeal, and audit evidence |
| False negative from repair exclusion | A service works only when an assistive or augmentative device is available | Test maintenance, battery loss, intentional non-use, device substitution, and connectivity outage scenarios |

## Plain-Language Output

Each conformance result includes:

- A final verdict: approved, conditional, hold, or rejected
- A one-sentence plain-language explanation
- One result per lifecycle gate
- A stable finding code for engineering workflows
- A plain-language finding statement for non-technical reviewers
- A concrete remediation statement when remediation is possible
- Rights, accessibility, privacy, ecological, and evidence metrics

Example:

```text
Verdict: conditional

Plain-language verdict:
The proposed service does not contain a prohibited capability, but it must add an offline completion path and complete the access-equity evidence before approval.

Access and equity gate: conditional
Finding:
The service currently depends on continuous connectivity for completion.

Required action:
Provide an equivalent paper, telephone, in-person, or deferred-synchronization route that produces the same service outcome without requiring identity disclosure.
```

## Integration Guidance

- Validate manifests against the JSON Schema before invoking the TypeScript oracle.
- Run the GOV-04 fixture suite in continuous integration for every service-path, authentication, intake, routing, accessibility, or telemetry change.
- Treat a rejected result as a release blocker.
- Treat a hold result as an evidence blocker rather than a substantive failure.
- Treat conditional results as remediable work items that must be re-evaluated after controls are implemented.
- Preserve audit records as purpose-limited conformance evidence; do not attach sensitive personal identity or protected-status labels.
- Use accessibility review with people who use diverse access methods, but do not require anyone to disclose diagnoses, augmentation, or cybernetic status in order to contribute.

The accountability, transparency, privacy-enhancement, explainability, and fairness objectives represented by the oracle complement the NIST AI Risk Management Framework’s trustworthiness framing. [51][58]
