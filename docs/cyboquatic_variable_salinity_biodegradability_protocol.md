# Variable-Salinity Biodegradability Evidence Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol defines a tiered evidence boundary for assessing whether a documented
Cyboquatic machinery material or releasable component has biodegradation potential
under controlled aquatic conditions relevant to a variable-salinity canal setting.

This protocol does not certify a complete machinery assembly, establish environmental
safety, grant a permit, authorize installation, or establish real-world biodegradation
rates without component-specific laboratory and field evidence.

## Unit of assessment

Each assessment MUST identify one material unit:

- polymer resin;
- coating;
- elastomer;
- lubricant;
- filter medium;
- sacrificial component;
- abrasion fragment;
- corrosion product;
- manufacturing residue; or
- leachate fraction.

A whole assembly MUST NOT be represented as one homogeneous biodegradable substance
unless its composition, accessible surface, release pathways, and test specimen are
demonstrably homogeneous.

## Required material record

Each material unit MUST retain:

- material identifier and supplier or formulation reference;
- mass and exposed surface area;
- expected use temperature range;
- expected salinity or conductivity range;
- expected hydraulic and ultraviolet exposure;
- intended service lifetime;
- known additives, colorants, coatings, and fillers;
- sample preparation method;
- laboratory chain of custody;
- selected assay and rationale;
- blank, reference material, and inhibition-control outcome.

## Tier 1: Standardized aerobic aquatic screening

For a documented soluble or dispersible fraction, perform an applicable OECD 301
method selected for the test substance properties.

Record:

- test method;
- inoculum source and conditioning;
- test concentration;
- dissolved organic carbon, oxygen uptake, or carbon dioxide endpoint;
- replicate count;
- blank and reference outcomes;
- temperature;
- pH;
- salinity or conductivity;
- duration;
- pass criterion and limitations.

A Tier 1 result is a controlled laboratory screening result. It MUST NOT be described
as a canal-deployment result.

## Tier 2: Polymeric aqueous assessment

For polymeric materials, perform a documented applicable aqueous polymer assessment
using oxygen-demand and/or carbon-dioxide-evolution endpoints.

The result MUST distinguish:

- physical fragmentation;
- mass loss;
- dissolved-organic-carbon change;
- oxygen consumption;
- carbon-dioxide evolution;
- microbial assimilation;
- additive release; and
- material integrity loss.

A visible or gravimetric change alone MUST NOT be treated as proof of biodegradation.

## Tier 3: Variable-salinity canal relevance

Conduct a controlled microcosm, mesocosm, or laboratory study with a documented
salinity schedule covering the declared canal operating envelope.

The study MUST include:

- representative water and, where relevant, sediment inoculum;
- a freshwater control;
- a high-salinity control;
- at least one variable-salinity cycle;
- dissolved oxygen, pH, temperature, and conductivity measurement;
- test material and blank controls;
- chemical analysis for released constituents where release is plausible;
- mass balance and uncertainty reporting;
- stop conditions for oxygen depletion, toxicity indication, uncontrolled release,
  or material failure.

The study MUST state whether it measures biodegradation potential, transformation,
  fragmentation, sorption, leaching, or another process.

## In-situ sensor boundary

In-situ sensors MAY provide continuous context for:

- temperature;
- electrical conductivity;
- dissolved oxygen;
- pH;
- oxidation-reduction potential;
- turbidity;
- water level;
- velocity or flow;
- sensor drift and calibration status.

In-situ sensors MUST NOT substitute for:

- standardized biodegradation test controls;
- laboratory DOC analysis;
- laboratory carbon-dioxide analysis;
- laboratory targeted PFAS analysis;
- laboratory BOD;
- laboratory TSS;
- laboratory CEC;
- chain-of-custody sample analysis; or
- environmental authorization.

## Claim language

Permitted before field commissioning:

- "The material was evaluated under the documented controlled assay conditions."
- "The component is designed for assessment under variable-salinity conditions."
- "Field sensors provide contextual water-quality and hydraulic telemetry."

Not permitted without additional evidence:

- "The machinery is biodegradable in Phoenix canals."
- "The component is environmentally safe."
- "The component has no ecological impact."
- "The system is approved for canal deployment."
- "Sensor telemetry proves biodegradation."

## Review gate

A material may advance from laboratory evidence to a proposed field evaluation only
after independent review of:

- material identity;
- assay applicability;
- salinity regime;
- leachate and additive risk;
- ecological exposure pathway;
- hydraulic containment;
- retrieval and maintenance plan;
- applicable permissions; and
- monitoring and stop-condition plan.
