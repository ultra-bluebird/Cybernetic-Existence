# CyberCongress Pilot Charter

## Status and Purpose

CyberCongress is a proposed voluntary review and coordination process for
CyberMind-related participation, privacy, continuity, representation, and
research-governance concerns.

This charter does not appoint a court, regulator, guardian, clinical authority,
or public registry. It does not determine consciousness, legal capacity,
personhood, identity, or the existence of a private integration.

The pilot is not operational merely because this document exists.

## Activation Requirements

Before receiving real confidential submissions, the operator must publish:

- Its accountable organizational and contact arrangements.
- The scope of the pilot and participating organizations.
- The commitments each organization has accepted.
- Staffed channels, service hours, and available accessibility support.
- Privacy, security, retention, correction, and deletion procedures.
- Funding, case capacity, and capacity-limit procedures.
- Independent review and appeal arrangements.
- Applicable participation terms reviewed for the operating jurisdiction.
- The pilot term, evaluation date, and orderly closure plan.

If these conditions are not established, use synthetic exercises only.

## Scope

The pilot may review:

- Whether a promised response or review occurred.
- Whether reasons were understandable and challengeable.
- Whether unnecessary sensitive information was requested.
- Whether a representative acted within a valid mandate.
- Whether a continuity or transition commitment was addressed.
- Whether temporary measures received required reassessment.
- Whether fiction, claims, observations, and findings were properly distinguished.
- Whether community participation was accessible and non-retaliatory.

Substantive legal, clinical, technical, and emergency determinations must be
referred to appropriately authorized processes where necessary.

## Permitted Functions

The pilot may:

- Receive concerns without requiring public identity disclosure.
- Request relevant information voluntarily.
- Facilitate discussion with willing parties.
- Review commitments within its published scope.
- Issue reasoned recommendations.
- Track accepted corrective actions and verification.
- Offer referrals with appropriate permission.
- Publish minimized learning after disclosure review.

It may not, under this charter alone:

- Compel evidence or participation.
- Access private accounts, integrations, devices, or neural information.
- Execute financial, technical, clinical, or security interventions.
- Order deletion, migration, confinement, or shutdown.
- Confer legal status or certify consciousness.
- Bind a nonparticipant.
- Require waiver of unrelated rights or remedies.

## Participation Safeguards

Participants retain direct access, correction, objection, and chosen assistance.

No person must disclose an implant model, neural traces, companion-system
inventory, diagnosis, or substrate classification for ordinary intake.

Anonymous and pseudonymous concerns may be considered, with return-channel and
verification limitations explained.

Silence is not consent, satisfaction, withdrawal, or permission to publish.

A mandate problem involving a proxy must not remove the person's direct route
to participate or choose different assistance.

CyberRank personal descriptions remain voluntary and private. CyberRank
governance duties concern access, minimal disclosure, correction, appeal, human
fallback, and review—not ranking or eligibility.

## Representation

Representation is based on documented, bounded mandates.

The adopted voting schedule must state allocations, quorum, selection,
replacement, and conflicts before a vote occurs.

Copy count, account count, compute ownership, and asserted substrate do not
automatically create voting weight.

A vote cannot authorize conduct outside the pilot's authority or override its
privacy, accessibility, preservation, or review safeguards.

## Review Roles and Conflicts

The operator must provide intake coordination, privacy stewardship, substantive
review, and independent appeal.

A person whose conduct or decision is challenged must not be the sole reviewer.

Material conflicts require reassignment or independent scrutiny.

Review capacity must be real and funded. Unavailable capacity is an institutional
limitation, not a deficiency of the complainant.

## Complaint Workflow

Receive → acknowledge → scope → review → reasons → corrective-action tracking
or explained closure → appeal.

Proposed pilot targets are:

- Human acknowledgement within 3 working days.
- Initial scope assessment within 5 working days.
- Substantive response or explained progress update within 15 working days.
- Further updates at least every 15 working days while unresolved.
- Appeal outcome or explained progress update within 20 working days.

Before launch, confirm that staffing supports these targets and publish the
working-time convention.

A missed target requires notice, a revised date, and capacity review.
It does not extinguish the complaint.

## Decisions and Closure

Every substantive outcome must identify:

- The issue and scope.
- Supported facts and unresolved claims.
- Relevant commitments.
- Reasons.
- Recommended or accepted action.
- Responsible role and verification method.
- Appeal route.

Administrative closure is not verified resolution.

A recommendation being delivered is not evidence that it was implemented.
No response from a participant is not evidence of satisfaction.

## Deadlock and Interim Protection

Deadlock does not create new powers.

The pilot may recommend a narrow interim measure, but implementation requires
separate appropriate authority.

Any proposed measure must state its purpose, evidence, effects on essential
capabilities, alternatives, responsible authority, review, expiry, and transition.

No automatic renewal is permitted.
Related and overlapping measures must be reviewed together.

## Information Handling

Keep contact information, confidential case material, procedural records, and
public summaries separated by purpose and access.

Do not place sensitive case content on a public ledger.

Retention must be purpose-specific and address indexes, replicas, backups,
derived records, and exports.

Public reports must undergo privacy and linkability review.

Feedback does not automatically authorize research, model training, quotation,
publication, or contact with another organization.

## Evaluation, Amendment, and Closure

Evaluate accessibility, reasons, appeal outcomes, verified corrective action,
recurrence, disclosure burden, and participant control.

Do not treat fewer complaints as proof of improvement.

Publish proposed material amendments for accessible review. Amendments must not
retroactively erase commitments or narrow an existing appeal without an
appropriate review process.

At the pilot's end, stop new intake according to published notice, explain
unresolved matters, offer appropriate transition or referral, and implement the
records-retention plan.

No ongoing service or perpetual institutional authority is promised by this
charter.
