# Hero Stewardship Framework

## Status

This framework defines voluntary, private, institutional recognition areas for documented public-interest stewardship contributions.

It is not a legal title, public registry, identity credential, medical classification, technical certification, social ranking, service-eligibility mechanism, wealth claim, authority over others, or measure of human worth.

The framework uses the term Hero as a private, voluntary institutional honorific. It recognizes a documented contribution or body of work, not an inherently superior person.

## Core Rule

Recognition must not require a person to disclose or prove:

- CyberRank, cybernetic-host, BCI, companion-system, integrated-technology, or assistive-technology status.
- Health, disability, neural, biometric, behavioral, communication, device, network, repair, or location information.
- Identity beyond what the recognizing institution lawfully and minimally requires for its own private process.
- External field labor, public exposure, fame, wealth, media coverage, or continuous productivity.

A person may choose private, pseudonymous, delayed, public, or no-publication recognition.

No explicit publication consent means recognition remains private.

## Recognition Areas

### Ecological Continuity Stewardship

This area recognizes documented public-interest work that reduces ecological risk, improves recovery capacity, preserves habitat or species-supporting knowledge, improves restoration readiness, or strengthens lawful community stewardship.

Examples may include reproducible restoration models, accessible conservation data, ecological-risk research, habitat planning tools, rights-preserving monitoring frameworks, and community-governed preservation artifacts.

The framework does not claim that a contributor individually prevented extinction unless credible evidence supports that specific statement.

### Food and Water Security Stewardship

This area recognizes documented contributions that improve food or water availability, equitable access, quality communication, resilience, community governance, or continuity during shocks.

Examples may include accessible food-assistance workflows, water-quality communication tools, drought-planning models, supply-chain resilience analysis, privacy-preserving distribution systems, and community-controlled water or food continuity frameworks.

The framework does not certify food safety, potable water, engineering adequacy, water rights, medical suitability, or supply guarantees.

### Knowledge and Cultural Continuity Stewardship

This area recognizes accessible preservation, translation, documentation, interoperability, provenance, archival, educational, and knowledge-access work.

Examples may include screen-reader accessible public-interest archives, offline educational packages, reproducible research corpora, plain-language technical guidance, interoperable records, and consent-respecting preservation designs.

The framework must not extract, publish, monetize, or claim ownership of private or community-held knowledge without meaningful authorization.

### Civic and Systemic Resilience Stewardship

This area recognizes work that strengthens essential-service continuity, accessible communication, correction, appeal, privacy, repair, interoperability, public accountability, environmental justice, or community self-determination.

Examples may include human-fallback systems, non-digital service routes, grievance workflows, privacy-minimizing civic-data design, repair standards, public-interest security work, and rights-preserving automation governance.

## Review Dimensions

A recognition review may consider:

- Public-benefit relevance.
- Serious-loss relevance.
- Credible contribution connection.
- Durability and reproducibility.
- Accessibility and community usability.
- Privacy, consent, and non-discrimination alignment.
- Attribution and governance quality.
- Methods, sources, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Evidence of adoption or impact when voluntarily available.

No numerical score may be published or used to rank people.

## Evidence Boundary

Permitted evidence may include:

- Versioned research notes.
- Reproducible code.
- Source lists.
- Models and simulations.
- Policy, schema, and documentation artifacts.
- Accessible educational materials.
- Voluntary review records.
- Authorized pilot or adoption records.
- Privacy-preserving aggregate outcome evidence.
- Bounded causal narratives distinguishing observed outcome, plausible effect, and unresolved uncertainty.

Recognition evidence must not require invasive verification.

## Recognition Modes

A recipient may choose:

- Private recognition.
- Pseudonymous recognition.
- Delayed recognition.
- Public recognition.
- No-publication recognition.

The institution must not publish, transfer, sell, or use recognition data beyond the selected mode.

## Hero at Rest

A recognized contribution remains recognized when a recipient rests, retires, loses connectivity, changes focus, declines publicity, uses an alternate communication method, or stops voluntary activity.

Rest is not demotion.

A recognition may be reconsidered only for a material problem directly tied to the recognized contribution, such as material fabrication, substantial authorship misrepresentation, consent violation, or serious rights-violating conduct directly connected to the work.

The recognizing institution must provide accessible notice, correction, review, and appeal before materially changing a recognition record.

## Limitation

This framework is a private governance and recognition policy.

It does not determine legal status, institutional status, clinical status, technical status, public credibility, service eligibility, identity, or human worth.
