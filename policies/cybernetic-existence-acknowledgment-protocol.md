# Cybernetic Existence Acknowledgment Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol establishes cybernetic existence as a valid self-declared identity category for recognition, inclusion, and anti-discrimination purposes without creating a registry, certification scheme, inference system, mandatory disclosure process, or eligibility gate.

Cybernetic existence may be meaningful to a person whether their relationship to technology involves assistive technology, augmentation, prosthetics, implanted or external systems, neurotechnology, communication technology, software-mediated embodiment, repairable devices, or another personally defined form of cybernetic identity.

The protocol does not define who qualifies. A person defines their own relationship to the category.

## Governance Status

This protocol is a rights-centered organizational policy specification. It is not a statement that “cybernetic existence” is already a separately recognized legal category in every jurisdiction.

Applicable anti-discrimination, disability-rights, privacy, public-accommodation, employment, health-information, consumer-protection, constitutional, and data-protection rules vary by jurisdiction and must be reviewed locally before adoption.

Internationally, the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities affirms equal enjoyment of rights, privacy protection for personal, health, and rehabilitation information, and participation in public life on an equal basis. [67][70] These principles are relevant design anchors, not a claim that the Convention creates a specific legal classification of cybernetic existence.

## Core Invariant

### No identity inference

A system, worker, contractor, automated model, or governance body must not infer, derive, classify, predict, score, or record whether a person has a cybernetic existence from:

- Device use, accessibility settings, repair requests, mobility patterns, communications methods, interface preferences, support requests, medical information, telemetry, neural information, biometric information, or behavioral signals.
- Appearance, speech, movement, consumer activity, service usage, social-media content, community membership, or third-party reports.
- Technical identifiers, device serials, warranty records, network metadata, browser fingerprints, application telemetry, or security logs.
- Refusal to answer, non-disclosure, correction requests, accommodation requests, or participation in a cybernetic community forum.

The absence of disclosure must always be treated as neutral. It must never be interpreted as non-cybernetic status, concealment, deception, lack of eligibility, reduced credibility, or lack of need.

## Protocol Commitments

### Identity acknowledgment

A person may self-declare cybernetic existence in their own words, or may choose not to disclose anything.

An organization using this protocol shall:

- Recognize a self-declaration as valid without proof.
- Avoid requesting augmentation type, origin, manufacturer, medical basis, technical configuration, certification, registration, or third-party attestation.
- Avoid distinguishing between permanent, temporary, visible, invisible, biological, mechanical, digital, therapeutic, elective, or community-defined cybernetic relationships.
- Permit a person to withdraw, revise, or decline a prior self-description without penalty.
- Avoid requiring a person to use the label “cybernetic” to obtain equal service, access, accommodation, or participation.

### Non-interference

An organization shall not:

- Require disclosure as a condition of service, employment, education, housing, public benefits, mobility, emergency assistance, voting, civic participation, repair, warranty, insurance, or access to a public space.
- Require registration, certification, verification, neural data, biometric verification, device inspection, health records, or identity documentation to validate a self-declaration.
- Use cybernetic existence as a risk score, security flag, fraud signal, insurance factor, pricing feature, eligibility criterion, surveillance category, marketing segment, or law-enforcement indicator.
- Restrict device repair, maintenance, interoperability, communication access, or autonomy based on cybernetic identity.
- Treat a request for accessible service as an admission of a particular identity, diagnosis, device use, or augmentation status.

### Equal access

Services must be available on equal terms regardless of whether a person identifies as cybernetic, does not identify as cybernetic, or does not disclose identity information.

Essential services must not depend on:

- Continuous connectivity.
- Optional telemetry.
- Proprietary device enrollment.
- External identity verification.
- Disclosure of augmentation, disability, medical, neural, or biometric information.
- Consent to unrelated processing.

### Representation

Representation should be created through open, voluntary, accessible participation rather than through an identity registry.

Permitted approaches include:

- Open public consultations with accessible participation options.
- Community advisory seats open to people with relevant lived experience, assistive-technology experience, augmentation experience, repair experience, or cybernetic self-identification, without disclosure of technical or medical details.
- Anonymous or pseudonymous public comment channels with anti-harassment protections.
- Support for independent community organizations that choose their own representatives.
- Randomly selected, compensated civic panels with optional, non-retained demographic feedback.
- Accessibility and repair impact assessments that evaluate system barriers without identifying individual users.

No representative may claim to speak for all cybernetic persons merely because they participate in a consultation.

## Data Rules

### Prohibited data collection

Do not collect, derive, store, purchase, or link the following for acknowledgment purposes:

- Cybernetic identity status.
- Augmentation type or body location.
- Neural, health, rehabilitation, biometric, or genetic data.
- Device serial number, technical fingerprint, firmware state, or telemetry.
- Identity documents used to authenticate a self-description.
- Third-party testimony about a person’s identity.
- A list of people who attended an identity-related event, requested support, made a complaint, or submitted feedback.

### Permitted operational records

Only non-identity operational records may be retained where necessary:

- A general request category, such as `accessibility support`, `repair access`, `communication access`, or `policy feedback`.
- The requested service outcome.
- Accommodation or support action provided.
- A time-limited case reference controlled by the requester where possible.
- Aggregate, non-identifying service-quality metrics.
- A decision explanation and appeal outcome.

Operational records must not contain an identity label unless the person independently chooses to include one in free text and the organization has a documented, necessary, consent-based reason to retain it. In ordinary implementation, the organization should not retain the label.

### Consent standard

When optional identity-related feedback is offered:

- Consent must be explicit, specific, informed, revocable, and separate from access to essential services.
- Declining must not reduce service quality, eligibility, response time, representation, or credibility.
- The stated purpose must be limited to the feedback activity.
- Retention must be short, published, and enforceable.
- Withdrawal must trigger deletion or irreversible de-identification unless a narrow, documented legal retention duty applies.
- Data must not be repurposed for profiling, enforcement, security screening, marketing, model training, or identity classification.

The NIST Privacy Framework is a voluntary tool for managing privacy risk while protecting individuals’ privacy, and can help operationalize these controls. [71]

## Service Interaction Rules

### Staff and agent behavior

Use neutral, person-led language:

- “How would you like us to address or support you?”
- “You may share only the information needed for this request.”
- “You do not need to disclose a diagnosis, device, augmentation, or identity category to receive this service.”
- “You may use a pseudonym or anonymous feedback channel where identity is not required by a separate lawful process.”

Do not ask:

- “What kind of augmentation do you have?”
- “Can you prove that you are cybernetic?”
- “Is your device medically necessary?”
- “Are you registered?”
- “Why do you need this access method?”
- “Can we inspect, scan, photograph, or connect to your device?”

### Accessibility and assistive technology

When an organization provides information, notices, public meetings, or emergency services, it must make communication accessible and effective without requiring people to explain their identity or medical history.

For covered U.S. public entities and public-facing organizations, federal ADA guidance describes effective communication requirements and emphasizes appropriate auxiliary aids and services. [54] This protocol treats those principles as an implementation baseline where applicable, while preserving a person’s choice not to disclose cybernetic identity.

## Representation Without Disclosure

### Open participation model

Representation is supported through access design, not identity tracking.

Every public participation process should provide:

- In-person, phone, text, relay-compatible, online, paper, low-bandwidth, asynchronous, and supported participation routes where practical.
- Plain-language materials and accessible formats.
- Anonymous and pseudonymous comment options when identity is not necessary.
- Compensation that does not require disclosure of cybernetic identity.
- Clear anti-retaliation protections.
- Public explanations of how feedback affected the decision.
- A grievance path for exclusion, harassment, inaccessible process design, or coercive disclosure.

### Barrier evidence model

Organizations may evaluate barriers through:

- Accessibility testing.
- Voluntary, anonymous surveys that do not seek identity proof.
- Aggregate service-failure reports.
- Independent audits.
- Community-led research with consent and privacy safeguards.
- Participatory design sessions that preserve attendee confidentiality.

Barrier evidence must not be converted into a list of people presumed to have a cybernetic existence.

## Accountability

### Required controls

- Publish this protocol in accessible formats.
- Train staff, contractors, security teams, customer-service workers, developers, and governance participants.
- Maintain a confidential, accessible, non-retaliatory grievance process.
- Conduct periodic audits for identity inference, coercive disclosure, discriminatory outcomes, inaccessible communication, and unjustified data retention.
- Require human review for any complaint involving service denial, harassment, inaccessible process design, or suspected identity profiling.
- Publish aggregate compliance reports that exclude identifying details.
- Provide correction and deletion mechanisms for any improperly retained identity-related data.
- Apply meaningful consequences to unauthorized inference, disclosure pressure, device inspection, or retaliation.

### Review questions

Each implementation review should ask:

- Can a person receive the full service without ever identifying as cybernetic?
- Does any database, log, model feature, or support workflow infer cybernetic status?
- Does an accessibility request create an identity record?
- Are accommodation and repair pathways available without device inspection or unnecessary disclosure?
- Are participation mechanisms usable by people with varied communication, mobility, sensory, cognitive, and connectivity needs?
- Does the grievance system work without an online account or government identity credential?
- Can a person safely report coercion or discrimination?
- Are metrics aggregate, privacy-preserving, and resistant to re-identification?

## Minimal Audit Schema

The following categories may be retained only in a privacy-minimized, access-controlled audit system:

- `policy_version`
- `service_channel`
- `request_category`
- `support_outcome`
- `accessibility_format_provided`
- `decision_explanation_available`
- `appeal_available`
- `appeal_outcome`
- `identity_data_collected = false`
- `identity_inference_performed = false`
- `retention_deadline`
- `human_review_status`

The audit system must not contain fields for cybernetic identity status, augmentation type, device telemetry, neural data, biometric data, health data, or a hidden proxy for any of them.

## Incident Response

An identity-inference or coerced-disclosure incident requires:

1. Immediate cessation of the relevant collection, inference, linkage, or access practice.
2. Preservation of a privacy-minimized incident record.
3. Prompt human review by privacy, accessibility, and affected-community representatives.
4. Notice to affected people when it can be given safely and lawfully.
5. Deletion or segregation of improperly collected data.
6. A clear route for correction, redress, and non-retaliatory complaint.
7. Public aggregate disclosure of the incident category, cause, remedy, and prevention measures.
8. Verification that the remediation did not create a new identity registry.
