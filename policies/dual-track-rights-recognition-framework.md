# Dual-Track Rights and Recognition Framework

## Status

This framework is a research, advocacy, and governance document.

It does not create legal status, a protected class, a fundamental right, a medical classification, technical certification, public registry, eligibility category, or automated legal determination.

The terms CyberRank, cybernetic host, cybernetic-existence, companion system, and continuity are used as private and voluntary governance vocabulary unless and until a competent legal authority adopts a different meaning in a specific jurisdiction.

## Purpose

The framework supports two concurrent forms of work:

1. Immediate remedy work for concrete harms under applicable existing law, contract, regulation, organizational policy, and accessibility commitments.
2. Long-term recognition work evaluating whether additional legal, regulatory, institutional, or common-law protections should be proposed.

The two tracks are complementary.

Immediate remedy work does not concede that current law is complete.

Recognition work does not delay accessible service, privacy protection, correction, appeal, or other available remedies.

## Conditional Legal Description

The statement that a jurisdiction does not currently recognize a proposed category is a description of present doctrine.

It is not:

- A forecast that recognition cannot occur.
- A conclusion that recognition is unwarranted.
- A denial of a person's dignity or voluntary self-description.
- A requirement that a person prove private technology, health, neural, biometric, medical, or companion-system information.
- A justification for unequal service access.

Every legal analysis must identify:

- The jurisdiction.
- The date of analysis.
- The covered actor or institution.
- The service or legal context.
- The asserted legal source.
- The factual assumptions.
- The requested remedy.
- The limits and uncertainty of the analysis.

## Track One: Immediate Remedy

Track One addresses concrete, present harms.

Possible harm categories include:

- Compelled or unnecessary disclosure.
- Inaccessible digital-only service pathways.
- Failure to provide effective communication or human assistance.
- Data overcollection, retention, transfer, sale, or insecurity.
- Differential treatment or retaliation.
- Loss of assistive access.
- Arbitrary interruption of an essential service.
- Lack of correction, explanation, review, or appeal.
- Coerced participation, telemetry, account creation, or device inspection.
- Unconsented publication or retention.
- Cross-platform loss of accessibility or continuity.

The list is representative and non-exhaustive.

Track One must map a claim to a specific source of authority that may include:

- Constitution or administrative law.
- Statute or regulation.
- Case law.
- Contract or consumer-protection rule.
- Accessibility obligation.
- Data-protection or security requirement.
- Organizational policy.
- International human-rights instrument where applicable.

Track One requests concrete and proportionate remedies, including:

- Human-assisted access.
- Non-digital or low-technology access.
- Effective communication.
- Accessible service-status information.
- Data access, correction, deletion review, or retention reduction.
- Privacy and accessibility review.
- Human review and reasoned decision.
- Correction and appeal.
- Material-change review.
- Non-retaliation safeguards.

## Track Two: Recognition Development

Track Two develops future-facing proposals.

Possible activities include:

- Comparative legal research.
- Documentation of recurring barriers and harms using privacy-preserving aggregate evidence.
- Rights impact assessments.
- Model legislation or regulation.
- Administrative comments.
- Public-interest scholarship.
- Coalition development.
- Institutional policy proposals.
- Carefully bounded strategic litigation analysis.
- International and regional human-rights analysis.

Track Two must distinguish:

- Existing legal obligation.
- Persuasive authority.
- Policy proposal.
- Research hypothesis.
- Personal self-description.
- Prediction.

No Track Two activity may require a person to disclose or validate private cybernetic, technical, neural, medical, biometric, health, device, network, or companion-system information.

## Vocabulary Layers

### Personal Layer

A person may voluntarily self-describe their identity, communication preferences, continuity needs, privacy boundary, or non-interference concerns.

A self-description does not require proof in order to receive dignity, accessible communication, equal service pathways, privacy, correction, or appeal.

### Governance Layer

Services and institutions should implement:

- Non-inference.
- Data minimization.
- Human-assisted access.
- Non-digital alternatives where relevant.
- Neutral access under uncertainty.
- Privacy-preserving incident intake.
- Correction and appeal.
- Rights-delta review for material changes.
- Accessible public status information.
- Outage fallback.

### Legal Layer

Legal claims depend on jurisdiction-specific law, facts, coverage, procedure, and remedy.

No automated system may determine the legal merits, legal status, or legal entitlement of a person.

### Advocacy Layer

Advocacy may propose recognition, new protections, legal reform, and public terminology.

Advocacy proposals must be clearly labeled as proposals rather than current law.

## Non-Concession Rule

The absence of a current legal category does not mean that recognition is unwarranted.

The use of existing legal protections does not waive, abandon, or diminish the ability to advocate for broader protections.

The use of voluntary private vocabulary does not reduce any person's rights to dignity, privacy, accessibility, non-discrimination, correction, appeal, or equal service access.

## Research Integrity Rules

All legal research records must:

- State jurisdiction and date.
- Identify source authority and authority level.
- Separate legal fact from policy proposal.
- Identify factual assumptions and unresolved questions.
- Avoid unsupported predictions.
- Avoid medicalization and pathologization.
- Avoid public status creation, ranking, or registry construction.
- Avoid compelled disclosure.
- Use privacy-preserving examples and synthetic facts where practical.
- Require qualified human legal review before reliance in a live dispute.

## Governance Metrics

Permitted metrics measure provider duties, not people:

- Human-assisted pathway coverage.
- Non-digital pathway coverage.
- Accessibility remediation time.
- Privacy review completion.
- Material-change review completion.
- Correction and appeal availability.
- Outage fallback test success.
- Data-minimization compliance.
- Number of policy changes corrected after rights review.

Prohibited metrics include:

- Number of cybernetic hosts.
- CyberRank prevalence.
- Private-status verification rate.
- Neural, biometric, health, device, or telemetry collection rate.
- Individual continuity score.
- Service eligibility or credibility score.
- Public identity or status registry.

## Limitation

This framework is not legal advice.

It does not determine legal status, clinical status, technical status, institutional recognition, public credibility, eligibility, or the outcome of a legal claim.
