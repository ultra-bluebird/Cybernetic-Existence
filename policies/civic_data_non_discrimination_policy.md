# Civic Data Non-Discrimination and Access-Equivalence Policy

## Purpose

This policy governs data related to public cooling access, shade, rest, water,
accessibility preferences, route preferences, low-sensory needs, communication
preferences, and private self-described terminology.

The policy ensures that access-oriented information is used only to provide,
improve, evaluate, or explain accessible public services. It must not be used
for profiling, credit, insurance, employment, housing, policing, surveillance,
immigration enforcement, eligibility ranking, resource denial, or punitive
decision-making.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- Municipal departments and publicly funded service providers.
- Contractors, vendors, consultants, researchers, and software providers.
- Public cooling centers, water-access programs, shade infrastructure programs,
  transit-access programs, and heat-response services.
- Civic dashboards, routing systems, public registries, prediction models,
  accessibility workflows, mobile applications, websites, kiosks, and call
  centers.
- Any dataset containing a request for shade, rest, cooling, water, language
  access, communication support, low-sensory conditions, or an accessibility
  adjustment.

## Core rule

Accessibility and heat-support preferences are service-access information.

They are not evidence of identity, health, disability, capability, credibility,
income, residency, citizenship, employment status, insurance status, public
safety risk, creditworthiness, or eligibility for unrelated programs.

Private self-description, including terms a person uses for their identity,
communication context, continuity needs, companion context, or access
preferences, must be handled respectfully. It must not be converted into a
public credential, diagnosis, certification, technical inventory, registry
status, ranking category, surveillance marker, or eligibility criterion.

## Permitted uses

Information may be used only when necessary for one or more of these purposes:

- Providing shade, cooling, rest, water, communication, or accessibility access.
- Showing publicly available cooling and heat-support locations.
- Improving accessibility of a civic service.
- Measuring aggregate service availability, wait time, access barriers, and
  infrastructure gaps.
- Conducting approved, privacy-preserving research on aggregate program quality.
- Responding to a correction, complaint, appeal, or accessibility barrier.
- Maintaining non-identifying audit records required for accountability.

## Prohibited uses

The following uses are prohibited:

- Credit scoring, insurance scoring, employment screening, tenant screening, or
  consumer profiling.
- Surveillance, persistent location tracking, device tracking, behavioral
  profiling, or inference of a person's routine, associations, or travel.
- Law-enforcement targeting, immigration enforcement, predictive policing, or
  punitive action.
- Determining eligibility for unrelated programs, public benefits, housing,
  transportation access, school services, health coverage, or civic rights.
- Ranking individuals, households, neighborhoods, or communities based on
  shade-seeking, cooling requests, low-sensory preferences, accessibility
  preferences, or private terminology.
- Inferring identity, race, color, national origin, disability, health,
  diagnosis, augmentation, neural status, credibility, competence, income,
  housing status, citizenship, or institutional affiliation.
- Requiring a person to disclose medical records, diagnostic information,
  biometric information, device information, private terminology, technical
  inventory, account credentials, or precise location as a condition of
  receiving basic heat-support or accessibility information.
- Reusing data for model training, advertising, analytics, or a new purpose
  without a separately stated purpose, human review, and explicit revocable
  consent where consent is the applicable basis.

## Minimum necessary collection

Collect only the minimum information needed for the stated service purpose.

For a basic request for shade, rest, cooling, water, or communication access,
the default required information is:

- Requested service category.
- Generalized service area only when necessary to identify a public facility.
- Request time window when needed for availability.
- An optional non-identifying return method if the person chooses follow-up.

Do not require identity, account creation, home address, medical information,
device identifiers, biometrics, private terminology, or precise route history
for basic public-service access.

## Purpose limitation and retention

Each data element must have a documented:

- Service purpose.
- Collection method.
- Access boundary.
- Retention period.
- Deletion method.
- Correction path.
- Appeal path.
- Human-review owner.

Content-bearing requests must be deleted when their stated purpose expires,
subject only to a documented and reviewed retention exception. A minimum,
non-identifying accountability record may be retained separately when needed to
show that the request, response, correction, or appeal process occurred.

## Access equivalence

Every supported interaction path must provide equivalent essential functions:

- Request service or information.
- Receive a plain-language explanation.
- Obtain an opaque receipt when a request is recorded.
- Correct inaccurate information.
- Request human assistance.
- Request review or appeal.
- Save and return later without a rigid time limit.

The following essential conditions must pass:

- Keyboard-operable flow.
- Semantic screen-reader compatibility.
- Plain-language availability.
- No unnecessary time pressure.
- Human-assisted path.
- Privacy-minimized path.
- No identity or medical disclosure requirement.

A composite score cannot compensate for failure of an essential access condition.

## Human oversight and correction

No automated system may make a final adverse decision about a person based on
heat-support, route, shade, rest, water, sensory, accessibility, or private
self-description information.

Any material model output, data-quality concern, access barrier, correction,
or appeal must have:

- A plain-language explanation.
- A human-review path.
- An independent-review option when appropriate.
- An append-only correction record.
- A non-retaliation safeguard.
- A material-change review when the model, policy, retention rule, or access
  pathway changes.

## Aggregate research and dashboards

Public dashboards may use aggregate, privacy-preserving information only.

Before publication, each metric must document:

- Purpose.
- Numerator and denominator.
- Data period.
- Source and version.
- Geographic generalization level.
- Minimum group size.
- Small-cell suppression rule.
- Privacy accounting method where applicable.
- Uncertainty or missing-data status.
- Review status.
- Correction route.

Dashboards must not expose individual requests, private preferences, personal
records, route histories, visitor logs, medical information, device data, or
private self-description.

## Legal and policy alignment

Programs receiving federal financial assistance must comply with applicable
civil-rights requirements. Title VI of the Civil Rights Act prohibits
discrimination on the basis of race, color, and national origin in programs and
activities receiving federal financial assistance.

This policy is designed to support equal access, privacy minimization,
non-discrimination, accessibility, and accountable public-service delivery. It
does not replace legal advice, statutory duties, procurement rules, disability
rights obligations, public-records duties, or jurisdiction-specific privacy law.

## Enforcement

A proposed collection, model feature, data transfer, dashboard field, contract
term, or workflow is blocked when it would:

- Create prohibited profiling, surveillance, credit, insurance, employment,
  housing, policing, immigration, eligibility, ranking, allocation, enforcement,
  or denial use.
- Require excessive disclosure for basic public cooling or accessibility access.
- Remove correction, appeal, human assistance, or accessibility pathways.
- Collect or retain data without stated purpose and retention treatment.
- Convert a private preference or self-description into a public classification.
- Release an aggregate that fails minimum-group-size, small-cell suppression,
  privacy, or uncertainty requirements.

## Review schedule

- Review this policy before each material system change.
- Review annually with accessibility, privacy, civil-rights, community, and
  operational stakeholders.
- Review after a data incident, access-equivalence failure, correction-pattern
  increase, model-calibration failure, or material public complaint.
- Preserve append-only review, correction, and revision records.
