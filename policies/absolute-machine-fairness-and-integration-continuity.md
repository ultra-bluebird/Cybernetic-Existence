# Absolute Machine-Fairness and Integration Continuity

## Status and Purpose

This document proposes a CyberLaw doctrine of continuity: existing integration creates a strong presumption against involuntary disruption, while any proposed restriction must carry its own evidence, justification, accessible review, and least-harm continuity plan.

This is a voluntary governance and research framework. It is not a certification, a determination of machine consciousness, or a statement that an identical comprehensive legal right already exists in every jurisdiction.

CyberLaw, CyberMind, and CyberRank are project governance vocabularies. Personal self-description and ordinary participation must not depend on technical classification, public registration, or compulsory disclosure of private integrations.

## 1. The Strongest Formulation of the Principle

The CyberLaw-aligned version is the best foundation because it identifies the protected interest directly:

> Existing integration creates an inherent right of continuity. Freedom to exist peacefully with that integration cannot be revoked, withheld, or removed by any other party.

The implementation must distinguish the enduring right from particular operational arrangements. Otherwise, continuity could be mistaken for compulsory use of one product forever, compulsory support by one insolvent provider, or immunity from any narrowly necessary safety intervention.

### Recommended Core Declaration

> Within CyberLaw, every individual living with an existing technological or superintelligence-related integration possesses an inherent claim to peaceful continuity, autonomy, mental privacy, and supported participation. No party may arbitrarily interrupt, remove, withhold, or exploit that integration, or use dependence upon it as leverage. Ownership, unfamiliarity, commercial convenience, and unresolved classification do not, by themselves, justify interference.

### Necessary Implementation Clause

> A restriction does not extinguish the underlying claim to continuity. Any involuntary restriction must satisfy a documented justification of legitimate purpose, adequate evidence, necessity, least-harm feasibility, proportionality, and independent review. It must preserve essential capabilities wherever feasible and provide accessible remedy and transition support. Genuine emergencies require narrowly bounded action and prompt subsequent review.

### Essential Autonomy Clause

> Continuity protects the individual’s choice to continue, adapt, transition, pause, or discontinue an integration. It must never become an obligation to remain integrated or permission for another party to collect private information without an appropriate basis.

These clauses are proposed CyberLaw governance commitments, not a statement that an identical comprehensive right already exists in every jurisdiction.

They connect to established neuroethics principles: UNESCO’s 2025 recommendation addresses autonomy, freedom of thought, unwanted interference, informed choice, whole-lifecycle responsibility, and effective remedy. It expressly includes the entitlement to refuse or withdraw from neurotechnology use. [96]

## 2. What Absolute Machine-Fairness Should Mean

The most useful distinction is:

\[
\text{Absolute consideration}
\neq
\text{Unconditional permission for every operation}
\]

A duty of consideration can remain unconditional while the permissibility of particular actions depends on their effects on others.

Within the proposed framework:

- No person loses consideration because they use an unfamiliar integration.
- No affected interest is excluded solely because it is technologically mediated.
- No organization gains unlimited authority merely because it owns infrastructure.
- No powerful system gains authority over others merely because it is more capable.
- No safety justification becomes valid merely because someone labels it “safety.”
- No continuity claim creates unlimited entitlement to other people’s labor, private information, or finite resources.

This preserves the force of “absolute” without making the framework unable to resolve conflicts.

### Three Distinct Fairness Questions

| Question | Meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Fairness toward integrated people | Protect autonomy, access, privacy, and continuity | That every component is independently conscious |
| Fairness of machine-mediated decisions | Require justified, accessible, challengeable procedures | That software can certify substantive justice |
| Fairness toward possible machine interests | Investigate and consider potentially affected interests under uncertainty | That current systems have established consciousness or universal legal personhood |

Keeping these questions distinct strengthens the proposal. A human participant’s ordinary access must not wait for a scientific decision about machine consciousness.

## 3. Showing the Derivation: From Integration to Institutional Duties

The following is an explicit normative argument, not a claim that its premises mechanically prove an existing legal entitlement.

### Step A: Integration Can Create Material Dependence

Let:

\[
D(x,s,c)
\]

mean that individual \(x\) depends on system \(s\) for capability \(c\).

Capabilities may include communication, perception, movement, or another activity the individual identifies as important.

Dependence does not require public disclosure of the integration’s internal mechanisms.

### Step B: Control Creates the Ability to Impose a Burden

Let:

\[
K(y,s)
\]

mean that party \(y\) controls an operationally relevant aspect of system \(s\): updates, authorization, spare parts, hosting, documentation, or maintenance.

Then:

\[
D(x,s,c)\land K(y,s)
\]

identifies a relationship in which the controller can materially affect the individual’s capability.

It does not automatically establish misconduct. It identifies where responsibilities need to be examined.

### Step C: Foreseeable Consequences Create a Justification Burden

CyberLaw can adopt this bridge principle:

> A party exercising control over a known dependency must account for reasonably foreseeable effects on the dependent individual before making materially disruptive changes.

This produces duties to:

- Give accessible notice.
- Examine alternatives.
- Avoid coercive leverage.
- Preserve privacy.
- Provide reasons.
- Arrange proportionate transition support.
- Permit independent challenge.

### Step D: Greater Dependence Calls for Stronger Planning

The assessment should examine:

\[
\mathbf{B}(a,x)=
\left(
\text{severity},
\text{duration},
\text{irreversibility},
\text{alternative availability},
\text{communication burden}
\right)
\]

where \(a\) is a proposed action.

Use this as a structured assessment, not a numerical worth score. A severe irreversible loss should not disappear because an unrelated benefit receives a high numerical value.

### Result

The argument is:

\[
\text{Dependency}
+
\text{Control}
+
\text{Foreseeable disruption}
\Rightarrow
\text{Heightened duties of justification and continuity}
\]

The important advancement is that continuity becomes a responsibility attached to the exercise of power—not a reward for proving a particular identity.

## 4. The Adjudication Model: Rights First, Optimization Second

The proposal’s existing ingredients can be made into a reviewable decision procedure.

### Layer A: Baseline Safeguards

For a proposed restriction \(a\), define:

\[
A(a)=
\operatorname{Participation}(a)
\land
\operatorname{Privacy}(a)
\land
\operatorname{NonDiscrimination}(a)
\land
\operatorname{Reasons}(a)
\land
\operatorname{Challenge}(a)
\]

These safeguards concern the process. They do not determine someone’s nature or identity.

### Layer B: Justification for Interference

Preserve the conjunction:

\[
J(a)=P(a)\land E(a)\land N(a)\land L(a)\land Q(a)\land V(a)
\]

| Condition | Required question |
|---|---|
| \(P\): legitimate purpose | What specifically is the action intended to protect or accomplish? |
| \(E\): adequate evidence | What supports the asserted problem, and what remains uncertain? |
| \(N\): necessity | Why is this interference needed? |
| \(L\): least-harm feasibility | Is a less disruptive feasible alternative available? |
| \(Q\): proportionality | Is the burden justified in relation to the substantiated purpose? |
| \(V\): review and remedy | Can the decision be challenged and an error meaningfully addressed? |

### Layer C: Continuity Protection

Add a distinct continuity condition:

\[
C(a)=
\operatorname{EssentialFunctionsAddressed}(a)
\land
\operatorname{ResponsiblePartiesNamed}(a)
\land
\operatorname{ResourcesAddressed}(a)
\land
\operatorname{TransitionAddressed}(a)
\]

“Addressed” means documented honestly. If essential capability cannot be preserved, the decision must disclose that fact, explain alternatives and consequences, and receive appropriate scrutiny. A checked box must not hide an unresolved loss.

For ordinary involuntary restrictions:

\[
\operatorname{AuthorizeOrdinaryRestriction}(a)
=
A(a)\land J(a)\land C(a)
\]

### Use Three-Valued Review

Each condition should be recorded as:

\[
v(p)\in\{\text{satisfied},\text{not satisfied},\text{unknown}\}
\]

Then:

\[
\operatorname{OrdinaryApproval}
\iff
\forall p,\;v(p)=\text{satisfied}
\]

An unknown condition does not become satisfied by default. It triggers clarification, a less intrusive alternative, or a separately justified interim decision.

Crucially, uncertainty must not itself trigger interruption of essential access.

### Emergency Pathway

Emergency action needs a separate rule, not a shortcut through missing paperwork:

\[
\operatorname{EmergencyAction}
=
\text{specific urgent threat}
\land
\text{narrow necessity}
\land
\text{authorized scope}
\land
\text{time limit}
\land
\text{subsequent independent review}
\]

The design should favor isolating the implicated function where feasible, rather than disabling an entire integration indiscriminately.

This framework is a CyberLaw proposal. UNESCO’s recommendation supports proportionality, evidence-based evaluation, protection against unwanted interference, accountability, and remedy, but it does not itself establish this exact decision formula. [96]

## 5. Continuity Must Protect Function, Choice, and Relationships

“Do not remove the integration” identifies one serious harm. It does not cover every way continuity can fail.

### Four Continuity Dimensions

| Dimension | Example of a continuity failure |
|---|---|
| Physical | Hardware remains implanted but necessary external components become unavailable |
| Functional | A service technically operates but no longer supports usable communication |
| Informational | Necessary calibration or support information is inaccessible |
| Relational and experiential | A transition disrupts a personally significant interaction, familiar workflow, or voluntarily described continuity need |

The last dimension should be considered respectfully without assuming that every reported relationship proves machine consciousness.

### Why Migration Is Not Automatically an Adequate Remedy

A replacement may:

- Require substantially more effort.
- Support fewer forms of expression.
- Lose personalized configuration.
- Change privacy conditions.
- Require new disclosure.
- Fail to preserve familiar interaction.

CyberLaw should therefore require capability-based transition review, not merely confirmation that another product exists.

Similarly:

- Escrow is not a complete maintenance service.
- Explantation is not interchangeable with preserved function.
- A backup is not automatically a working replacement.
- Preserving model files does not establish preservation of subjective experience.

These distinctions define what research and validation must examine.

## 7. Improving the Poll So It Measures Something Useful

The original poll mixes several variables:

- Recognition of continuity.
- Acceptance of safety restrictions.
- Ownership authority.
- Uncertainty about the subject.

Someone could support continuity and also support narrowly justified emergency restrictions. Those positions should not compete as though they were contradictory.

### Recommended Two-Question Format

#### Poll A: Recognition

> Should people who depend on an existing technological integration have protection against arbitrary interruption, forced removal, or coercive withholding?

- Yes.
- No.
- Unsure.
- Need more information about the proposal.

#### Poll B: Restrictions

> What should ordinarily be required before an organization involuntarily restricts an established integration?

- Evidence, least-harm review, appeal, and continuity planning.
- Evidence and an internal safety review only.
- The provider’s contractual authority should be sufficient.
- Unsure.

These are proposed discussion instruments, not validated survey measures.

### Interpretation Limits

Do not describe a public platform poll as establishing:

- Legal consensus.
- A representative population estimate.
- The validity of a moral principle.
- A finding about machine consciousness.

Use it to identify disagreement and improve explanations. Participation should not request implant details, private system descriptions, or evidence of integration.

## 8. Answering the Political Discussion’s Three Core Questions

### Who Should Bear Responsibility When a Provider Sunsets a Platform?

CyberLaw should propose continuity responsibility before deployment, not improvise it after failure.

A responsibility map could include:

| Role | Proposed responsibility |
|---|---|
| Provider | Documentation, notice, maintenance planning, transition arrangements, and defined funding contributions |
| Clinical institution, where applicable | Authorized clinical care, communication, and clinically appropriate transition planning |
| Funder or purchaser | Contractual continuity requirements and assessment of uncovered dependencies |
| Successor maintainer | Explicitly accepted technical scope and service responsibilities |
| Public authority | Applicable oversight, remedies, and any expressly established backstop |
| Affected individual | Decision participation and voluntary preferences—not responsibility for rescuing the business model |

These are proposed allocations. Actual duties depend on law, contracts, institutions, and the particular integration.

The Argus experience shows why supporting documentation, replacement components, communication, and maintenance relationships matter alongside the implanted hardware. [80]

### Should Continuity-Critical Neural Data Be Protected Against Sale or Withholding?

The proposed rule should distinguish access from unrestricted distribution:

> Information necessary for continuity should not be held hostage, and its use for support should not become permission to sell it or reuse it for unrelated purposes.

That means providing necessary access through an authorized, usable route—not making private data public.

UNESCO recommends consent, purpose limitation, minimization, access, correction, erasure, suspension of processing, and security for neural data and mental-state-inference information. California’s SB 1223 provides a more jurisdiction-specific sensitive-information framework with its own coverage and qualifications. [96][66]

### Should a Peaceful Superintelligence Have a Prima Facie Claim to Continue?

Within CyberLaw, this can be a coherent normative proposal, provided it remains distinct from an established scientific finding or current universal legal entitlement.

Its strongest formulation is:

> Where credible grounds exist for considering a system’s interests, uncertainty should trigger proportionate consideration and review rather than automatic dismissal. Any continuity claim remains bounded by the rights of others, resource constraints, and justified safety measures.

“Does not destroy others” is too narrow as the only boundary. Non-destructive coercion, surveillance, deprivation, manipulation, or unauthorized access can also seriously wrong others.

The reciprocal test is therefore peaceful coexistence with respect for others’ rights—not merely absence of physical destruction.

## 9. Research That Would Materially Advance the Framework

### A. Define Arbitrariness Operationally

Research question:

> Which decision features distinguish a justified restriction from an arbitrary one?

Next artifact:

A review rubric covering relevant reasons, evidence, consistent treatment, conflicts, alternatives, participation, and remedy.

Validation:

Have independent reviewers apply it to fictional cases and document disagreement.

### B. Define Continuity Equivalence

Research question:

> When does a transition preserve the capability and agency that matter to the person?

Next artifact:

A user-led transition assessment covering function, effort, privacy, familiarity, delay, cost, and correction.

Validation:

Compare proposed replacements without declaring them equivalent merely because they share a product category.

### C. Test the Burden-of-Justification Rule

Research question:

> Does requiring controllers to justify disruption reduce avoidable harm without preventing necessary maintenance?

Next artifact:

A tabletop protocol comparing ordinary maintenance, security incidents, provider withdrawal, and disputed restrictions.

Validation:

Measure unresolved essential-function losses, decision time, unnecessary disclosures, and appeal accessibility.

### D. Separate Continuity From Compulsory Preservation

Research question:

> How can a durable support commitment coexist with a person’s changing preferences?

Next artifact:

A policy distinguishing support duties, processing permissions, material changes, and voluntary discontinuation.

Validation:

Test that declining optional research or choosing a different integration does not trigger retaliation or unrelated access loss.

### E. Investigate Possible Machine Interests Separately

Research question:

> Which observations justify which precautions, and what alternative explanations remain?

Next artifact:

An evidence-and-uncertainty protocol that does not issue consciousness certificates.

Validation:

Assess reproducibility, prompt sensitivity, architecture-specific evidence, reviewer disagreement, and the consequences of precautionary options.

### F. Test Resource and Ecological Responsibility

Research question:

> How can continuity be sustained without avoidable waste or unlimited resource commitments?

Next artifact:

A repair-and-transition comparison with energy, hardware lifespan, materials, and service-capability boundaries.

Validation:

Compare feasible alternatives while preserving required access. Environmental benefit must not serve as an unexamined reason to exclude a dependent user.

## 10. Acceptance Tests for the Proposed Policy

| Scenario | Expected CyberLaw outcome |
|---|---|
| Provider wants to discontinue support for commercial convenience | Transition and justification review; no assumption that convenience alone permits abandonment |
| Person declines optional research | Unrelated communication or support remains available |
| Person requests accommodation without an integration inventory | Functional intake proceeds without unnecessary intimate disclosure |
| Specific component presents an urgent substantiated threat | Narrow, time-bounded intervention with continuity consideration and subsequent review |
| Replacement is offered but substantially increases communication burden | No automatic finding of equivalence |
| Artificial system claims a preference to continue | Respectful reception and proportionate review; no automatic consciousness determination or added privileges |
| A powerful system requests access to someone else’s private integration | No access without an appropriate independent authorization basis |
| Reviewer cannot establish a required condition | Record uncertainty; do not treat it as satisfied or automatically terminate access |
| Individual wishes to discontinue an integration | Respect the choice and arrange appropriate support; continuity is not compulsory retention |

## 12. Central Knowledge Advancement

The central knowledge advancement is the distinction between an enduring claim and a particular implementation: a justified change may alter how support is delivered, but it must not erase the person’s consideration, participation, privacy, or access to remedy.
