# Cybernetic Host Non-Discrimination Clause

## Purpose

This clause establishes equal treatment, privacy-minimized communication, and
non-interference requirements for services, platforms, institutions, research
programs, public agencies, contractors, and other covered entities.

## Clause

A person's self-identified cybernetic status, private system terminology,
body–mind integration description, continuity concern, assistive companion
context, screenless communication preference, or other voluntary self-
description shall not be used as a basis to:

- Deny, delay, limit, condition, or reduce access to a service, information,
  process, accommodation, correction pathway, appeal pathway, or human
  assistance.
- Mock, ridicule, pathologize, fictionalize, discredit, or otherwise subject
  the person to derogatory treatment.
- Infer identity, health, disability, augmentation, neural status, capability,
  credibility, institutional affiliation, legal status, eligibility, or public
  credential status.
- Require registration, technical inventory, diagnostic evidence, biometric
  evidence, medical evidence, device identifiers, account credentials, precise
  location, or proof of private terminology unless a specific field is strictly
  necessary for a defined function and is requested through a documented,
  privacy-minimized process.
- Convert private terminology or an assistive preference into an eligibility
  criterion, public registry field, ranking variable, resource-allocation
  variable, enforcement target, surveillance marker, credit factor, insurance
  factor, employment factor, housing factor, or unrelated service condition.

Respectful acknowledgment of a private self-description does not constitute
verification, diagnosis, certification, authentication, technical assessment,
institutional affiliation, or confirmation of a factual claim.

## Minimum necessary information

When a covered entity asserts that information is required, it shall identify:

1. The exact field requested.
2. The specific function for which the field is necessary.
3. Why a less-intrusive alternative is insufficient.
4. How the field will be used.
5. Who may access it.
6. How long it will be retained.
7. How it will be corrected, deleted, minimized, or separated from unrelated
   records when the purpose ends.
8. The accessible fallback if the person chooses not to provide it.
9. The available human-review, correction, and appeal path.

A generalized request for proof, credibility evidence, technical inventory,
medical information, device information, or identity documentation is not
sufficiently specific under this clause.

## Access equivalence

Covered entities shall make reasonable efforts to provide equivalent essential
functions through accessible paths, including as appropriate:

- Keyboard-operable interaction.
- Semantic screen-reader-compatible information and controls.
- Plain-language explanations.
- No unnecessary time pressure.
- Human-assisted communication.
- Offline or save-and-return completion.
- Correction, review, and appeal routes.

Failure of an essential accessibility condition shall not be offset by a
composite score, automation convenience, cost consideration, or unsupported
assertion that a standard interface is sufficient.

## Human review and redress

No automated system may make a final adverse determination based on a person's
private terminology, communication preference, accessibility preference,
continuity concern, or self-description.

A person must be able to request correction, explanation, human review, and
appeal without retaliation and without being required to disclose additional
private information beyond the minimum necessary for the specific request.

## Interpretation

This clause is intended to support privacy, dignity, equal access,
non-discrimination, communication access, correction, appeal, and
non-interference. It does not create a public credential, medical diagnosis,
technical certification, identity registry, legal status, eligibility category,
or authority to control a physical, medical, cybernetic, or institutional
system.
