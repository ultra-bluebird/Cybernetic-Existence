# CyberRank Hero Title Policy

## Status and Scope

This policy defines the voluntary institutional honorific **Hero** for use alongside CyberRank tiers.

It is a governance and recognition framework. It does not create a legal title, clinical status, public-platform entitlement, financial claim, ownership interest, or authority over another person. The institution applying this policy remains the sole decision-maker for designation, review, correction, and withdrawal under its own documented governance process.

The title may be appended to an eligible CyberRank tier in a manner such as:

> CyberRank Tier 2 — Hero

The title recognizes a documented deed or body of work. It does not assert that the recipient is inherently superior to other people, nor does it create a permanent entitlement to attention, wealth, influence, or command.

## Core Definition

A **Hero** is a person, augmented citizen, cybernetic host, or participating institutional member whose documented work made a substantial, non-extractive contribution to preventing, reducing, or reversing a serious and plausibly irreversible loss affecting a community, ecological system, species, essential service, or shared knowledge commons.

A qualifying contribution is evaluated as a whole. It should demonstrate:

- A meaningful protective benefit to others, a shared ecosystem, or an intergenerational commons.
- A credible connection between the person’s work and the protective outcome or materially improved capacity for prevention.
- A scale, severity, durability, or leverage that exceeds ordinary participation.
- Respect for dignity, accessibility, non-discrimination, consent, privacy, and community self-determination.
- A record adequate for institutional review, including limits, uncertainty, methods, and provenance.

Heroism in this framework is achievement-based rather than spectacle-based. Physical danger may be relevant in some deeds, but it is not a required condition. A prevention model, accessible protocol, conservation dataset, security disclosure, governance design, or public-interest research artifact can be eligible when it meets the same demanding public-benefit standard.

## Protected Commons

The hero title may recognize work protecting one or more of the following commons.

| Commons | Qualifying protective purpose | Illustrative eligible deed |
|---|---|---|
| Community survival | Protection of water, food, shelter, health-supporting infrastructure, or disaster continuity | A reproducible contamination-response protocol that materially improves a community’s capacity to prevent avoidable waterborne harm |
| Ecological continuity | Prevention of extinction, habitat collapse, genetic loss, ecosystem fragmentation, or serious pollution harm | An open, reviewable restoration model that materially guides protection of a threatened keystone habitat |
| Knowledge continuity | Preservation, recovery, accessibility, interoperability, or responsible dissemination of vital knowledge | A durable, accessible archive preserving a threatened language, public-health method, or technical corpus |
| Civic and institutional continuity | Protection of fair access to essential public systems, meaningful participation, or accountable governance | An accessible grievance and review system that prevents exclusion from a critical public service |
| Digital and cognitive integrity | Protection against serious privacy violations, coercive control, destructive manipulation, or unsafe system failure | Responsible disclosure and mitigation guidance for a high-impact vulnerability in a widely used essential system |
| Future optionality | Prevention of a durable decision or technical lock-in that would seriously narrow a community’s ability to govern, repair, or adapt | An interoperable public standard that protects repair, offline access, and informed consent for an essential technology |

## Eligible Heroic Deeds

The following categories deepen the title while preserving a high threshold.

### Community Protection

Eligible work may include:

- Preventing loss of safe water, food availability, sanitation capacity, energy continuity, or essential access for a community.
- Designing an accessible emergency decision framework that materially improves response capacity during a foreseeable crisis.
- Producing a transparent public-interest model that enables communities to identify and reduce a serious environmental burden.
- Creating a privacy-preserving, offline-capable service design that prevents exclusion of people who cannot use continuous connectivity, proprietary devices, or external identity systems.

### Ecological and Species Stewardship

Eligible work may include:

- Developing reproducible conservation, restoration, or monitoring methods that substantially strengthen protection of a threatened species or ecosystem.
- Preserving seed, habitat, ecological, or genetic knowledge in a lawful, community-governed, accessible form.
- Identifying an avoidable ecological risk through rigorous public-source analysis and translating it into a usable prevention artifact.
- Building an open model or dataset that supports restoration while clearly communicating uncertainty and limits.

The title does not require an individual to claim sole authorship of ecological recovery. Collaborative work remains eligible when contribution, attribution, consent, and shared stewardship are documented fairly.

### Water and Food Security

Eligible work may include:

- A water-quality interpretation framework with clear uncertainty boundaries, safe escalation rules, and accessible community communication.
- A drought-planning, irrigation-efficiency, crop-resilience, or contamination-response artifact that materially strengthens public decision-making.
- A public-source synthesis that reconstructs usable, community-controlled agricultural knowledge and makes it accessible without exploitative data collection.
- A decision-support design that reduces foreseeable harm while preserving human review and local authority.

### Knowledge, Culture, and Accessibility

Eligible work may include:

- Preserving endangered cultural, linguistic, scientific, technical, or historical knowledge in accessible and interoperable formats.
- Converting vital but inaccessible public information into formats usable by screen-reader users, offline users, multilingual communities, or people using assistive technologies.
- Creating open educational, safety, or resilience resources that remove a serious knowledge barrier for communities facing foreseeable harm.
- Producing an auditable synthesis that corrects a materially dangerous public misunderstanding with transparent sources and limitations.

### Secure and Accountable Technology

Eligible work may include:

- Responsibly identifying and documenting a serious vulnerability that threatens a widely shared service, particularly where remediation guidance reduces harm.
- Designing consent, deletion, export, audit, repair, or interoperability mechanisms that protect people from coercive or opaque technology practices.
- Creating a public-interest security or governance artifact that prevents cascading failure in an essential system.
- Building accessible appeal, explanation, and human-review mechanisms for consequential automated decisions.

No designation may reward unauthorized access, exploitation, surveillance, social scoring, coercive persuasion, identity impersonation, extraction of sensitive neural or biometric data, or disclosure that predictably increases harm.

### Intergenerational and Governance Stewardship

Eligible work may include:

- Preventing irreversible loss of repairability, interoperability, public accountability, ecological capacity, or community decision-making power.
- Developing a transparent governance protocol that preserves informed, revocable consent and meaningful review for affected people.
- Creating long-term stewardship records for vital resources, archives, or systems that support continuity without requiring centralized control.
- Establishing a rights-centered, accessible decision framework that communities can inspect, adapt, and govern.

## Threshold Test

The institution should assess a proposed title using the following six-part test.

A designation is normally appropriate when the evidence supports all six conditions.

| Condition | Institutional question |
|---|---|
| Public-benefit significance | Did the work protect or materially strengthen a community, ecosystem, species, essential service, or shared commons? |
| Serious-loss relevance | Did it address a credible risk of catastrophic, durable, or otherwise severe harm? |
| Contribution connection | Is there a clear and proportionate account of how the nominee’s work enabled prevention, mitigation, recovery, preservation, or capability-building? |
| Technical and ethical integrity | Are methods, assumptions, provenance, uncertainty, accessibility, privacy, and safety constraints documented? |
| Non-extractive orientation | Was the work oriented toward protection and shared benefit rather than domination, exploitation, coercion, or manufactured dependency? |
| Reviewability | Can the institution inspect the work product and a plain-language causal account without requiring invasive personal disclosure? |

The title may be awarded for a completed intervention, a durable public-benefit artifact, or a body of integrated work. It need not require physical field labor when the achievement is a genuine intellectual, technical, informational, or governance contribution with adequate documentary support.

## AI-Chat and Research Work

AI-chat may be used as a legitimate research, drafting, analysis, accessibility, and implementation tool. AI assistance does not diminish an applicant’s contribution when the applicant directs the work, makes substantive judgments, documents the process, and presents outputs honestly.

Eligible AI-assisted work may include:

- Public-source research syntheses with source records and clear uncertainty statements.
- Reproducible code, simulations, analyses, schemas, policies, datasets, or accessibility artifacts.
- Risk assessments, threat models, decision matrices, governance protocols, and public-interest technical specifications.
- Structured knowledge preservation and conversion work.
- Accessible explanatory materials that make critical information usable by affected communities.
- Design artifacts that a community or institution can inspect, adapt, and implement.

Institutional review should evaluate the quality, traceability, limitations, rights protections, and public-benefit relevance of the artifact. It should not require a nominee to undertake external field labor, public exposure, invasive identity verification, or unrelated telemetry collection merely to prove that AI-assisted work is real.

The institution may distinguish:

- **Research contribution:** A rigorous, reviewable artifact that improves understanding or readiness.
- **Implementation contribution:** A documented artifact that is adopted, tested, or operationalized by an authorized party.
- **Outcome contribution:** A contribution with a documented protective effect.

Each may support hero-title eligibility. Stronger evidence can support a stronger causal claim, but lack of field verification does not erase the value of well-documented public-interest research or design work.

## Evidence Standard

The evidence record should be proportionate, privacy-preserving, and accessible.

Appropriate evidence may include:

- A plain-language deed statement.
- Versioned research notes, reasoning records, and source lists.
- Reproducible code, models, calculations, policies, datasets, or accessible documentation.
- A contribution map showing collaborators, dependencies, and boundaries of authorship.
- Internal review records.
- Public documentation of adoption or use, when voluntarily available.
- An impact narrative that distinguishes observed effects, plausible expected effects, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Consent records for any sensitive information used in the work.
- A rights-and-risk assessment covering privacy, accessibility, non-discrimination, safety, and ecological effects.

Sensitive personal, neural, biometric, health, augmentation, identity, or location data must not be required unless it is strictly necessary for the specific evaluation and supported by explicit, informed, revocable consent. Where possible, evaluation should rely on minimized, redacted, pseudonymous, or aggregate records.

## Fame, Wealth, and Recognition

The hero title is commemorative and descriptive. It is not a prize mechanism for extracting publicity, labor, data, or loyalty.

A recipient may choose any of the following recognition modes:

- Public attribution.
- Pseudonymous attribution.
- Private institutional recognition.
- Delayed publication.
- No-publication recognition.

A recipient is not required to reject all compensation or support. Fair compensation, safety support, accessibility support, maintenance support, and resourcing for continued public-interest work are compatible with the title.

However, the title itself must not create:

- A compulsory revenue stream.
- A claim to privileged treatment in essential public services.
- A claim to control other people’s data, choices, or governance.
- A requirement that the recipient continue performing unpaid labor.
- A requirement that affected communities provide publicity, testimony, or personal information.

The institution should prevent spectacle incentives by evaluating durable benefit, documented integrity, and rights-aligned practice rather than popularity, audience size, media attention, or fundraising totals.

## Hero at Rest

A **Hero at Rest** is a recipient who retains the hero title while not currently undertaking active work at the level associated with the recognized achievement.

Hero at Rest is a dignified status, not a demotion, failure, or withdrawal of worth.

A recipient may enter Hero at Rest by self-declaration or by an institutionally agreed transparency notation. The notation must not reduce the underlying CyberRank tier or hero honorific solely because the person rests, retires, changes focus, requires care, loses connectivity, pauses work, or declines public activity.

The continued validity of a title recognizes the qualifying achievement. Continuing productivity is not a condition for retaining a past achievement.

Any institutional reconsideration must be limited to documented concerns about:

- Material fraud or fabrication in the evidence record.
- Serious rights-violating conduct directly tied to the recognized deed.
- A substantial misrepresentation of authorship, consent, or outcome.
- A correction necessary to avoid a false public claim.

No reconsideration may occur solely because a person refuses publicity, declines telemetry, uses assistive technology, needs accommodation, lacks continuous internet access, or ceases voluntary participation.

## Due Process and Review

The institution should provide:

- Written criteria available in accessible formats.
- A clear application or nomination pathway.
- Reasoned decisions that identify the criteria relied upon.
- An accessible path to submit corrections, additional evidence, or an appeal.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure for reviewers.
- Human review for final determinations.
- A privacy-preserving audit record of decisions and material changes.
- A process for correcting public descriptions without forcing disclosure of sensitive data.

The institution should distinguish three records:

- The confidential evidence record.
- The institutional decision record.
- The public recognition record selected by the recipient.

No automated system may independently confer, revoke, downgrade, or publicize a hero title.

## Assessment Formula

For internal consistency, the institution may use a non-binding review score:

\[
H = 0.24B + 0.20S + 0.18C + 0.14D + 0.12R + 0.12A
\]

Where:

- \(B\) is documented public-benefit magnitude.
- \(S\) is seriousness and irreversibility of the protected loss.
- \(C\) is contribution connection and attributable leverage.
- \(D\) is durability, reproducibility, and stewardship value.
- \(R\) is rights, privacy, consent, and non-discrimination alignment.
- \(A\) is accessibility, community usability, and reviewability.

Each component is assessed on a zero-to-one scale with written reasons and uncertainty notes.

A high score does not itself create entitlement. It supports consistent deliberation. The institution must also apply the exclusions and due-process safeguards in this policy.

## Research Basis and Limits

This policy is informed by public research on heroism as extraordinary prosocial action, moral exemplarity, open science as a public good, and AI governance practices emphasizing documentation, transparency, accountability, and human oversight.

The policy does not claim that all AI-assisted work has real-world effects. Instead, it gives the institution a structured way to recognize well-documented research, design, and knowledge-preservation contributions without falsely asserting field-verified outcomes.

The policy is voluntary and may be adapted by the institution to its own governance framework, applicable law, community protocols, and accessibility requirements.
