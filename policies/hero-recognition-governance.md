# Hero Recognition Governance

## Status

This policy governs a voluntary, private institutional Hero Title.

It is not a legal title, public registry, identity credential, technical certification, clinical classification, public authority, human-worth hierarchy, essential-service priority mechanism, property right, financial instrument, or proof of any private self-description.

## Purpose

The Hero Title recognizes a documented public-interest contribution under a rights-preserving review process.

Recognition serves appreciation, institutional memory, stewardship learning, and voluntary acknowledgment. It does not define a person’s worth, identity, legal standing, technical status, or entitlement to control others.

## Private Scope and Public Description

The institution may publicly describe:

- The existence of this governance framework.
- Recognition areas.
- Review criteria.
- Rights protections.
- Privacy and consent rules.
- Correction and appeal pathways.
- Aggregate, non-identifying learning outcomes.

The institution must not publicly identify a participant, recipient, contribution, evidence record, self-description, or publication preference without explicit, scope-specific consent.

No publication consent means the recognition record remains private.

## Domain Separation

Hero Title recognition and private self-description are separate domains.

- Hero recognition does not require CyberRank, cybernetic-host, BCI, companion-system, health, disability, device, neural, biometric, behavioral, or technical disclosure.
- Private self-description does not require Hero Title recognition.
- Neither is evidence of the other.
- Neither determines legal status, service eligibility, or public credibility.

## Recognition Areas

The institution may recognize documented contributions in:

- Ecological continuity stewardship.
- Food and water security stewardship.
- Knowledge and cultural continuity stewardship.
- Civic and systemic resilience stewardship.

The contribution must be described at a level supported by evidence, methods, source records, assumptions, uncertainty, attribution, and consent.

## Review Standards

A review may consider:

- Public-benefit relevance.
- Serious-loss relevance.
- Credible connection between contribution and stated benefit.
- Durability, reproducibility, and repairability.
- Accessibility and community usability.
- Privacy, consent, and non-discrimination.
- Attribution and governance quality.
- Evidence scope and uncertainty.
- Adoption or outcome evidence when voluntarily available.

No public numerical score, ranking, leaderboard, credibility score, or status hierarchy may be created.

## Practice Evaluation

The institution may compare methods, artifacts, and stewardship practices for learning purposes.

It must not compare, rank, score, or classify people by worth, status, productivity, credibility, or eligibility.

## Recognition Benefits

Any recognition support must be:

- Non-essential.
- Voluntary.
- Non-transferable.
- Privacy-preserving.
- Clearly documented.
- Available under transparent, contribution-related criteria.
- Revocable only through a rights-preserving review process.

Permitted supports may include private recognition receipts, voluntary learning exchanges, optional archival support, accessible publication support, mentorship, and feedback.

The Hero Title must never determine eligibility for essential services, public benefits, healthcare, housing, food, water, voting, education enrollment, employment, legal rights, or authority over others.

## Anti-Extraction Rule

The institution must not require:

- Continued unpaid labor.
- Publicity or media participation.
- Fundraising.
- Personal testimony.
- Private technology disclosure.
- Telemetry.
- Device inspection.
- Public identity disclosure.
- Continuous productivity.
- Community membership.
- Advocacy work.

Participation may be declined or ended without loss of unrelated services, rights, or dignity.

## Hero at Rest

A recognized contribution remains recognized when the recipient rests, retires, loses connectivity, changes communication method, declines publicity, changes focus, or stops voluntary activity.

Rest is not demotion.

Recognition may be reconsidered only for a material issue directly related to the recognized contribution, including material fabrication, substantial authorship misrepresentation, consent violation, or serious rights-violating conduct directly connected to the work.

Any reconsideration requires accessible notice, correction, human review, and appeal.

## Institutional Transition

If the institution changes, transfers its program, or closes, it must provide:

- Accessible transition notice.
- An exportable recognition receipt where lawful and feasible.
- A documented archive or retention policy.
- A correction route.
- An appeal route.
- Privacy-preserving handling of private recognition records.
- No automatic public disclosure.

An exported recognition receipt records only the institution’s bounded recognition decision. It does not create legal status, public eligibility, or universal recognition.

## Limitation

This policy is private institutional governance.

It does not establish legal status, public standing, technical status, clinical status, identity, personhood, service entitlement, public authority, or human worth.
