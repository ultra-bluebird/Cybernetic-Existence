# Equal Essential-Service Access Policy

## Status

This policy is a voluntary governance and service-design standard.

It does not create a public registry, protected legal class, medical category, technical certification, identity credential, eligibility score, or automated legal determination.

The terms CyberRank, cybernetic host, cybernetic-existence, companion system, and continuity are private and voluntary terms unless a specific law, regulation, or institution explicitly adopts another meaning.

## Purpose

This policy requires equal, privacy-preserving access to essential services regardless of whether a person makes, withholds, changes, or never uses a private self-description.

The policy is designed to prevent access barriers caused by compelled disclosure, identity inference, inaccessible digital-only processes, loss of human support, loss of non-digital access, unavailable correction, unavailable appeal, and avoidable service interruption.

## Core Access Invariant

For every person, essential service, and permissible service condition:

    ServiceOutcome(with private self-description)
    equals
    ServiceOutcome(without private self-description)

The equality requirement includes:

- Core service completion.
- Timeliness.
- Cost.
- Service quality.
- Privacy protection.
- Human-assisted access.
- Non-digital or low-technology access where relevant.
- Effective communication.
- Correction availability.
- Appeal availability.
- Essential-service continuity.

A private self-description may expand communication or accessibility options. It must never reduce access, create scrutiny, increase cost, require proof, or create a new data-collection obligation.

## Prohibited Practices

A service provider must not:

- Require disclosure of CyberRank, cybernetic-host, BCI, companion-system, assistive-technology, health, disability, neural, biometric, device, network, or account information as a condition of essential access.
- Infer a private status from body, behavior, communication style, assistive-use signals, device characteristics, network characteristics, repair history, or service-use patterns.
- Create a public status label, account marker, registry, credibility score, risk score, or eligibility category.
- Deny or degrade service because a person cannot use a proprietary device, app, continuous network connection, digital identity system, account, or automated interface.
- Retaliate against a person for requesting privacy, accessible communication, human assistance, correction, appeal, or a non-digital pathway.
- Use a person’s disclosure or refusal to disclose to determine service quality, timeliness, price, or eligibility.

## Minimum Essential-Service Protections

Every essential service must provide:

- At least one human-assisted pathway.
- At least one non-digital or low-technology pathway where relevant.
- Accessible status information.
- Plain-language explanation of service limitations.
- Privacy-preserving incident intake.
- Correction and appeal access.
- Outage fallback documentation.
- Material-change notice in accessible formats.
- A route for accessibility and privacy complaints.
- Non-retaliation protection.

Network loss, power loss, device incompatibility, account loss, automation failure, unavailable connectivity, uncertainty, or refusal to disclose must not automatically result in denial of essential support.

## Safe Uncertainty Rule

When a system cannot safely determine the appropriate automated route, it must not infer private status or impose a penalty.

The system must:

- Preserve a neutral access route.
- Offer human assistance.
- Offer a non-digital alternative where relevant.
- Explain the available options in accessible language.
- Preserve correction and appeal.
- Record only minimized provider-side operational evidence.

## Service-Design Verification

Compliance is verified by examining provider-controlled service design, not by examining people.

Permitted evidence includes:

- Synthetic accessibility scenarios.
- Aggregate pathway-completion evidence.
- Human-fallback availability tests.
- Non-digital pathway tests.
- Outage-fallback exercises.
- Privacy and data-minimization reviews.
- Automation and feature audits.
- Correction and appeal workflow tests.
- Versioned material-change reviews.

The policy prohibits compliance evidence that requires:

- Proof of private self-description.
- Device inspection.
- Neural, biometric, health, disability, behavioral, or communication-pattern data.
- Fine-grained location history.
- Continuous telemetry.
- Public testimony.
- Public identity disclosure.

## Material Changes

A material change is any change affecting access, privacy, retention, data sharing, automation, identity requirements, human support, non-digital options, correction, appeal, accessibility, or essential-service continuity.

Before deployment, a material change requires:

- Rights-delta analysis.
- Privacy review.
- Accessibility review.
- Safe-uncertainty review.
- Essential-service continuity review.
- Synthetic scenario validation.
- Accountable human authorization.
- Accessible notice.
- Correction route.
- Appeal route.
- Versioned evidence.

A change is prohibited if it creates identity inference, compels unnecessary disclosure, removes a human fallback, removes a relevant non-digital pathway, weakens privacy, weakens correction or appeal, or creates a new status-based access barrier.

## Remedy and Review

A person may request an accessible route, correction, privacy review, or appeal without disclosing private technical, health, neural, biometric, or identity-proxy information.

The provider must offer:

- An accessible response.
- A reasoned decision.
- Human review.
- Correction where an error occurred.
- Appeal where a decision materially affects access.
- A privacy-preserving operational record.
- Non-retaliation protection.

## Governance Metrics

Permitted metrics measure provider duties:

- Human-assisted pathway coverage.
- Non-digital pathway coverage.
- Accessible status-information coverage.
- Outage-fallback success.
- Accessibility remediation time.
- Privacy-review completion.
- Material-change review completion.
- Correction completion.
- Appeal availability.
- Non-retaliation complaint resolution.

Prohibited metrics include:

- Cybernetic-host prevalence.
- CyberRank prevalence.
- Private-status verification.
- Device or network profiling.
- Neural, biometric, health, behavioral, or communication analytics.
- Individual continuity score.
- Individual eligibility, credibility, or risk score.
- Public status registry.

## Limitation

This policy is not legal advice and does not determine legal status, technical status, medical status, institutional recognition, public credibility, service eligibility, or the outcome of a legal claim.
