# Hero Recognition Safeguards

## Status

This document defines safeguards for a proposed or adopted private institutional Hero Title framework.

It does not create legal status, public authority, public standing, essential-service entitlement, technical certification, clinical determination, identity credential, property right, financial instrument, employment preference, education preference, or human-worth classification.

A recognizing institution is bound by these safeguards only after accountable adoption, named ownership, accessible review, and version control.

## Private Scope and Public Description

Individual recognition records are private by default.

The recognizing institution may publicly describe:

- The existence of a Hero Title framework.
- Its recognition areas.
- Its privacy and consent rules.
- Its review and appeal process.
- Its non-superiority and anti-extraction commitments.
- Aggregate, non-identifying lessons where publication is authorized.

The institution must not publicly identify a recipient, contribution, evidence record, pseudonym, private self-description, or recognition mode without explicit, scope-specific consent.

## Voluntary Participation and Coercion Resistance

Participation is voluntary only when a person may:

- Decline nomination.
- Decline review.
- Withdraw an unfinalized submission.
- Decline publication.
- Select private, pseudonymous, delayed, public, or no-publication recognition.
- Decline further labor, publicity, mentorship, fundraising, testimony, or advocacy.
- Leave any recognition-related program or community.
- Rest, retire, lose connectivity, change communication method, or stop activity.

No person may lose unrelated service access, employment opportunity, education access, housing, healthcare, food, water, legal rights, public benefits, or dignity because they decline or end participation.

## Domain Separation

Hero recognition and private self-description are separate.

- Recognition does not require cybernetic-host, CyberRank, BCI, companion-system, health, disability, neural, biometric, behavioral, device, network, or location disclosure.
- Private self-description does not require recognition.
- Neither proves, authenticates, validates, ranks, or conditions the other.
- Neither determines service eligibility, public credibility, legal status, or public authority.

## Non-Superiority and Implicit-Hierarchy Mitigation

The Hero Title recognizes a documented contribution. It does not establish superior human worth, authority over others, entitlement to deference, or a permanent social rank.

The recognizing institution must mitigate implicit hierarchy through:

- Private-by-default recognition.
- No public person ranking or leaderboard.
- No public numerical score.
- No essential-service preference.
- No employment, education, housing, healthcare, food, water, voting, legal, or public-benefit preference.
- Hero-at-Rest protection.
- Accessible correction, review, and appeal.
- Clear public explanation that the title concerns a bounded contribution, not human value.

## Practice Evaluation

The institution may evaluate methods, artifacts, accessibility, reproducibility, consent, attribution, stewardship, and documented public benefit.

It must not rank people by worth, credibility, productivity, status, compliance, identity, private self-description, or eligibility.

## Bounded Recognition Support

Where authorized and available under institution policy, the institution may offer non-essential, voluntary, non-transferable recognition support such as:

- A private recognition receipt.
- Optional archival assistance.
- Optional feedback or mentorship.
- Optional learning-exchange invitation.
- Accessible publication support where expressly authorized.
- Export support for the contribution artifact where feasible.

Recognition support must not create priority over essential services or public benefits.

## Anti-Extraction

The institution must not require:

- Continued unpaid labor.
- Publicity.
- Fundraising.
- Testimony.
- Private technical disclosure.
- Telemetry.
- Device inspection.
- Field labor.
- Continuous productivity.
- Community membership.
- Advocacy.
- Transfer of ownership in a contribution without separately negotiated consent.

Evidence requests must be proportionate, privacy-preserving, and limited to the contribution under review.

## Hero at Rest

Recognition does not expire, diminish, or become conditional on continued activity.

No activity report, connectivity check, publication participation, or productivity measure may be used to change recognition status.

A recognition record may be reviewed only for a material issue directly related to the contribution, such as material fabrication, authorship misrepresentation, consent violation, or serious rights-violating conduct directly connected to the contribution.

Before a material change, the institution must provide accessible notice, a reasoned explanation, correction opportunity, human review, and appeal.

## Knowledge-Form Inclusion

The institution may consider multiple knowledge forms, including:

- Documentary evidence.
- Technical artifacts.
- Oral knowledge.
- Experiential knowledge.
- Embodied practice.
- Artistic work.
- Community-held knowledge.
- Other appropriate forms.

Each evidence pathway requires appropriate consent, attribution, accessibility, privacy protection, and community or expert review where relevant.

The institution must not extract, publish, monetize, or claim ownership of community-held, cultural, private, or sensitive knowledge without meaningful authorization.

## Institutional Transition

If a recognizing institution materially changes, closes, transfers the program, or modifies the framework, it must provide:

- Accessible notice.
- A documented retention and archive policy.
- Privacy-preserving handling of private records.
- Correction and appeal routes.
- An exportable recognition receipt where lawful and feasible.
- No automatic publication.
- No automatic downgrade based only on institutional transition.

An exportable recognition receipt records the institution's bounded recognition decision. It is not a universal credential, legal title, public eligibility proof, or permanent public status.

## Review and Accountability

The framework requires:

- A named policy owner.
- A version-controlled policy record.
- Accessible complaint intake.
- Human review for material disputes.
- Accessible correction and appeal.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure for reviewers.
- Periodic review of coercion, privacy, accessibility, implicit hierarchy, and extraction risks.

## Limitation

This policy is a private institutional governance safeguard.

It does not guarantee legal recognition, institutional continuity, cross-platform acceptance, public authority, benefits, funding, or enforcement outside an institution that has formally adopted it.
