# Community Knowledge Moderation Policy

## Purpose

This policy governs a privacy-preserving, community-oriented knowledge graph for
local problem-solving, resource sharing, and evidence-aware civic research.

The graph records claims, evidence, review context, and supersession relationships.
It does not verify personal identity, assign public status, rank contributors, or
authorize real-world actions.

## Submission rules

- Contributors may submit issues, resources, solutions, questions, and evidence links.
- The system must offer accessible, plain-language, and non-digital submission paths.
- A contributor must not be required to disclose legal identity, health information,
  assistive-technology use, augmentation status, device details, or private telemetry.
- Location references must use the least precise form adequate for the stated purpose.
- Submissions containing personal data, private addresses, protected locations, threats,
  credentials, raw telemetry, or other sensitive information require restriction or
  deletion review before any broad publication.

## Claim labels

Every claim must be labeled as one of:

- cited
- assumption_labeled
- open_question_labeled
- unsupported
- superseded
- withdrawn

A claim label describes evidence status. It does not describe the worth, reliability,
identity, or eligibility of a contributor.

## High-impact claims

A high-impact claim requires:

- at least two independent evidence records where feasible;
- disclosure of known conflicts or limitations;
- recorded human review;
- an accessible appeal path;
- a privacy and location-sensitivity review.

If these conditions are not met, the claim must be marked as needing evidence,
restricted, or presented only as an explicitly labeled open question.

## Moderation decisions

Moderation decisions must include:

- the policy rule applied;
- a concise explanation;
- the minimum necessary content reference;
- a review date;
- an accessible appeal route;
- a supersession or withdrawal reference when applicable.

Moderation must not rely on a contributor's self-description, communication style,
assistive needs, refusal to disclose, health, identity, device use, or inferred status.

## Appeals

A contributor may appeal a moderation decision without providing unnecessary private
information. Appeal review must be accessible and may include a human or non-digital
path. The appeal record must distinguish a request for review from a finding of fact.

## Transparency

Publish aggregate moderation statistics and policy revisions when safe to do so.
Do not publish contributor identities, private correspondence, sensitive locations,
raw submissions, or restricted evidence.

## Non-actuation

A graph entry, moderation outcome, verification report, or endorsement does not
authorize enforcement, service denial, resource allocation, field deployment, or any
other external action.
