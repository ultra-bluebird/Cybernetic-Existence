## Purpose

The arrangement supports a reviewed transition when contracted maintenance
or support ends. It does not guarantee continued function, clinical safety,
or the availability or suitability of explantation.

## Roles and effectiveness

The executed arrangement must identify the depositor, escrow agent,
beneficiary representative, successor-maintenance review role, and
responsible clinical or institutional roles where applicable.

It must specify governing law, covered releases, funding, review periods,
notice routes, cure periods, and dispute procedures.

These model terms are not an executed agreement.

## Public interoperability materials

The depositor supplies rights-cleared documentation of:
- Version negotiation and supported compatibility.
- Data classes, units, time bases, and error semantics.
- Supported import/export boundaries.
- Maintenance and end-of-support expectations.
- Synthetic conformance fixtures.

Public materials exclude personal records, private calibration,
operational credentials, and undisclosed proprietary implementation.

## Confidential deposit

The deposit covers the agreed source release, build instructions,
necessary dependency information, lawful maintenance permissions,
test materials, known issues, and maintenance documentation.

Third-party restrictions and missing dependencies must be explicit.

Individual clinical and calibration records remain outside this deposit
unless separately authorized and governed.

## Deposit verification

Verification must establish whether the agreed materials are complete
and whether the deposited release can be built and evaluated in the
approved non-clinical test setting.

A received archive is not equivalent to a verified maintenance package.

Unresolved build, licensing, staffing, or support gaps must be reported.

## Release triggers

The executed schedule must define objective evidence and review for:
- Planned support termination without an accepted transition.
- Cessation of the contracted maintenance service.
- Material deposit or maintenance failures after the agreed cure process.
- Other specifically agreed continuity failures.

Insolvency-related enforceability and licensing consequences require
jurisdiction-specific legal review.

A trigger does not itself authorize public disclosure or device changes.

## Conditional maintainer access

Access is limited to the reviewed successor-maintenance purpose and
the permissions actually granted.

The successor must satisfy confidentiality, security, competency,
funding, and applicable safety-review conditions.

Necessary subcontracting and dependency rights must be addressed
expressly rather than assumed.

Confidential access is not an open-source license.

## Change and deployment review

Possession of source does not authorize deployment.

Material maintenance changes require the applicable engineering,
security, clinical, regulatory, and person-directed authorization reviews.

No escrow event automatically alters parameters, disables a system,
publishes credentials, or transfers personal data.

## Beneficiary choice and ordinary support

The arrangement must provide accessible explanations, correction,
human-supported contact, and notice of material changes.

A person is not required to disclose an augmentation inventory or
private physiological information merely to obtain those routes.

Discussion of continued use, alternatives, or explantation must remain
person-directed and appropriately clinically supported.

Explantation is not an automatic response to commercial termination.

## Funding and transition capability

Custody, verification, successor transition, and ongoing maintenance
costs must be addressed in the executed funding schedule.

The parties must assess non-code dependencies, including staffing,
parts, hosting, and lawful credential-service continuity.

A funded archive without a workable transition is not demonstrated
continuity.

## Correction, dispute, and termination

Disputes must distinguish archive completeness, licensing rights,
maintenance capability, and clinical deployment authority.

A dispute must not automatically remove ordinary explanation,
communication support, or correction access.

Retention, return, deletion, incident handling, and confidentiality
survival must be defined in the executed arrangement.

Publication of any proprietary component requires a separate,
rights-cleared decision and license.
