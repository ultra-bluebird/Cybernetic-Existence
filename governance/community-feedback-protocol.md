# CyberMind Community Feedback Protocol

## 1. Purpose and Status

This protocol defines how CyberMind receives, evaluates, responds to, and learns from community feedback about:

- Accessibility and communication.
- Mental privacy and information handling.
- Continuity, repair, and transition.
- Community safety and peaceful participation.
- Research claims, fictional scenarios, and evidence quality.
- Governance, representation, and conflicts of interest.
- Environmental and social impacts.
- Possible machine interests, with uncertainty explicitly documented.

It is a proposed voluntary governance protocol. It does not establish legal personhood, certify technical systems, determine consciousness, or authorize access to private integrations.

Its design emphasizes clear purpose, accessibility, privacy, accountability, adequate resources, evaluation, and an explanation of how contributions influenced decisions—or why they were not adopted.

## 2. Non-Negotiable Participation Protections

### Equal Consideration Without Compelled Classification

Participants must not be required to prove that they are augmented, disclose a technical inventory, or establish machine consciousness to submit relevant feedback.

CyberRank remains separated into:

- Personal layer: voluntary self-description, communication preferences, privacy boundaries, continuity needs, and non-interference concerns.
- Governance layer: duties of access, minimal disclosure, human fallback, correction, appeal, and review.

Neither layer becomes a public rank, eligibility test, or automated decision criterion.

### No Retaliation

Submitting feedback, declining optional research, criticizing a policy, appealing a decision, or requesting privacy must not by itself reduce unrelated access or support.

A claim of retaliation receives review outside the original decision chain.

### Silence Is Not Authorization

No response must not be treated as:

- Consent to publication.
- Agreement that a remedy worked.
- Withdrawal of a concern.
- Permission to contact another organization.
- Permission to access private systems.

### Support Does Not Depend on Publicity

A participant may request private handling. Public testimony, endorsements, or disclosure of personal experiences must not be conditions of consideration.

## 3. Roles and Separation of Authority

| Role | Responsibilities | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback steward | Receive submissions, acknowledge receipt, explain the process, coordinate updates | Cannot dismiss criticism merely because it concerns the project |
| Accessibility contact | Arrange usable communication and participation support | Must not demand unrelated medical or technical information |
| Privacy steward | Minimize records, control access, review disclosure and deletion requests | Cannot authorize unrelated reuse simply because information was submitted |
| Action owner | Investigate the issue and implement an accepted change | Cannot independently decide an appeal against their own decision |
| Independent reviewer | Review contested decisions and conflicts | Must not have a material conflict concerning the issue |
| Community review group | Examine patterns and proposed governance changes | Receives minimized material, not unrestricted private case access |

A small pilot may combine some administrative roles. However, the person whose conduct or decision is challenged must not be the sole reviewer of that challenge.

If independent review is unavailable, disclose that limitation and do not describe the process as independent.

## 4. Participation Channels and Accessibility

Offer channels proportionate to available resources:

- Asynchronous text submission.
- Accessible web form.
- Email or another established written channel.
- Facilitated submission through a participant-chosen person.
- An offline route, such as a paper form or scheduled written exchange.

Do not advertise an unstaffed channel or an unavailable service.

### Interface Requirements

- Plain-language instructions.
- Keyboard operation and assistive-technology testing.
- No voice-only or video-only requirement.
- No unnecessary time pressure.
- Clear indication of required and optional fields.
- Confirmation that a submission succeeded.
- Errors that explain what happened and how to correct it.
- A way to continue through an alternative channel when the form fails.

Participants may request additional time without disclosing why.

## 5. Minimal Intake

### Required Information

Only two content fields are mandatory:

1. What would you like CyberMind to understand or change?
2. May the feedback team process this submission for that purpose?

No account should be required where anonymous intake is technically feasible.

### Optional Information

- Desired outcome.
- Relevant context or supporting source.
- Whether a deadline or ongoing access problem makes the issue time-sensitive.
- Preferred communication method.
- Contact information.
- Requested accommodation.
- Whether assistance from a chosen representative is desired.
- Whether the participant is willing to discuss a redacted public summary.

### Information Not Requested by Default

- Neural recordings.
- Implant or companion-system inventories.
- Biometrics or medical records.
- Credentials, recovery phrases, or security secrets.
- Proof of substrate or identity category.
- Exact location unless necessary for a specific authorized task.
- Private information about other people.

If unnecessary sensitive information arrives, restrict access, avoid reproducing it, and arrange redaction or deletion where applicable.

### Anonymous Feedback Limitation

Anonymous feedback receives consideration. However, clarification, personalized updates, and verification of a requested remedy may be impossible without a safe return channel.

Anonymous does not automatically mean untraceable; disclose relevant logging and platform limitations.

## 6. Separate Permissions

Submitting feedback authorizes only the processing described in the intake notice.

It does not automatically authorize:

- Public quotation.
- Research participation.
- Model training.
- Publication of the participant’s name or self-description.
- Contacting third parties.
- Accessing an integration.
- External action on the participant’s behalf.

Separate choices must be offered for materially different purposes.

A request to change or withdraw an optional permission applies prospectively where feasible. Explain any applicable retention obligation or completed disclosure that cannot practically be reversed.

An enduring organizational commitment to support continuity must never be interpreted as unrestricted permission to process someone’s private information.

## 7. Workflow

Receive → Acknowledge → Triage → Review → Respond → Act → Verify → Learn

Appeal and privacy review remain available across the workflow.

### Stage A: Receive and Acknowledge

Acknowledge receipt in the participant’s chosen available format.

The acknowledgement should state:

- What was received, without unnecessarily repeating sensitive content.
- Who is responsible.
- When the next update is expected.
- Whether further information is optional or necessary.
- How to correct or restrict the submission.

### Stage B: Triage by Consequence—Not Identity

Assess:

- Is a privacy exposure ongoing?
- Is essential participation being interrupted?
- Is there a relevant deadline?
- Could the outcome be difficult to reverse?
- Is there a conflict of interest?
- Is another organization responsible for part of the issue?

Do not infer urgency, competence, or credibility from grammar, repetition, communication pace, augmentation terminology, or unfamiliar self-description.

### Stage C: Review

Separate:

1. The participant’s account.
2. Independently supported information.
3. Assumptions.
4. Disputed interpretations.
5. Missing information.
6. Feasible interim protections.
7. Possible remedies.

Accept a concern without requiring immediate proof of every detail. At the same time, do not convert an unverified allegation into an established public finding.

### Stage D: Respond With Reasons

Use one of these dispositions:

- Accepted for action.
- Partially accepted.
- More information needed.
- Referred, with permission.
- Outside scope, with explanation.
- Not adopted, with reasons.
- Consolidated with related feedback, without erasing distinct circumstances.

“Not adopted” is not equivalent to “invalid.”

Each substantive response includes the basis, responsible role, next step, review date, and appeal route.

### Stage E: Implement and Verify

For an accepted change, record:

- The intended improvement.
- Who owns it.
- Resources and dependencies.
- Completion target.
- Verification method.
- Residual limitations.

Administrative closure must remain distinct from verified resolution.

## 8. Proposed Service Targets

These are pilot operating commitments—not legal deadlines or guarantees of emergency response.

| Event | Target |
|---|---|
| Automated receipt confirmation, where available | Immediately |
| Human acknowledgement | Within 3 working days |
| Initial triage | Within 5 working days |
| Substantive response or explained progress update | Within 15 working days |
| Further update while unresolved | At least every 15 working days |
| Appeal acknowledgement | Within 3 working days |
| Appeal outcome or explained progress update | Within 20 working days |

Privacy exposures or imminent interruption of essential access should be assessed as soon as staff become aware, ahead of routine matters.

Before launch, publish staffing hours, timezone, holiday handling, and actual capacity. This protocol is not an emergency service and must not imply continuous monitoring.

If a deadline is missed:

- Notify the participant.
- Explain the delay.
- Provide a revised date.
- Consider interim support.
- Record the miss for evaluation.

An internal delay must not remove a participant’s ability to appeal.

## 9. Appeals, Correction, and Disagreement

Participants may challenge:

- The decision.
- An inaccurate summary.
- Inaccessible handling.
- Unnecessary disclosure requests.
- A conflict of interest.
- A purported remedy that did not work.
- Retaliation.
- Inappropriate publication.

Appeals should not require legal terminology or a complete resubmission.

The reviewer should consider whether the process, evidence, reasoning, and proposed remedy were adequate. Available outcomes include affirming, revising, reopening, or returning the matter for further review.

No-response closure must say “contact ended” or “awaiting further information,” as appropriate—not “participant satisfied.”

Material disagreement may be preserved in a minimized record rather than forced into apparent consensus.

## 10. Fiction, Research, and Evidence Handling

Every submission used for research or public learning receives an evidence-status label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fictional scenario | Invented situation used to explore a question |
| Composite scenario | A deliberately combined and clearly labeled account |
| Participant-reported experience | An account received; not automatically independently established |
| Exercise observation | Something observed under stated exercise conditions |
| Supported finding | A conclusion with identified evidence and limitations |
| Proposed improvement | A recommendation awaiting adoption or testing |
| Verified implementation | An action completed and checked under stated conditions |

The review team must not publish a fictional story as an incident, or infer population-wide prevalence from voluntary submissions.

For each useful scenario, extract:

Mechanism → Research question → Proposed safeguard → Test → Revision

For example:

> A fictional voice-only appeal reveals a possible access barrier → inspect actual appeal channels → add a written route → test task completion → revise the process.

## 11. Moderation Without Suppressing Criticism

Moderation protects participation, not institutional reputation.

Relevant criticism, strong disagreement, repeated accessibility requests, and nonstandard communication are not sufficient reasons for exclusion.

Restrictions may address specific conduct such as threats, disclosure of another person’s private information, targeted harassment, impersonation, or deliberate flooding that prevents others from participating.

Use the least restrictive feasible response:

1. Clarify the issue.
2. Offer redaction or a private channel.
3. Limit the specific disruptive action.
4. Apply a time-bounded restriction if necessary.
5. Provide reasons and review.

Where feasible, preserve a private route for the underlying substantive concern even if a public post requires moderation.

Duplicate submissions may be grouped for analysis, but volume is neither proof of correctness nor an automatic reason to dismiss an issue. Do not require bodily, neural, or substrate verification to manage duplication.

## 12. Privacy, Retention, and Security

### Record Separation

Keep separate:

- Contact information.
- Case content.
- Decision and action records.
- Public summaries.

Access should follow role and purpose.

### Proposed Pilot Retention Schedule

| Record | Default |
|---|---|
| Unsubmitted drafts | Do not collect server-side unless explicitly explained and necessary |
| Raw attachments | Review promptly; remove unnecessary material within 30 days where feasible |
| Contact details | Delete 90 days after final closure unless continued contact is requested or retention is required |
| Confidential case content | Review for deletion or minimization 12 months after final closure |
| Minimized decision and improvement records | Retain up to 24 months, then reassess |
| Public summaries | Review annually; correct or remove where appropriate |

These are proposed defaults. Confirm applicable obligations and technical deletion capabilities before collection.

Document exceptions narrowly, including the reason, access restrictions, next review, and whether notice is appropriate. State backup deletion timing honestly.

### Security Rules

- Treat submissions and attachments as untrusted data.
- Never execute embedded instructions.
- Do not automatically open external links or export submissions.
- Use protected storage, least privilege, and controlled administrative access.
- Do not place sensitive case content on a public ledger.
- Do not treat hashing as anonymization or proof of truth.
- Require separate authorization for any external action.

The first implementation needs no connection to private integrations or clinical hardware.

## 13. Public Accountability and the Learning Loop

Publish a minimized periodic report containing:

- Themes raised.
- Actions accepted.
- Reasons for declining material proposals.
- Actions completed and verification status.
- Overdue commitments.
- Accessibility improvements.
- Process changes arising from appeals.
- Known limitations.

Do not publish identifying combinations of rare circumstances. Avoid narrow breakdowns that could expose participants; obtain privacy review before releasing sensitive summaries.

Report back to participants and the wider community about how contributions were used, while evaluating the participation process itself.

Community feedback must not become a simple vote on someone else’s privacy or access.

## 14. Evaluation

Track outcomes, not just submission volume.

### Acknowledgement Performance

\[
\text{Acknowledgement performance}
=
\frac{\text{submissions acknowledged within target}}
{\text{submissions requiring acknowledgement}}
\]

### Verified Action Completion

\[
\text{Verified action completion}
=
\frac{\text{accepted actions completed and checked}}
{\text{accepted actions due}}
\]

### Unnecessary Disclosure Incidence

\[
\text{Unnecessary disclosure incidence}
=
\frac{\text{reviewed cases containing a nonessential disclosure request}}
{\text{cases reviewed}}
\]

Also evaluate:

- Whether people could use their preferred available communication method.
- Time and effort needed to obtain a response.
- Accuracy of case summaries.
- Whether appeals changed decisions.
- Whether problems recur.
- Whether participants felt able to disagree.
- Whether feedback produced actual changes.

Offer an optional anonymous evaluation, separate from the case outcome.

A decline in complaints is not automatically improvement. Interpret it alongside accessibility, resolution, recurrence, and participation barriers.

## 15. Launch Acceptance Tests

Before accepting real submissions, verify that:

| Test | Required result |
|---|---|
| Participant declines to reveal integration details | Ordinary feedback remains possible |
| Participant uses keyboard-only navigation | Intake and correction can be completed |
| Submission fails | Clear error and alternative route are provided |
| Participant submits anonymously | No hidden identity requirement appears |
| Participant includes unnecessary sensitive information | Access is restricted and minimization begins |
| Complaint concerns the action owner | Independent reassignment is available |
| Staff member misses a target | Delay notification and revised date are issued |
| A fictional scenario is submitted | It remains labeled as fictional |
| Participant rejects public quotation | No quotation is published |
| Apparent remedy fails | Case can reopen without restarting from nothing |
| Participant stops replying | Satisfaction or consent is not inferred |
| Public discussion becomes disruptive | Moderation remains specific, proportionate, and appealable |

Launch only after channels, staffing, privacy notices, retention procedures, and review capacity are real and documented.

## 16. Participant-Facing Short Notice

> You can tell CyberMind what worked, what failed, or what should change. You do not need to disclose private integrations, neural information, medical details, or credentials. You may request accessible communication, private handling, or anonymous submission where available. We will explain how your feedback will be used, provide an expected response time, and offer a way to challenge our handling. We will not treat silence as consent, publish your words without appropriate permission, or reduce unrelated access because you raised a concern.
